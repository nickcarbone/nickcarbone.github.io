Field guide · Twenty clubs, one black ball

The Clubland Atlas

Twenty historic private members' clubs, from St James's to Tokyo: how each began, what it makes you wear, whom it turned away, and ten libraries and bars where anyone can walk in.

Published 18 November 2026 · 104 sources

A private members' club runs on two pieces of furniture: a book and a box. A candidate's name goes into the book, where a proposer and a seconder sign for him and other members add their names in support. Then comes the box. Members vote anonymously, traditionally with white and black balls, and how many black balls it takes to sink a candidate varies from club to club 1. At the Jockey Club in Paris one black ball cancels five white 2. At its namesake in Buenos Aires two black balls from the 21-member board are enough 3.

The twenty clubs here were founded between 1693 and 1891, from St James's Street to Roppongi. Most of them were built to keep people out, and their histories are mostly histories of whom. White's and Brooks's grew out of gaming; the Athenaeum, the Reform and the Union League out of science, politics and civil war. In Kolkata, Mumbai and Hong Kong the line was drawn by race, and stayed drawn until 1959 at the Bengal Club and the late 1970s at the Hong Kong Club 4,5.

The ballot board below shows the other line. Most of the New York clubs here opened to women in the late 1980s, after a 1984 city law covering large clubs that do business on their premises, which the US Supreme Court upheld in 1988 6. London moved more slowly, from the Reform in 1981 to the Garrick in 2024 1,7. Seven of the twenty still admit only men.

Each entry is laid out like a page in a candidates' book: how the club was proposed, its house rules, what it makes you wear, the famous refusals, the kind of evening it is known for, and how, if at all, a non-member gets through the door. Dress codes and access rules change, so each carries the date it was checked. After the twenty come ten libraries, reading rooms and one long bar that let anyone in.

The book

Candidates are proposed and seconded by members, and their names are entered in a book where others can add support. At the Athenaeum nobody applies: they are nominated.

The box

Members vote in secret, with white and black balls. The number of black balls that sinks a candidate is each club's own rule.

The guest

Almost every door here opens to a non-member only on a member's arm or a reciprocal club's letter of introduction. The exceptions are flagged.

Candidate 01

White's

London · St James's Street · Est. 1693

Cards, bets and gamesMembers and guestsMen only

Proposed
Opened in 1693 by Francesco Bianco, an Italian immigrant, as Mrs White's Chocolate House in Mayfair, it had turned into a club and a gaming house by the early 18th century 8,9. Its clubhouse is at 37–38 St James's Street 8.
House rules
Its betting book recorded members' wagers on politics, sport and each other. The best-known entry, repeated in every history and impossible to verify, has Lord Alvanley staking £3,000 on which of two raindrops would reach the bottom of a window pane first 8.
Dress
No dress code is published.
Women
Still men only. It was on the Evening Standard's list of London's remaining men-only clubs in May 2024 10,1.
Black balls
White's turned away two men, Boothby and James, whose own society, formed in 1762, became the forerunner of Brooks's and Boodle's, according to Wikipedia's history of Brooks's 11.
The evening
Historically, gambling: Jonathan Swift called it the "bane of half the English nobility" 1.
Getting in
Only as a member's guest. No tours, open days or hire found (as of November 2026).
Candidate 02

Brooks's

London · St James's Street · Est. 1764

Cards, bets and gamesMembers and guestsMen only

Proposed
Founded in March 1764 by 27 Whig nobles, among them the Dukes of Portland and Roxburghe. It first met at 49 Pall Mall, a tavern run by William Almack, and was known simply as Almack's 12.
House rules
In 1777 William Brooks commissioned the present clubhouse from Henry Holland; it was finished in October 1778 12. The club kept a betting book too: a 1785 entry records Lord Cholmondeley staking two guineas against Lord Derby's 500 on a bawdy wager involving a balloon 13.
Dress
No dress code is published.
Women
Men only, as of the Evening Standard's May 2024 survey 10.
Black balls
Its origins lie in a rejection: the society that grew into Brooks's was started by two men blackballed at White's (see above) 11.
The evening
Built for high-stakes gaming in the 18th century 12; today the club says little in public about its evenings.
Getting in
Only as a member's guest (as of November 2026).
Candidate 03

The Travellers Club

London · 106 Pall Mall · Est. 1819

Library and talkMembers and guestsMen only

Proposed
Founded in May 1819, the brainchild of Viscount Castlereagh, with the 2nd Lord Auckland as first chairman 14. Charles Barry won the competition for its Pall Mall clubhouse, completed in 1832 next door to the Athenaeum 14.
House rules
The founding qualification: members had to have travelled 500 miles in a straight line outside England. The club also set out to welcome "foreigners of distinction" and diplomats 14.
Dress
Gentlemen wear jacket, collar and tie in all public rooms, the tie excepted at weekends; no denim or casual wear; "ladies should dress with comparable formality" (fact sheet, January 2024) 15.
Women
Women cannot be members. They are "welcome as guests and reciprocal members", and the cocktail bar is listed as gentlemen only (January 2024) 15. In 2014 the Archbishop of Canterbury, Justin Welby, said he would leave over the policy, as reported in Wikipedia's account 16.
Black balls
None documented; the Welby resignation is the club's best-known membership row.
The evening
The library, which Country Life calls the finest collection of travel books in private hands 14, and dinner in the Coffee Room, Monday to Friday 15.
Getting in
As a member's guest (a member may bring up to seven to the restaurant), or as a member of a reciprocal club with card and letter of introduction (January 2024) 15.
Candidate 04

The Athenaeum

London · 107 Pall Mall · Est. 1824

Library and talkMembers and guestsWomen admitted: 2001

Proposed
Founded in 1824 at the urging of John Wilson Croker, Secretary to the Admiralty, as a club for "literary and scientific men and followers of the fine arts". Michael Faraday was its first secretary and Sir Humphry Davy its first chairman 17. Decimus Burton's clubhouse, with a gilded Athena over the door, opened in February 1830 17,18.
House rules
Candidates are nominated; they do not apply. Under Rule II the committee may elect nine people of distinction a year ahead of the waiting list. The rules on "strangers" were strict enough that in 1924 Ramsay MacDonald, then Prime Minister, had to cut short a visit after unknowingly breaking them 17.
Dress
Men: collared shirt and jacket, ties optional until 7pm. Women: equivalent formality. No denim jeans or sports shoes for anyone (club website, read November 2026) 18.
Women
The club's timeline dates the admission of women to March 2001, after three debates over 17 years 17; The Week gives 2002 1.
Black balls
No famous blackballing found; the MacDonald episode is the story the club tells on itself 17.
The evening
Library, talks, concerts, quizzes and films for about 2,000 members 19.
Getting in
Only as a member's guest (as of November 2026).
Candidate 05

The Reform Club

London · 104 Pall Mall · Est. 1836

Library and talkTours or open daysWomen admitted: 1981

Proposed
Radicals and Whigs founded it in the ferment around the Great Reform Act of 1832; it opened to members at 104 Pall Mall on 24 May 1836 20. Charles Barry's clubhouse, finished in 1841, borrows its façade from the Palazzo Farnese in Rome 20.
House rules
Jules Verne sent Phileas Fogg out from here, on a wager made in the club, in Around the World in Eighty Days (1872) 20.
Dress
Visitors must follow a dress code that the club publishes separately 21.
Women
Admitted women on equal terms in 1981, the first of the old London clubs to do so, according to The Week 1.
Black balls
In February 1913 the club blackballed Baron de Forest, a prominent Liberal supporter. A London cable printed in New Zealand's Dominion reported that Lloyd George and Churchill "have already resigned" in protest 22.
The evening
Dining, the library and the Smoking Room 23. Open weekdays only, closed at weekends and on bank holidays 21.
Getting in
The most open of the Pall Mall clubs here. It takes part in Open House London every September (listed for 12–20 September 2026 as a 45-minute visit) 20,23, and groups of ten or more can book weekday-morning visits for a £15-a-head donation to its conservation trust (as of November 2026) 20.
Candidate 06

The Garrick Club

London · Garrick Street, Covent Garden · Est. 1831

Stage peopleThe long tableTours or open daysWomen admitted: 2024

Proposed
Founded in 1831 by a group of writers under the patronage of the Duke of Sussex, as a place where "actors and men of refinement and education might meet on equal terms" 24,25.
House rules
A candidate's name goes into the candidates' book, where members second it 26.
Dress
No denim, trainers or gym shoes; women "may wear trousers but not jeans" (club website, read November 2026) 27,25.
Women
A 2015 vote found 50.5 per cent in favour, short of the two-thirds needed 25. After the Guardian published members' names in March 2024, a vote in May 2024 passed with 59.98 per cent 7,26. Judi Dench and Siân Phillips became the first women members that July 28.
Black balls
In 2011 Hugh Bonneville nominated Joanna Lumley. The club took legal advice and she was not admitted 25.
The evening
Dinner in theatrical company 24.
Getting in
As a member's guest. The club arranges guided group tours for 20 to 30 people through its office (as of November 2026) 24.
Candidate 07

Pratt's

London · 14 Park Place · Est. 1857

The long tableMembers and guestsWomen admitted: 2023

Proposed
Named after William Nathaniel Pratt, steward to the Duke of Beaufort, who lived on the premises from 1841; the Duke and his friends kept coming back, and the club dates from 1857 29,30. It is a basement club off St James's Street, now owned by the Earl of Burlington of the Devonshire family 29,30.
House rules
Every steward is called George, whatever his name. The first woman steward, Margaret Ouroux, was called Georgina 30.
Dress
No dress code is published.
Women
Women were admitted as full members in May 2023, the first time in 166 years; until then they could come only as lunch guests. Some members objected that there had been no ballot 30,31.
Black balls
None documented. The 2023 row was about how the decision to admit women was made 30.
The evening
Dinner at one table seating 14 in a basement dining room. The Telegraph says the club partly inspired Blades, James Bond's club 30.
Getting in
Only as a member's guest (as of November 2026).
Candidate 08

The New Club

Edinburgh · 86 Princes Street · Est. 1787

The long tableMembers and guestsWomen admitted: 2010

Proposed
Conceived at a Caledonian Hunt ball and founded on 1 February 1787, it calls itself Scotland's oldest club. It moved from Bayle's Tavern to St Andrew Square in 1809 and to Princes Street in 1837 32. The present building, by Alan Reiach, Eric Hall and Partners, replaced the old one in the 1960s 32,33.
House rules
Wives became associate members in 1970, but women were kept off the main staircase until 1998 32.
Dress
On weekdays "gentlemen are expected to wear a jacket and tie"; smart casual at weekends (club website, read November 2026) 34.
Women
Full membership opened to women in October 2010 32,33.
Black balls
None documented.
The evening
Dining and banqueting above Princes Street Gardens 35.
Getting in
As a member's guest. Private events can be booked only by or on behalf of a member or a member of a reciprocal club (as of November 2026) 35.
Candidate 09

Jockey Club de Paris

Paris · 2 rue Rabelais, 8e · Est. 1834

Turf and waterMembers and guestsMen only

Proposed
The Société d'Encouragement was set up in 1833 to promote thoroughbred racing; in 1834 it opened the social club that became known as the Jockey Club 36,37.
House rules
Candidates need two sponsors. In the ballot, "une seule boule noire annule cinq blanches": one black ball cancels five white. La Libre Belgique counted at least four candidates blackballed since 2014, among about 1,200 members, two-thirds of them titled (August 2023) 2.
Dress
No dress code is published; La Libre describes white-gloved service 2.
Women
"Les dames ne sont pas admises": women are not admitted (August 2023) 2.
Black balls
Its loudest night was in the opera house: at the 1861 Paris Tannhäuser, members who arrived for the second act expecting their customary ballet nearly hissed the production off the stage, and Wagner never allowed another Paris production of the opera 38.
The evening
Racing society: the club grew out of the turf, and the Société's great race at Chantilly is still called the Prix du Jockey Club 36.
Getting in
No access for non-members found (as of November 2026).
Candidate 10

Union Club of the City of New York

New York · 101 East 69th Street · Est. 1836

Cards, bets and gamesMembers and guestsWomen: not published

Proposed
Founded in 1836 at a meeting at 1 Bond Street 39. The New York Times called it the "mother of clubs" in 1936 40: the Union League and the Knickerbocker were started by members who left it, and the Metropolitan by J.P. Morgan after it refused his nominee 39,41. Its Delano & Aldrich clubhouse on Park Avenue opened in 1933 39,42.
House rules
Using or even showing a phone is "strictly forbidden in all common areas" 43.
Dress
Men wear jacket and tie at all times except on floors 5, 5M and 6; women "similar standards"; no denim, shorts or sneakers; squash in whites (guest page, read November 2026) 43.
Women
The club does not say publicly whether women are full members, and we could not confirm it from a reliable source.
Black balls
In 1890 younger governors blackballed John King, J.P. Morgan's nominee, and Dr W. Seward Webb, the Vanderbilts' nominee. Morgan's answer was the Metropolitan Club 44,45.
The evening
Card and backgammon rooms, squash courts and bottle pool, a pocket-billiards game the club still favours 39,42.
Getting in
Only as a member's guest or a visiting member of a reciprocal club (as of November 2026) 43.
Candidate 11

The Metropolitan Club

New York · 1 East 60th Street · Est. 1891

The long tableMembers and guestsWomen admitted: 1974 or after 1984

Proposed
Founded in 1891 by men who had lost faith in the Union Club's ballot: its founders met on 20 February 1891 and announced J.P. Morgan as first president that March 44. American Heritage puts it more bluntly: Morgan "could not get a friend of his into the Union" and "built his own club" 41.
House rules
The press nicknamed it the Millionaires' Club 44. Stanford White's clubhouse on Fifth Avenue opened in 1894 and became a city landmark in 1979 44.
Dress
No dress code is published.
Women
Sources differ. Wikipedia says widows were admitted from 1935 and any woman could join from 1974 45. The club's president told the Daily News in the 1980s that women had belonged without a vote and gained voting rights and equal dues only after the city's 1984 law on large clubs, "with all the rights of male members, including the gymnasium" 46.
Black balls
Born of one: see the Union Club 44.
The evening
Luncheons, dinners, debutante balls and business meetings 45.
Getting in
Only as a member's guest (as of November 2026).
Candidate 12

The Union League Club

New York · 38 East 37th Street · Est. 1863

Library and talkMembers and guestsWomen members

Proposed
Founded in February 1863 by pro-Union New Yorkers, among them Frederick Law Olmsted, the minister Henry Whitney Bellows and the diarist George Templeton Strong, some of them leaving the Union Club, which would not expel Confederate sympathisers 47,39. The club says it was founded "to help preserve the Union" 48.
House rules
It raised and sponsored the 20th US Colored Infantry during the Civil War 47.
Dress
Jackets for men before 4pm on Friday, and ties only at dinner in the Mary Murray Room and Lounge; after that, collared shirts, and jeans "in good condition" (house rules, read November 2026) 49.
Women
Women are members today; Wikipedia records the first, Faith Whittlesey, in 1986 47.
Black balls
In 1893 the club refused Theodore Seligman, son of its vice-president Jesse Seligman; the father resigned. The Jewish Encyclopedia says the refusal was "for racial reasons" 50, and the New York Times headline read "Mr. Seligman Blackballed" 51.
The evening
Political talk, and a picture collection that grew partly from artist members paying their dues in paintings 47.
Getting in
As a member's guest or a registered reciprocal visitor. Local residents who belong to a reciprocal club as non-resident members get no privileges (as of November 2026) 49.
Candidate 13

The Century Association

New York · 7 West 43rd Street · Est. 1847

Library and talkMembers and guestsWomen admitted: 1988

Proposed
Founded in January 1847 out of the Sketch Club 52 for "authors, artists, and amateurs of letters and fine arts" 53.
House rules
"Its main activity is conversation" 53. Photography is banned except at private events, and phone calls are made from booths by the entrance 54.
Dress
"No formal dress code"; guests should dress in a way "respectful of the traditions of the Century" (read November 2026) 54.
Women
Members voted to admit women in 1987 55, and the first joined in 1988 52. Susan Morrison became its first woman president in 2016 56.
Black balls
In 2011 members argued over, and voted to end, reciprocity with the men-only Garrick Club in London 57,52.
The evening
Conversation, with musical and dramatic performances, exhibitions and lectures 53.
Getting in
Only as a member's guest or a visiting member of a reciprocal club (as of November 2026) 54.
Candidate 14

The Players

New York · 16 Gramercy Park South · Est. 1888

Stage peopleTours or open daysWomen admitted: 1989

Proposed
The actor Edwin Booth bought the Gramercy Park house in 1888 for $75,000 and deeded it, with his collection and library, to the new club on 31 December 1888. Mark Twain and General William T. Sherman were among the 16 incorporators 58. Booth wanted actors to meet people from outside the profession: "We should measure ourselves through personal contact with outsiders" 58.
House rules
Booth's rooms on the third floor have been kept as he left them at his death in 1893. Pipe Nights, named after churchwarden pipes, began in 1905 58.
Dress
"Neat, smart casual"; well-kept denim is fine, shorts, hoodies and baseball caps are not (read November 2026) 59.
Women
Members voted to admit women in September 1987 60; thirty were inducted in April 1989, led by Helen Hayes 58.
Black balls
None documented.
The evening
Theatre people at the bar and the Pipe Night testimonial dinners 58.
Getting in
Docent-led 60-minute tours on request, mostly on weekdays (as of November 2026) 61.
Candidate 15

Jockey Club (Buenos Aires)

Buenos Aires · Avenida Alvear 1345 · Est. 1882

Turf and waterCards, bets and gamesMembers and guestsMen only

Proposed
Founded on 15 April 1882 by Carlos Pellegrini, its first president, to run the national turf and "pursue the improvement of horse breeds" 62.
House rules
On 15 April 1953, during political unrest, its Calle Florida palace was burned with almost all its art; the club was dissolved and regained legal status in 1958, then moved to Avenida Alvear 62. A candidate needs references from five members of more than five years' standing, and two black balls on the 21-member board reject him 3; a rejected candidate waits two years 63.
Dress
Suits at Alvear, and a tie in some rooms (La Nación, 2016) 3.
Women
Women cannot be members; they come as a member's wife, daughter or widow, without a vote (May 2025) 63,3.
Black balls
The admission of the trade unionist Jorge Triaca in the 1990s set off a long argument among members 3.
The evening
Bridge and card tables, snooker, a small cinema and English-style bars 3.
Getting in
The Alvear clubhouse is closed to non-members. The club owns the San Isidro racecourse 62.
Candidate 16

The Bengal Club

Kolkata · Chowringhee and Russell Street · Est. 1827

Garden and lawnMembers and guestsWomen admitted: 1990

Proposed
Proposed at a meeting in Calcutta Town Hall in November 1826 as the Calcutta United Services Club, with Lt-Col Finch as first president; the club gives 1 February 1827 as its founding date, Wikipedia 8 February 4,64. David Gilmour writes that it filled a gap: nothing like a respectable hotel or coffee house existed in the city 65.
House rules
In 1845 it moved into the Chowringhee house Macaulay had called the best in Calcutta 4. Indians were admitted only in 1959 4,65.
Dress
In 2014 Business Standard reported a dress regime like the Calcutta Club's in the main rooms and a looser one in the Sports Bar 66. No current code is published.
Women
The club says women became members in their own right in 1990 4; Wikipedia, citing a 2001 history, says 1988 64.
Black balls
In 1896 an Englishman, Mr Ellerthorpe, invited Gandhi to the Bengal Club, where he was staying, not realising that an Indian could not be taken into the drawing room. He took Gandhi to his own room instead and apologised for "this prejudice of the local Englishmen", as Gandhi recalled in his autobiography 67,4.
The evening
Its Christmas Eve party on the tennis court, with a live band and a toast at midnight 68.
Getting in
Only through members or reciprocal arrangements (as of November 2026) 4.
Candidate 17

Royal Bombay Yacht Club

Mumbai · Apollo Bunder · Est. 1846

Turf and waterMembers and guestsWomen: not published

Proposed
Founded in 1846 as the Bombay Yacht Club, with Henry Morland as commodore; the "Royal" came under Queen Victoria's patronage in 1876 69,70. Its seafront clubhouse at Apollo Bunder dates from 1881 69.
House rules
It admitted Europeans only until the 1950s; Wikipedia records Kerse Naoroji as the first Indian member, in 1959 70.
Dress
No dress code is published.
Women
The club does not publish when women became members.
Black balls
When Lord Willingdon, as Governor of Bombay, brought Indian friends to the Yacht Club, they were refused, and he went off to found the Willingdon Sports Club, opened in 1917. Tellings differ in the detail: Gilmour says Willingdon was told he could not make the Byculla Club or the Yacht Club change their rules 71,65.
The evening
Sailing, and residential chambers that won a UNESCO Asia-Pacific heritage award in 2013 69.
Getting in
Members of reciprocal clubs may stay up to ten days and make four day visits a month 72.

The Hong Kong Club

The long tableMembers and guestsWomen admitted: after 1996

Proposed
Opened on 26 May 1846 on Queen's Road by British merchants and officials 5; the South China Morning Post says eight taipans set it up 73.

Dress

Women

Black balls
No named blackballing found. Admission is "by invitation and ballot" 76.
The evening
Formal dining in the Red Room 75.
Getting in
Members of reciprocal clubs, or visitors proposed by a member, for up to 30 days a year (as of November 2026) 76.
Candidate 19

The Tokyo Club

Tokyo · Roppongi 1-chome · Est. 1884

Cards, bets and gamesMembers and guestsMen only

Proposed
Founded on 14 May 1884 by Foreign Minister Inoue Kaoru as a British-style club where Japanese statesmen and financiers could mix with foreign diplomats, "to promote international goodwill" 77,78. It began in the Rokumeikan, the government's Western-style guesthouse, and used it until 1897 77.
House rules
A member of the imperial family is usually patron; it is currently Prince Hitachi. Since 2005 the club has had a purpose-built house in Roppongi 77.
Dress
Jacket and tie for men. From June to September ties are optional, but jackets stay on in the bar, dining room and private rooms (February 2025) 78.
Women
Membership is "gentlemen only"; women come as guests or as members of reciprocal clubs (February 2025) 78.
Black balls
None documented.
The evening
French dining, the bar, snooker, and the boards for go and shogi. Closed on Sundays 78.
Getting in
Members of reciprocal clubs with a letter of introduction, for up to 12 days a year; members living in Japan are excluded (February 2025) 78.

The Melbourne Club

Melbourne · 36 Collins Street · Est. 1838

Garden and lawnTours or open daysMen only

Proposed
Founded in 1838 at a gathering of 23 men at John Pascoe Fawkner's hotel 79: squatters, civil servants, judges, professional men and merchants 80. It has been in Leonard Terry's clubhouse on Collins Street since 1859 80,81.
House rules
The walled garden dates from 1858 and holds the largest London plane known in Victoria 81. In 2025 members voted to spend A$5.5 million on half the lease of the car park next door to keep out a Sydney hospitality group 82.
Dress
No dress code is published.
Women
Does not admit women as members 79. In 2015 Victoria's Chief Justice, Marilyn Warren, and Justice Chris Maxwell turned down invitations to speak at men-only clubs including this one 83.
Black balls
Robert Menzies was not invited to join in the mid-1930s, apparently because he had not enlisted in the First World War; offered nomination in 1939, he hesitated 84.
The evening
The garden, where, the story goes, resident members planted the plane trees after an 1895 ball to keep marquees off the lawn 85.
Getting in
Only on rare garden open days, such as Open Gardens Victoria's in February 2016 85. No regular public access.

Doors that open

Ten libraries, reading rooms and one long bar from the same world that anyone can walk into.

The Long Bar

Waldorf Astoria, The Bund, Shanghai · Est. 1911

The Shanghai Club, founded in 1861, opened its Bund clubhouse in 1911 around an L-shaped mahogany bar measured at 110.7 feet, long billed as the longest in the world, a claim the sources repeat as legend 86,87. By legend, the end nearest the river was reserved for the most powerful bankers and taipans 87,86. The bar was lost during the Japanese occupation; the one Hilton reopened with the hotel in 2010–11 was rebuilt from photographs 88,86.

Walk in: Open to the public every evening from 5.30pm (as of November 2026) 89.

Hôtel de la Païva

25 avenue des Champs-Élysées, Paris · Est. 1903

The mansion built in the 1850s and 1860s for the courtesan known as La Païva has been the Travellers Club of Paris since the early 1900s; sources give 1903 and 1904. It is a listed historic monument, and the club, about 750 men, owns it 90,2.

Walk in: Occasional guided tours, about €25 and quickly booked, plus European Heritage Days (as of January 2025) 90,91.

The London Library

14 St James's Square, London · Est. 1841

Thomas Carlyle's subscription library has been in St James's Square, a few minutes' walk from the Pall Mall clubs, since 1845. Anyone who pays the subscription can join 92.

Walk in: Occasional public tours for adults, booked ahead (one ran on 16 June 2025) 93.

The Leeds Library

18 Commercial Street, Leeds · Est. 1768

Founded in 1768, with Joseph Priestley among its founders, and in its Commercial Street rooms since 1808; the Yorkshire Post calls it the county's oldest surviving subscription library 94.

Walk in: Non-members can ring the buzzer and look round at set times on weekdays, and book free volunteer-led tours (as of November 2026) 95.

The Portico Library

57 Mosley Street, Manchester · Est. 1806

A Greek Revival subscription library opened in 1806, with a café under its dome 96.

Walk in: Open to the public and free to enter, Monday to Saturday; group tours on Wednesdays for £6 a head (as of November 2026) 96.

The Linen Hall Library

17 Donegall Square North, Belfast · Est. 1788

Belfast's oldest library, founded in 1788, with a licensed coffee house 97.

Walk in: Free entry on weekdays, with a suggested £3 donation, and guided tours (as of November 2026) 97.

The Boston Athenæum

10½ Beacon Street, Boston · Est. 1807

A members' library founded in 1807 out of the Anthology Society, a Boston literary club 98.

Walk in: First-floor admission $11; a $40 day membership opens every floor; ticketed art and architecture tours (as of November 2026) 99.

New York Society Library

53 East 79th Street, New York · Est. 1754

The city's oldest library, founded in 1754 100.

How this was put together

Facts were checked against the clubs' own websites, fact sheets that clubs give to reciprocal partners, heritage and landmark records, and newspaper reports, with two sources where they could be found. Where sources disagree (the Bengal Club's founding day and the year it admitted women, the years the Athenaeum and the Metropolitan admitted women, the date the Travellers Club of Paris moved into the Hôtel de la Païva), both are given. Wikipedia is used, and marked in the sources, only as secondary support. Blackballings are described as reported at the time or by the clubs and historians cited; anecdotes the sources themselves treat as legend are labelled. Dress codes, hours, prices and access rules were read in November 2026 unless another date is given, and all of them change: check with the club before you go.
