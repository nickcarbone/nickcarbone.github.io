A field guide to twenty recording studios

The Album Rooms

Twenty recording studios where landmark records were made, from a Memphis storefront to a Berlin ballroom by the Wall: what each room added to the sound, what stands there now, and whether you can still visit or book it.

Rooms 20Records 92Years 1931–1979Status as of Nov 2026

Opening years, on tape

Sam Phillips’s room at 706 Union Avenue in Memphis is about the size of a large living room. He tiled it himself in a custom pattern, made sure no two surfaces ran parallel and put the control room up a short step at the back 1. In July 1954 Elvis Presley recorded ‘That’s All Right’ in it. The tile is still on the walls.

Studios are usually described by their gear, but the records in this guide carry the shape of particular rooms: the long natural decay of a Berlin ballroom, the height of a timber chapel in New Jersey, a church nave in Manhattan, eight concrete chambers under a Hollywood office tower, an old cinema in Memphis with its sloping floor left in. Some rooms gave their sound through the people who were always there, the house bands that played on hundreds of sessions. Others, like the residential studios in Wales, France and the Caribbean, worked by taking musicians away from everything else.

The twenty here are grouped by region, like the sides of a box set. Each entry records what the room is documented to have added, the landmark records cut there, what stands on the site today and whether you can visit or book it. Visiting and booking details change, so treat them as as of November 2026 and check with the studio before you travel. Each record has a link that opens a search for it on Spotify, so you can build a playlist for any room as you read.

Where sources disagree, on dates, dimensions or who designed what, the entry says so. Where a record was only partly made in a room, it is marked.

Labels on the box

  • Working studio: still takes sessions; no regular public tours
  • Studio and tours: you can visit, and the room still records
  • Museum: visitable, but not a working studio for hire
  • Closed or gone: demolished, closed or not open to visitors
  • Ruin: the building stands, abandoned

Sun Studio

706 Union Avenue, Memphis, Tennessee

Studio and tours
Opened
January 1950, as the Memphis Recording Service
Built by
Sam Phillips
Visit
Yes, guided tours
Book
Enquire

What the room added

The room is small, roughly 18 by 30 feet, with the control room raised about two feet at the back. Phillips fitted it with custom-patterned acoustic tile; the ceiling undulates and tiles on the front and rear walls project outward, so there are no parallel surfaces for sound to bounce between. The landmark nomination calls it one of the first studios designed with acoustics in mind, and the tile is still there 1. The studio’s own line, as relayed by uDiscover, is that Phillips didn’t know not to use so much echo, so a three-piece band sounded like an all-night party 2.

What stands there now

A museum and tour attraction since 1987, designated a National Historic Landmark in 2003 3. Tours start on the half hour 4. The room is still used for recording after hours, but how outside artists book it was not confirmed for this guide.

Session notePhillips sold Elvis Presley’s contract to RCA Victor on 21 November 1955 for $35,000, plus $5,000 in back royalties 1.

RCA Studio B

Music Row, Nashville, Tennessee

Museum
Opened
1957; RCA leased it from the builder, Dan Maddox
Built by
Dan Maddox, who leased it to RCA Victor; Chet Atkins ran RCA in Nashville
Visit
Yes, via the Country Music Hall of Fame
Book
Occasional sessions only

What the room added

The main room is roughly 40 by 27 feet with a 13-foot ceiling; published measurements differ slightly 67. Hi-Fi News describes accordion-pleated walls that change angle every four feet, tiled from three feet above the floor to the ceiling 6. When the room’s bass resonance gave trouble, engineer Bill Porter hung fibreglass triangles at different heights and marked spots on the floor with an X, where the boom was least and singers and acoustic guitarists should stand 7. The room is associated with the smooth, string-and-chorus ‘Nashville Sound’ 8.

What stands there now

RCA stopped using it in 1977. The Country Music Hall of Fame and Museum runs tours, and the room still hosts occasional sessions: John Hiatt recorded there with the Jerry Douglas Band in 2021 87.

Session noteThe Hall of Fame says Elvis Presley recorded more than 240 songs in the room 8. Other counts range from more than 200 to about 260.

FAME Studios

603 East Avalon Avenue, Muscle Shoals, Alabama

Studio and tours
Opened
1959 above a drugstore in Florence; on Avalon Avenue from the early 1960s
Built by
Rick Hall, with Billy Sherrill and Tom Stafford
Visit
Yes, guided tours
Book
Yes

What the room added

The Avalon Avenue building began as a 20-by-70-foot concrete-block structure with one studio and built-in echo chambers; a second studio followed in 1967 10. Its sound belonged to its rhythm section: from 1964 the players later nicknamed the Swampers (Jimmy Johnson, Roger Hawkins, Barry Beckett and David Hood), who left in 1969 to open their own place up the road 10.

What stands there now

Still a working studio, and open for guided tours most days 11. It has been on the Alabama Register of Landmarks and Heritage since 1997 10.

Session noteAretha Franklin’s first Atlantic session at FAME, in early 1967, broke up after a row; she finished the album in New York 1213.

Stax Records (Soulsville)

926 East McLemore Avenue, Memphis, Tennessee

Museum
Opened
Around 1959-60, in the former Capitol Theatre
Built by
Jim Stewart and his sister Estelle Axton
Visit
Yes, museum with a replica studio
Book
No

What the room added

Stewart and Axton turned a cinema into a studio. They partitioned the auditorium, built the control room where the screen had been and kept the sloping floor as a cheap way of controlling the acoustics 14. The Tennessee Encyclopedia credits that slope, carpeted walls and big cinema speakers with part of the sound 15; it is a widely repeated account with no measurements behind it. Axton mortgaged her house to buy the Ampex recorder 14.

What stands there now

The original building was demolished in 1989 after the label’s collapse in the mid-1970s 16. The Stax Museum of American Soul Music opened on the site in 2003 with Studio A rebuilt on its old footprint 17. The Soulsville Foundation also runs the Stax Music Academy alongside it.

Session noteMemphis Magazine reports that after the bank foreclosed, a church bought the empty building for $10 and later razed it 16.

Muscle Shoals Sound Studio

3614 Jackson Highway, Sheffield, Alabama

Studio and tours
Opened
April 1969
Built by
Barry Beckett, Roger Hawkins, David Hood and Jimmy Johnson, with backing from Atlantic’s Jerry Wexler
Visit
Yes, guided tours
Book
Yes, by proposal

What the room added

A concrete-block building about 25 by 68 feet. The recording room, about 25 by 37 feet, has an intentionally irregular layout, a sloped floor and acoustic panelling 19. The house band was the owners themselves, and the 8-track machine was chosen to match Atlantic’s equipment 20.

What stands there now

The studio moved to a larger site in the late 1970s. The Muscle Shoals Music Foundation bought the Jackson Highway building in 2013, restored it and opened it for tours in 2017; it can still be used for recording 2021. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2006 19.

Session noteIt is often described as a former coffin showroom. The National Register nomination tells a plainer story: a venetian-blind shop, then a run of supply and appliance businesses 19.

Columbia 30th Street Studio (‘The Church’)

207 East 30th Street, Manhattan, New York

Closed or gone
Opened
Late 1940s, in a disused 1870s church
Built by
Columbia Records
Visit
No
Book
No

What the room added

A former Presbyterian church about 97 feet long, 55 feet wide and some 50 feet high 23. Columbia’s A&R chief Mitch Miller is said to have forbidden changes to the main room for fear of spoiling it 24. It had an unvarnished wooden floor and a concrete echo chamber in the basement 2526.

What stands there now

Gone. Columbia stopped using it in the early 1980s, and a ten-storey apartment building has stood on the site since about 1985 23.

Session noteGlenn Gould, who made his name with the Goldberg Variations here in 1955, returned in 1981 to record them again, among the last sessions in the room 23.

Capitol Studios

Capitol Records Tower, Hollywood, Los Angeles

Working studio
Opened
1956, in the base of the new Capitol Records Tower
Built by
Capitol Records
Visit
No regular tours
Book
Yes

What the room added

The studios’ signature lies underground: eight concrete echo chambers about 30 feet below the street, trapezoidal with no parallel surfaces, each roughly 2,000 cubic feet and capable of reverberation of up to about five seconds 2728. Four came with the building and four were added later. Who designed them is disputed: some accounts credit Les Paul with the first four, while Sound On Sound credits the acoustician Michael Rettinger 2729.

What stands there now

A working commercial studio under Universal Music’s Capitol Music Group. There is no regular public tour; the studio has opened its doors for one-off events, such as its 60th anniversary in 2016 30.

Session noteNat King Cole’s piano is still in Studio B, and the tower was nicknamed the House That Nat Built 3029.

Chess Records

2120 South Michigan Avenue, Chicago

Museum
Opened
1957, when Chess moved into a remodelled 1911 building
Built by
Leonard and Phil Chess
Visit
Yes, guided tours
Book
No

What the room added

Chess occupied the building from 1957 to 1967; the city’s landmark citation describes a modest facility 31. The studio was designed by engineer Jack Weiner and first traded as Sheldon Recording Studios, then Ter-Mar 32. The journalist Dave Hoekstra, who toured it, describes walls with no parallel surfaces and a ceiling that rises to a central peak 33. Much of the sound came from the people: Willie Dixon worked as writer, bassist and producer 34. Many earlier Chess classics were cut at the label’s previous addresses, so not everything on the label came from this room.

What stands there now

Willie Dixon’s widow, Marie Dixon, bought the building in 1993; it reopened in 1997 as the home of Willie Dixon’s Blues Heaven Foundation, which runs tours 35. It has been a Chicago Landmark since 1990 31.

Session noteMinnie Riperton worked at Chess as a receptionist before her singing career took off, according to Hoekstra 33.

Hitsville U.S.A. (Motown Studio A)

2648 West Grand Boulevard, Detroit, Michigan

Museum
Opened
1959, in a family house Berry Gordy bought that August
Built by
Berry Gordy Jr
Visit
From December 2026 (paused before then)
Book
No

What the room added

Studio A, known as the Snakepit, is a small, crowded room in what had been a house 3738. The house band, the Funk Brothers, played there at all hours 37. The echo came from the attic: the first chamber had parallel walls that produced a flutter, and a second, built next door at 2644, had non-parallel, varnished plaster walls 39. Sound On Sound records Lamont Dozier stomping on floorboards for the beat of ‘Where Did Our Love Go’ 38.

What stands there now

The Motown Museum, founded by Esther Gordy Edwards in 1985, keeps Studio A as it was 40. As of November 2026 tours were paused during a large expansion; the museum says Hitsville reopens to visitors in December 2026, with the wider new campus due in 2027 414243.

Session noteGordy bought the house with an $800 loan from his family, according to Historic Detroit 40. Motown used it as studio and headquarters until the move to Los Angeles in 1972.

Van Gelder Studio

445 Sylvan Avenue, Englewood Cliffs, New Jersey

Working studio
Opened
1959; before that, his parents’ living room in Hackensack
Built by
Rudy Van Gelder
Visit
No
Book
Yes

What the room added

An optometrist who recorded in his parents’ living room built himself a timber chapel. The main room rises to about 39 feet at its apex 4445, designed by David Henken, a former student of Frank Lloyd Wright 45. Van Gelder hired no acousticians 44. He was famously secretive, moving the microphones himself and letting nobody else touch them 46.

What stands there now

Still a working studio. Van Gelder died in 2016 and his longtime assistant Maureen Sickler now runs it with her husband, Don Sickler 47. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2022 48. There are no public tours.

Session noteVan Gelder was a licensed optometrist, and he reportedly taped over manufacturers’ names on his equipment so visitors could not see what he used 4649.

Sound City

Van Nuys, Los Angeles

Working studio
Opened
1969, in a former Vox building
Built by
Joe Gottfried and Tom Skeeter
Visit
No
Book
Enquire

What the room added

Studio A’s live room became famous for its drums: a large room whose natural reverb gave kits attack without harshness 5051. The other half of the legend was its Neve 8028 console, installed in the early 1970s 5052. Wikipedia reports that the interior was left largely unchanged for decades, a claim the studio’s fans often repeat 51.

What stands there now

Sound City closed in 2011 and Dave Grohl bought the Neve console for his own studio 52. Recording carried on in the building: Fairfax Recording leased it from 2011, and Wikipedia reports that it reopened under the Sound City name in 2017 and was taken over by Tony Berg and Blake Mills in 2018 5051.

Session noteRolling Stone reports that Nirvana recorded Nevermind there in 16 days 52.

Electric Lady Studios

52 West 8th Street, Greenwich Village, New York

Working studio
Opened
August 1970
Built by
Jimi Hendrix and his manager Michael Jeffery
Visit
No (exterior only)
Book

What the room added

Hendrix bought a defunct club intending to run it as a venue; the engineer Eddie Kramer persuaded him to build a studio instead 53. The architect John Storyk designed it with Hendrix and Kramer. Hendrix asked for soft, curving surfaces, white walls and lighting that could turn the room any colour 53. PBS describes it as a pioneering artist-owned studio 53.

What stands there now

A working commercial studio, renovated and expanded after new owners took over in 2010 54. Atlas Obscura notes there are no public tours, though you can look at the outside 55.

Session noteHendrix died on 18 September 1970, about three weeks after the opening party 5456.

Abbey Road Studios

3 Abbey Road, St John's Wood, London

Working studio
Opened
12 November 1931, as EMI Studios
Built by
The Gramophone Company, which became part of EMI that year
Visit
Occasional ticketed events
Book
Yes

What the room added

Studio Two was built for big bands and small orchestras and keeps its decorative plaster ceiling and the wooden stairs up to the control room 57. The studio’s own history points to its herringbone floor, dovetailed brickwork and hanging quilts, the first of them made from parachute silk stuffed with seaweed 58. EMI’s Alan Blumlein developed stereo recording techniques here in the early 1930s 59.

What stands there now

A working studio. It has been Grade II listed since 2010, as has the zebra crossing outside 5759. There are no regular tours; it opens for occasional ticketed events. Sessions are booked through the studio 60.

Session noteElgar conducted the London Symphony Orchestra at the opening. Historic England says more than 190 of the Beatles’ 210 recordings were made here 5957.

Rockfield Studios

Rockfield, near Monmouth, Wales

Studio and tours
Opened
Early 1960s, in a farmhouse attic; residential from the mid-1960s
Built by
The brothers Kingsley and Charles Ward
Visit
Set dates and by appointment
Book
Yes, residential

What the room added

A working cattle farm turned into one of the first residential studios: bands live on site for weeks 6162. The Coach House has a live room of about 150 square metres with stone walls and a wooden ceiling; the Quadrangle, opened in 1973, has about 170 square metres 61. The Wards credit the countryside with shaping the songwriting 62.

What stands there now

Still family-run, now by Lisa Ward 62, and still bookable as a residential studio. Visit Monmouthshire lists guided tours on set dates and group visits by appointment 63.

Session noteAccording to Wikipedia, the Stone Roses’ long residency in the late 1980s helped keep the studio afloat 64.

Trident Studios

17 St Anne's Court, Soho, London

Closed or gone
Opened
1968
Built by
The brothers Norman and Barry Sheffield
Visit
No
Book
No

What the room added

Trident was among the first British studios with an eight-track recorder and Dolby noise reduction 6566. Its other asset was a century-old Bechstein grand with a hard, percussive attack that is audible on ‘Hey Jude’ 6766. Cambridge Audio’s history adds that the gents’ toilet was used as an echo chamber 67.

What stands there now

The Sheffields sold up in 1981 and the building has changed hands several times since; accounts of its current use differ 6567. A plaque marking Bowie’s work there went up in 2017 65. Not open to the public.

Session noteThe Bechstein has been offered at auction, but accounts of when, and of where it ended up, conflict 656766.

Château d’Hérouville

Hérouville-en-Vexin, Val-d'Oise, France

Working studio
Opened
About 1970, in an 18th-century château
Built by
The composer Michel Magne
Visit
No regular tours
Book
Yes

What the room added

A country house with about 30 rooms, a pool and grounds, where artists lived as well as worked: with a residential studio set up by Magne 68. Tony Visconti spoke of a strange energy in the house, and RedShark reports that Bowie refused to sleep in the master bedroom 69.

What stands there now

The studio closed in 1985 and the château lay derelict for decades 6869. A group of audio and film-scoring professionals bought and restored it; it reopened around 2020 and takes bookings through its own site 7068.

Session noteThe Grateful Dead played an impromptu free concert in the grounds on 21 June 1971 6871.

Hansa Tonstudio and the Meistersaal

Köthener Straße 38, Berlin

Studio and tours
Opened
1970s; the Meisels bought the building in the mid-1970s (sources give 1975 or 1976)
Built by
Peter and Thomas Meisel
Visit
Guided tours by appointment
Book
Yes

What the room added

The Meistersaal opened in 1913 as a chamber-music hall 73: about 650 square metres under a coffered ceiling some 15 metres high 74. In the 1970s the Wall ran close by; about 150 metres away by deutschland.de’s account, though estimates vary 7574. On ‘Heroes’, Tony Visconti set microphones at increasing distances from Bowie and gated them so that the hall’s natural reverb, about 1.5 seconds, opened up as the singing grew louder 76.

What stands there now

According to Sound On Sound, Hansa Studio 1 still operates and the Meistersaal, now an events venue, can be booked for recording through tie-lines to it 7773. Local tour companies run guided visits by appointment 74.

Session noteVisconti told Mix that East German guards sometimes watched the control room through binoculars 76.

Studio One

13 Brentford Road (now Studio One Boulevard), Kingston, Jamaica

Closed or gone
Opened
1963
Built by
Clement ‘Coxsone’ Dodd
Visit
Not formally
Book
No

What the room added

Britannica calls the first studio crude and tiny, a one-track room that later moved up to eight tracks 79. It helped set the bass-forward sound of ska, rocksteady and reggae. Grammy.com credits Dodd as the first producer to put a house band on a weekly wage instead of paying by the record; a run of house bands, from the Skatalites to Sound Dimension, cut the rhythms that are still being reused 80.

What stands there now

Still in the Dodd family. The building was refurbished and murals were unveiled in 2020 81, but there was no organised tour programme in the sources checked for this guide.

Session noteBrentford Road was renamed Studio One Boulevard on 1 May 2004. Dodd died three days later, on 4 May 8283.

What the room added

Martin set out to give artists the facilities of his London studio with the advantage of an exotic location 87. Clients lived in villas on site, with a pool outside the control-room window 88.

What stands there now

Hurricane Hugo struck in September 1989 and the studio never reopened 8788; sources differ on how badly it was damaged. Volcanic eruptions from 1995 emptied much of the island. The ruins sit at or near the edge of the exclusion zone and are fenced off and closed 8990.

How this was made

Facts were checked against official studio and museum sites, National Register and landmark records, national encyclopaedias, and trade and music publications. Where a fact rests on a single source, the entry attributes it. Opening years on the timeline are approximate where sources disagree. The Spotify links run a search; they are not official playlists, and availability varies by country. Years beside records are release years; where the recording date differs notably, the note says so.
