The Stadium Atlas

Twenty-two stadiums and arenas, from Lambeau to La Bombonera: what stood on each site before, who paid for the land and the building, and how easily you can get there and home without a car.

Dodger Stadium stands where sheriff's deputies removed the last families from Chavez Ravine in 1959. Madison Square Garden sits on the stump of Pennsylvania Station. Perth's stadium is on an old rubbish tip, Koshien on a river bed that a railway company bought, and the Emirates on a council waste depot cleared by a compulsory purchase order that the planning inspector had advised against.

This atlas asks the same questions of 22 grounds: what the land was, how it changed hands, who paid, and how the crowd gets in, eats, drinks and gets home. The money is the hardest part to pin down. Clubs and cities rarely publish complete accounts, figures here are in the money of their day and not adjusted for inflation, and where sources disagree the entry says so.

The last question is the practical one, so each block carries a car-free score out of eight. Three grounds take full marks: Wrigley Field, the Emirates and Perth's Optus Stadium. Lambeau Field and Dodger Stadium, both built around their car parks, score two.

How the car-free score works

Each ground gets up to two points on four counts, judged by the Daily desk from the operators', transport authorities' and cities' own published information, as of November 2026. It measures how easy it is to arrive and leave without a car, not how good the day out is.

  • Rail2: a train, metro or tram station within about ten minutes' walk. 1: rail further away or only via a shuttle. 0: no useful rail.
  • Matchday service2: documented extra trains, dedicated shuttles or event running. 1: good regular service only. 0: nothing documented.
  • Walk & cycle2: a dense, walkable district with cycle provision. 1: walkable with caveats. 0: an island of roads and car parks.
  • Parking scarcity2: little or no public parking. 1: some, restricted or pre-booked. 0: car parks for thousands.

Two grounds have a criterion we could not score for lack of a source; their totals are shown out of six.

Stand A North America

Block 01

Lambeau Field

Green Bay, Wisconsin · opened 1957 · Green Bay Packers (NFL)

Before

Farmland in the Town of Ashwaubenon. Forty-eight acres were bought from Victor Vannieuwenhoven in August 1956 for $73,305; the family kept their house, and a farm survived in what became the car park until 1963.5

The deal

Green Bay voters approved a $960,000 bond for the stadium on 3 April 1956, with more than 62 per cent in favour.6 The site was annexed into the city that September, which mattered for one reason above others: Ashwaubenon was dry, and the mayor ruled that the annexed land would not fall under the ordinance that would have stopped beer sales.5 The big rebuild of 2001–03 had a $295.2 million budget, of which $160 million came from bonds issued by the Green Bay/Brown County Professional Football Stadium District, $126.1 million from the Packers and $9.1 million from the state transport department.7 Brown County paid it off with a 0.5 per cent sales tax that ran from January 2001 to 2015.7,8 Ownership is tangled between the Packers, the city and the stadium district, and published accounts describe it differently.9,10,11

Getting there

No passenger rail serves the stadium. Green Bay Metro runs four free gameday bus routes every 30 minutes, though the Packers' own pages disagree on when they start and stop.12,13 Stadium lots go to season parking-pass holders, and nearby private yards and free residential streets take the rest.12

In, fed, out

The tailgate is the arrival: a setup up to 9 by 6 feet behind your vehicle, charcoal or propane but no wood fires or turkey fryers, and all of it over at kick-off.12 Inside, most concession stands are worked by volunteers raising money for local nonprofits, beer is limited to two per purchase and sales stop at the end of the third quarter.13

Block 02

Wrigley Field

Lake View, Chicago · opened 1914 · Chicago Cubs (MLB)

Before

The grounds of the Chicago Lutheran Theological Seminary: four wooden buildings on a 7.7-acre site, vacant after the seminary moved to Maywood in 1910.14

The deal

Charles Weeghman, owner of the Federal League's Chi-Feds, leased the land in late 1913 rather than buying it, and built the park for $250,000 according to the building permit.14 The Cubs moved in in 1916.15 No public money is recorded in the build. The Ricketts family bought the team and the ballpark from Tribune Co. for $845 million in 2009, and the 1060 Project renovation approved in 2013 was presented as privately funded, paid for partly by new signage.16,17

Getting there

The Red Line's Addison station is about half a block from the park. In 2018 the CTA added weeknight game-day Red Line trains and stopped southbound Purple Line Express trains at Sheridan, three blocks away.18 A free remote lot on Clarendon Avenue has shuttles for night and weekend games.19

In, fed, out

Chicago law shapes the evening. The city's ordinance, as published by the ward office, allows up to 43 night games a season and bars regular-season starts after 4.10pm on Fridays and Saturdays, with limited exceptions.20 On night games the surrounding residential permit zone becomes a tow zone from 5pm to 10pm, and Addison and Clark can be closed at police discretion.21 The rooftop bleachers across Waveland and Sheffield are their own economy: in 2004 most of them agreed to pay the Cubs 17 per cent of gross revenue, and by 2018 the Ricketts family owned 11 of them.17

Block 03

Fenway Park

Fenway–Kenmore, Boston · opened 1912 · Boston Red Sox (MLB)

Before

Filled marsh. The area was 19th-century landfill tied to the Back Bay Fens, finished by 1891 and still largely empty in 1908, held by a few owners as a speculation.22

The deal

The Taylor family, publishers of the Boston Globe and owners of the club, bought an awkward, asymmetrical parcel in early 1911; its shape is why the field's dimensions are odd. The name came from the district and conveniently promoted the family's Fenway Realty Company.23 The park cost about $650,000, privately paid.24 In 2000 the state approved, and asked the city to approve, a $312 million public package for a replacement Fenway that was never built.25 The public asset the club did get came in 2013, when the Boston Redevelopment Authority agreed to sell game-day rights over Yawkey Way and air rights over Lansdowne Street for about $7.34 million; in 2015 the state Inspector General wrote that the price "appears to be insufficient".26,27

Getting there

Kenmore, on three Green Line branches, is about five minutes' walk from Gates A and D, and Lansdowne commuter-rail station is about a three-minute walk, though the club warns its last trains can leave before a night game ends.28,29,30 There is free bike valet at Gate D.28

In, fed, out

Jersey Street is closed and run as part of the ballpark from a little over two hours before games, an arrangement that grew from a 2002 experiment.26 Lansdowne Street, behind the Green Monster, has sausage vendors and music venues. The Fenway Frank is made by Kayem of Chelsea, which sells more than a million a year here.28 After the game, crowds split between Kenmore, Fenway and Lansdowne, and WBUR suggests walking a stop further to avoid the crush.28

Block 04

Dodger Stadium

Chavez Ravine, Los Angeles · opened 1962 · Los Angeles Dodgers (MLB)

Before

Chavez Ravine, a largely Mexican-American, semi-rural community of three neighbourhoods: La Loma, Palo Verde and Bishop. Estimates of how many families lived there at its peak run from 1,100 to more than 1,800.31,32,33

The deal

In 1950 the land was earmarked for Elysian Park Heights, a federally assisted public-housing project designed by Richard Neutra and Robert E. Alexander, and eminent-domain proceedings began. The housing was dropped after the 1953 mayoral election, and the city reacquired the land.31,32,33 Under a 1957 contract the city gave the Dodgers about 300 acres (one account says 352) in exchange for the minor-league Wrigley Field in Los Angeles, plus park and maintenance commitments.31,32,34 Opponents forced a referendum, which the deal survived narrowly in June 1958, and a taxpayer suit that ended when the US Supreme Court declined to hear it in 1959.31,35 On 9 May 1959 sheriff's deputies removed the last holdouts, including the Arechiga family, and their homes were bulldozed.31,32 The stadium itself was privately financed; the family archive counts the land, $2 million of city grading and $2.74 million of county roads as the public share.34,36

Getting there

The nearest rail, Chinatown on the A Line, is 1.2 miles downhill; a FOX 11 reporter walked it but found missing sidewalks, lighting and crossings.37 The free Dodger Stadium Express from Union Station uses a bus lane on Sunset Boulevard.38,39 A city transport study says most attendees drive.40

In, fed, out

The stadium was built for 16,000 cars and buses on terraced lots, so fans park near the level of their seats, and more than 8 million cubic yards of earth were moved to make it.34,36 Outside food is allowed in a small clear bag.41 A proposed gondola from Union Station had council opposition and further approvals outstanding as of mid-2026.42,43

Block 05

Madison Square Garden

Midtown Manhattan, New York · opened 1968 · Knicks (NBA), Rangers (NHL)

Before

The above-ground halls of Pennsylvania Station, designed by McKim, Mead & White and opened in 1910. Demolition began on 28 October 1963; the trains below kept running.4,44

The deal

In November 1960 Irving Mitchell Felt of Graham-Paige accepted the Pennsylvania Railroad's offer of the right to move the Garden above the station, a deal over the railroad's own site.45 The loss of the station is widely credited, including by the City Planning Commission, with spurring New York's Landmarks Law of 1965.4,44 The arena was privately financed.45 Its public subsidy is a property-tax exemption granted under 1982 state legislation, worth $43.0 million in fiscal 2025 by the owner's own accounts; the city's Independent Budget Office put forgone tax at about $1 billion over 41 years.46,47 The arena runs on a city special permit, and in 2023 the City Council granted only five years, explicitly to push a fix for the station below.4,48

Getting there

Penn Station is underneath: Amtrak, the Long Island Rail Road, NJ Transit and the subway. The LIRR added late trains after Garden events in 2024.4,46,49 There is no public parking on the site.4 Applicant surveys cited by the planning commission put mass transit at 50–61 per cent of attendees, and private cars still at 23–38 per cent.4

In, fed, out

About 70 per cent of visitors enter from Seventh Avenue, and crowds leaving the arena pour into the same concourses as commuters.4 In 2023 the MTA, Amtrak and NJ Transit said the Garden imposes "severe constraints" on the station; the owner said that reflected "the opinion of a few".50

Block 06

SoFi Stadium

Inglewood, Los Angeles County · opened 2020 · Rams and Chargers (NFL)

Before

Hollywood Park racetrack, opened on 10 June 1938 by a turf club whose directors included Jack Warner and Al Jolson, and closed on 22 December 2013 after 75 years.51

The deal

Stan Kroenke bought a neighbouring 60-acre parcel and combined it with the Hollywood Park land in a venture with Stockbridge Capital.52 After a petition qualified the plan for the ballot, the Inglewood City Council approved it unanimously on 24 February 2015.53 The stadium cost about $5 billion and was privately financed; the city put in no upfront tax dollars and agreed not to reimburse the developer for roads and streetlights until the project had generated $25 million in tax revenue.54,55,56 Reason reported that the city also lets the developer keep sales taxes worth an estimated $180 million.55

Getting there

The K Line's Downtown Inglewood station opened in 2022, but the official advice is to ride to LAX/Metro Transit Center and take Metro's game-day shuttle.57,58,59 A $2.4 billion people mover meant to carry fans the last mile was halted in 2024, with opposition from local businesses including the owners of the Rams.60 Shared e-scooters and hire bikes are not allowed in the district.58

In, fed, out

Everyone parking needs a prepaid pass, and nearby streets are residents-only on event days.58,61 The stadium is cashless; at NFL games alcohol sales stop at the end of the third quarter, and no one may hold more than two drinks at a time.62,63 LAist advises allowing at least an hour to get out.62

Block 07

Estadio Azteca (Estadio Banorte)

Santa Úrsula Coapa, Mexico City · opened 1966 · Club América, Mexico

Before

Part of the former ejido, or communal farmland, of Santa Úrsula, on volcanic rock left by the Xitle eruption.64,65 The Buenos Aires Herald reports that about 180,000 tonnes of basalt were blasted away and that hundreds of farming families were relocated; we found no primary record of the relocation.66

The deal

How the communal land passed to the stadium's builders is poorly documented: one uncredited 2016 account says President Adolfo López Mateos sold it to Emilio Azcárraga Milmo.65 The build is put at about 260 million pesos of the day.67 Part of the money came from selling boxes with 99-year rights, which collided in 2026 with FIFA's demand for full control of World Cup venues; the owner, Grupo Ollamani, agreed to pay FIFA US$62.4 million so that box holders could attend.68,69 The 2024–26 renovation, about US$235 million, was private, largely funded by two Banorte loans, hence the new name.69 Mexico City reported spending 200 million pesos on works around the stadium and allocated 1.4 billion to renewing its light rail.70

Getting there

The Tren Ligero light rail has its own Estadio Azteca station and runs an express from Taxqueña on match days.71 For the 2026 World Cup a 1.6 km "last mile" cordon admitted only ticket holders and residents, with seven walking routes of 12 to 30 minutes.71

In, fed, out

Stadium parking was closed for the World Cup and for the November 2026 NFL game.72,73 The box holders' legal claims include bringing their own food and drink.68 It is the only stadium to have hosted three World Cup opening matches (1970, 1986, 2026).64,67

Block 08

La Bombonera

La Boca, Buenos Aires · opened 1940 · Boca Juniors

Before

Railway land of the Ferrocarril del Sud, used as a shunting yard, and then Boca's own wooden stadium, which opened on the same site in 1924 and was last used in April 1938.74,75

The deal

Boca bought the land in 1931; the city's own history gives the price without a clear unit, and accounts differ on whether the seller was the municipality or the railway.74,75 Works on the concrete stadium began on 30 August 1938, while the club played at Ferro Carril Oeste. The contract went to the firm of the architect Viktor Sulčič, of Slovene parentage,, who had designed it in 1932 and won the commission even though the plot looked too small for the crowd it was meant to hold.75,76 That cramped plot is why one side is a flat wall of boxes rather than a curved stand. How the 1938–40 build was paid for is not documented in the sources we found. In March 2026 Boca announced a plan to close the "U" and reach about 80,000 places, without disclosing cost.77

Getting there

There is no metro station nearby; the neighbourhood is served by about twenty colectivo bus lines, and it is about half an hour on foot from San Telmo.78,79 Getting there by car is hard, and the club's lots are for members.74,79

In, fed, out

For the Boca–River match of November 2025 more than 1,000 police set three perimeter rings, gates opened four hours before kick-off and every fan's national ID was checked against banned lists.80 Away fans were banned nationwide in 2013 after a fan was shot dead near La Plata; their return since 2025 has been partial and optional.81,82 Argentine law penalises supplying alcohol within 800 metres of a stadium from four hours before kick-off to two hours after.83

Block 09

Maracanã

Maracanã, Rio de Janeiro · opened 1950 · Flamengo, Fluminense

Before

The disused Derby Club horse-racing track, on land owned by the city government of what was then Brazil's capital. The Army occupied part of the area.84,85

The deal

A 1947 agreement between the federal government and the city gave the job of building it to the city, and the city council passed the enabling bill on 27 October 1947, after a fight over where it should go: the journalist and politician Carlos Lacerda wanted it in Jacarepaguá.85 It opened on 16 June 1950 for the World Cup, and its reported record crowd, 199,854, came at the Brazil–Uruguay match that July.86,87 The 2010–13 rebuild for the 2014 World Cup was budgeted at R$600 million and reached about R$1.2 billion, funded by development-bank loans and the state treasury.88 The state audit court estimated overbilling, and in 2017 prosecutors demanded more than R$200 million back from three contractors; Play the Game reported no convictions.88,89 In 2013, ahead of the works, military police removed the Indigenous "Aldeia Maracanã" community from the old Museu do Índio beside the stadium.90 The state still owns it; Flamengo and Fluminense won a 20-year concession in 2024.91,92

Getting there

Two metro stations on Line 2 face the gates: Maracanã for gates A to C and São Cristóvão for D to F, with a SuperVia suburban station beside the first. On match days MetrôRio keeps both open for boarding until an hour after the final whistle.93,94

In, fed, out

There is no official public parking on match days.93 A 2015 state law allows beer only, sold from the opening of the gates to the end of the match in plastic or paper.95 On the eve of the 1950 opening, the engineers and workers played a kickabout on the pitch.96

Block 10

Wembley Stadium

Wembley Park, London · opened 2007 · England, cup finals

Before

The 1923 Empire Stadium, itself built on the site of Watkin's Tower, a Victorian attempt to outdo the Eiffel Tower that stalled at 47 metres and was blown up in 1907. The old stadium went up around the craters left by its foundations.97

The deal

The Football Association's subsidiary, Wembley National Stadium Ltd, paid £103 million for the old stadium and its business in 1999, mainly with a £120 million National Lottery grant from Sport England.98 Public money in all came to £161 million, including £20 million from the culture department and £21 million from the London Development Agency.98,99 The new stadium's cost was reported at £798 million at handover in 2007.100 The FA did not buy the land around it, which the developer Quintain went on to build on.101

Getting there

Wembley Park (Jubilee and Metropolitan) and Wembley Stadium station (Chiltern Railways) are each about eight minutes' walk.102 The Tube station was rebuilt for events in 2006 with a separate events ticket hall.103 Surveys in 2018 found 78 to 88 per cent of spectators arriving by public transport and about 6 to 7 per cent by car.1

In, fed, out

Olympic Way, the pedestrian spine from the station, was built for the 1948 Olympics, partly by German prisoners of war.97 At football the stadium bars drinking in the bowl or in view of the pitch, and for UEFA and FIFA competitive matches it limits sales to restaurants and hospitality.104 The Casey Review of the Euro 2020 final, as summarised in a Brent council report, described "reckless and life endangering" behaviour outside the ground, much of it by people under the influence of alcohol or drugs.105 In September 2024 Brent's planning committee approved raising the cap to 54 major events a year.1,106,107

Block 11

Emirates Stadium

Holloway, London · opened 2006 · Arsenal

Before

Ashburton Grove, an industrial and waste-disposal estate about 450 metres from Highbury, including Islington's waste recycling centre.108,109

The deal

Islington made a compulsory purchase order covering 134 plots in 2002. The planning inspector recommended against it; the Secretary of State confirmed it anyway in 2004, and the High Court dismissed a challenge by businesses on Queensland Road in January 2005.108 Arsenal paid about £60 million to rebuild the recycling centre at Lough Road.110 The stadium was privately financed: £357 million at financial close in 2004, with a £260 million bank loan, £97 million of club equity followed that October by a £100 million Emirates sponsorship deal.110 Transport for London described £50 million of transport improvements around it.111

Getting there

Arsenal station on the Piccadilly line is about three minutes away, and Finsbury Park and Highbury & Islington about ten.112 Holloway Road, the nearest station to the south, is exit-only before matches and closed for up to two hours afterwards; TfL judged in 2006 that even costly work would not raise its capacity enough.111,112

In, fed, out

After the whistle, stewards run managed queues outside three stations and close Hornsey Road to pedestrians.112 Islington's matchday parking controls cover most of the surrounding streets.113 The ground is cashless.114

Block 12

Stade de France

Saint-Denis, north of Paris · opened 1998 · France

Before

A Gaz de France gasworks at Le Cornillon. An October 1993 audit found more than 50,000 cubic metres of contaminated soil, and a protective wall and monitoring pipes were installed under the stadium after hydrocarbons and solvents were found in the groundwater.115

The deal

The state organised the project and bore the clean-up. Of the 2.7 billion francs construction cost, it paid 1.254 billion, according to a 1998 ministerial answer; a Senate report gives about €375 million, with €193 million from the state.115,116,117 Because no club plays there, the state paid the operator an "absence of resident club" indemnity, €114 million in all before a 2013 amendment suspended it.117 A new operator, GL events, took over under a 30-year concession in August 2025.118

Getting there

The stadium is served by two RER lines, Metro 13 and the T8 tram, each 10 to 15 minutes' walk according to the local tourist board, plus Metro 14. About 1,000 permanent bike spaces sit at the foot of the stadium.119

In, fed, out

The weakness showed at the 2022 Champions League final. With RER B on strike, more than 37,000 people came through the RER D station, more than three times the usual number, into a screening point only 4 to 5 metres wide. The Senate's inquiry found the state had failed to manage the crowd and criticised the blame placed on Liverpool supporters.3,120 French law bans alcohol sales at sports venues in principle, with exceptions for hospitality and limited temporary permits.121

Block 13

Spotify Camp Nou

Les Corts, Barcelona · opened 1957 · FC Barcelona

Before

Land beside the old Can Planes country house, between the district's maternity hospital and its cemetery, a pairing locals called "the whole of a life". Its earlier use is not described in the club's histories.122

The deal

Barça's members chose the land by referendum in November 1950, 7,835 votes to 1,132, and the club completed the purchase that December for 10,029,945 pesetas; a supporters' history gives a higher figure.122,123 The stadium was budgeted at 66.6 million pesetas and cost around 288 million, leaving the club heavily in debt until it sold its old Les Corts ground to a developer for 226 million in 1966.124,125 The current rebuild is financed with €1.45 billion raised from investors in 2023, which the club says is not secured on the stadium.126 Under the 2018 planning change, the club pays for re-urbanising about 111,000 square metres of surrounding land as public space.127

Getting there

Three metro lines and the tram ring the site, with a dedicated perimeter bus on match days. There is no general car parking, but the club provides 3,200 motorbike spaces.128,129

In, fed, out

Spanish law bans bringing in, selling or drinking alcohol at sports venues, and the club's entry rules ban cans and caps on bottles.130,131 Barça returned on 22 November 2025 after 909 days away; as of October 2026 the ground was licensed for 62,652, with the top tier and roof still to come.132,133,134

Block 14

Santiago Bernabéu

Chamartín, Madrid · opened 1947 · Real Madrid

Before

Undeveloped rustic land beside the club's old Chamartín ground, in the path of the planned extension of the Paseo de la Castellana.135

The deal

The land was bought from private families in 1944, and the stadium was financed with bonds issued by the club with the backing of a bank.135 The modern land story is a public one. In 2011 Madrid compensated the club with land valued at €22.7 million for a plot it had been promised in 1998 but could not legally hand over; the European Commission ruled that unlawful state aid in 2016 and the club repaid about €20.3 million including interest, then the EU General Court annulled the decision in 2019.136,137 The 2019–25 rebuild was financed by club loans, beginning with €575 million in 2019, and reported running totals put it at €1.35 billion to €1.4 billion.138,139,140,141

Getting there

Metro Line 10 normally stops beside the stadium, but the section was closed from 28 March 2026 to the end of the year while the station is rebuilt; elDiario.es reported the new station is due in early 2027.142,143 A free replacement bus runs every two to four minutes in the daytime.143 The club's planned car parks were provisionally halted by the courts; in February 2026 the Madrid High Court upheld the suspension, finding the scheme served private rather than public interest.144

In, fed, out

Spanish law bans alcohol in the stands.131 The Bernabéu Market food hall opened in November 2025 and closes two hours before kick-off on match days.145 A residents' association is challenging whether the stadium's licences allow concerts; in June 2026 the Supreme Court refused to admit the club's appeal against a Madrid High Court ruling, sending the case back to a lower court.146

Block 15

San Siro (Stadio Giuseppe Meazza)

San Siro, Milan · opened 1926 · AC Milan, Inter

Before

Land on the city's western edge next to a racecourse built a few years earlier; the sources we found say nothing more about it.147,148

The deal

AC Milan's president Piero Pirelli built the stadium in 1925–26, for about 5 million lire by one account. The city bought it in 1935.147,148 Ninety years later the city sold it back: the council approved the sale to Milan and Inter at a valuation of €197 million on the night of 29–30 September 2025, shortly before a heritage protection on the second tier would have taken effect, and the deed was signed on 5 November.149,150 Milan prosecutors have opened an inquiry into the tender; no wrongdoing has been established.151 The clubs plan a new stadium by Foster + Partners and MANICA, with construction now expected to start in 2028, and most of the Meazza is to be demolished.149,152

Getting there

Metro line M5 ends at San Siro Stadio, and Lotto on M1 is about 15 minutes' walk. ATM runs late M5 trains after matches and closes some stations around full time on police instruction.153,154 Inter's advice for 2026–27 is to use public transport, because car parking has been reduced and will shrink further.155

In, fed, out

The paninari, street vendors selling salamella sausage sandwiches and beer outside the stadium, have fed crowds for decades; since August 2026 buyers pay with tokens bought through an app, and the plans for the new stadium do not say where they would go.156 For higher-risk matches the prefect bans takeaway alcohol in the surrounding streets.157

Block 16

Allianz Arena

Fröttmaning, Munich · opened 2005 · Bayern Munich

Before

A plot zoned for commercial use beside the Fröttmaninger Berg, the city's old rubbish tip, which gradually swallowed the village of Fröttmaning. Only its church survives.158,159

The deal

Munich voters approved the project on 21 October 2001 by 65.8 per cent to 34.2.160 The land is city-owned and held by the stadium company under a long ground lease.158,161 The stadium, €340 million by the arena's own figures, was privately financed by Bayern and TSV 1860, and Bayern says it paid the debt off in 2014.158,162,163 A 2006 question in the Bavarian parliament put the public cost of the roads and rail at just under €300 million; the government's answer itemised €104 million of city investment and €29.3 million to rebuild Fröttmaning U-Bahn station.158

Getting there

The U6 reaches Fröttmaning in about 16 to 20 minutes from Marienplatz, and the station was rebuilt with four tracks for event crowds. From there it is a signposted 1.1 km walk, about 15 minutes.164,165,166 A Bayern ticket does not include public transport.167

In, fed, out

The site has 9,800 spaces in four garages, 1,200 in the stadium and 350 coach bays.162 Around 11,000 cars can take up to two and a half hours to clear, so the operator invites fans to linger at the fan restaurants, store or museum.167 In 2017 the operator said 80 bike stands were enough.168

Block 17

Tokyo Dome

Kōraku, Bunkyō, Tokyo · opened 1988 · Yomiuri Giants (NPB)

Before

The Korakuen Velodrome, which ran from 1949 to 1972, next to the old Korakuen Stadium. Before that the area was a feudal lord's garden estate and, from 1871, an army weapons factory; in 1944 the army took over the neighbouring ballpark and grew potatoes and corn on its field.169,170

The deal

Korakuen Stadium Co. was founded in December 1936 and bought just over 9,500 tsubo, about 31,400 square metres, of the former arsenal site, sold off by the state.170,171 The 1988 dome was a private development; we found no reliable construction figure and no public money. Mitsui Fudosan took over the operator in a friendly bid in 2021.172

Getting there

Four stations on six lines sit at or beside the complex, including JR Suidobashi and Tokyo Metro Kōrakuen, about 8 to 10 minutes from Tokyo Station.173,174,175 There are about 670 car spaces for a building that holds up to 55,000, and the operator recommends public transport.176

In, fed, out

The dome is fully cashless, and visitors may bring food but not alcohol.174,177 Beer comes to the seats on the backs of uriko, sellers carrying kegs of up to 15 kg who sell about 20,000 cups at an average night game, AFP reported in 2023.178 The roof is held up by air pressure about 0.3 per cent above that outside, and slopes on the garden side so sunlight still reaches the 17th-century Koishikawa Kōrakuen garden.173

Block 18

Hanshin Koshien Stadium

Nishinomiya, Hyōgo · opened 1924 · Hanshin Tigers (NPB), national high-school tournaments

Before

The beds of two branch channels of the Muko River, the Edagawa and the Mōshigawa, cut off by river works around 1920–23; sources differ on the year.179,180,181

The deal

Hyōgo Prefecture, short of money, sold the dead riverbed to Hanshin Electric Railway for ¥4.1 million including river works.181,182 The railway built a package on it: housing, whose buyers got a free year's rail pass, a tram line along the old channel and, in 1924, the ballpark, built in under five months.180,181,183 The name comes from kinoe-ne, the year 1924 in the sexagenary cycle.183 No public subsidy for the build is documented.

Getting there

Hanshin Koshien station is about two minutes' walk from the stadium, roughly 15 minutes from Umeda in Osaka.184,185 Stadium Journey's tip is to buy your return ticket on arrival, because the machines are swamped after games.186 The stadium has no public car park.184,187

In, fed, out

Tigers fans release balloons in the seventh inning, a custom suspended from 2020 and resumed on 7 April 2026.186,188 Koshien curry has reportedly been served since 1924.185 At the high-school tournaments losing players scoop infield soil into bags to take home; in 1958 the first Okinawan team was not allowed to take theirs, because Okinawa was under US administration.183,189

Block 19

Beijing National Stadium

Olympic Green, Beijing · opened 2008 · Olympics, concerts

Before

The corn and wheat fields of Wali village, home to about 3,000 people and demolished in 2005. NPR reported that each family received about US$120,000 in compensation.190

The deal

The stadium was built under a 30-year franchise, with the city's state-assets company holding 58 per cent and a CITIC-led consortium 42; ownership reverts to the city at the end.191,192 Reported costs run from RMB 3.1 billion to RMB 3.5 billion. A 2004 cost review dropped the planned retractable roof and cut seating from 100,000 to 80,000.191,192 In 2009 management passed quietly to a state-owned institution to stem losses.193 Citywide, a housing-rights group claimed in 2007 that the Games would displace 1.5 million people; China's Foreign Ministry called that "groundless" and said 6,037 households had been demolished for nine venues, all compensated.194

Getting there

Line 8's Olympic Sports Center station is about 823 metres from the stadium, and Line 8 and Line 15 also serve Olympic Park station.195,196 We found no figures for parking.

In, fed, out

Its second life is as a concert venue: 39 large concerts and more than 2.16 million attendees in 2024.197 From 2025 police introduced colour-coded tickets, stands and gates, with separate departure routes for subway, bus and ride-hailing users.198

Block 20

Eden Gardens

Maidan, Kolkata · ground from 1864 · Bengal, Kolkata Knight Riders (IPL), India

Before

The extended part of the Eden Gardens pleasure ground, named after Lord Auckland's sisters, on the Maidan, where in 1854–55 grazing rights were let for Rs 500 a year: horses, cows, goats, sheep and donkeys allowed, pigs and buffalo not.199

The deal

The Army at Fort William had seen the Calcutta Cricket Club as an "encroacher", and in 1871 the government approved a pavilion only on condition that it could be removed without compensation.199 The land still belongs to the Ministry of Defence, with the Cricket Association of Bengal as leaseholder, as a Calcutta High Court order of 2025 recorded.200 The courts have also held that the ground is not a "public place" for municipal advertisement tax, and the Supreme Court dismissed the city's appeal in November 2025.200 The 2011 World Cup renovation cost Rs 70 crore, paid by the association and the national board.201

Getting there

Eden Gardens station on the Kolkata Circular Railway is named for the ground. For IPL 2025 the metro ran midnight specials from Esplanade, with a Rs 10 surcharge, plus late suburban trains and 23 special buses.202,203,204

In, fed, out

Police run colour-coded, sticker-controlled parking on the Maidan, which the Army objected to in 2025.205,206 IPL ticket terms ban outside food and say alcohol may be drunk only in designated areas, "if any".207 A Burmese pagoda brought from Prome and was set up in the gardens in 1856.199

Block 21

Optus Stadium (Perth Stadium)

Burswood Peninsula, Perth · opened 2018 · West Coast Eagles, Fremantle (AFL)

Before

Mudflat granted to a settler in 1829, later home to sewage filtration beds, a cement works that dredged river shells and rubbish tips, and from 1988 to 2013 a public golf course.208,209 Accounts differ on when dumping stopped.208,209,210

The deal

The land was the state's, and the state kept the stadium. It was built under a design-build-finance-maintain partnership with the Westadium consortium, with the state paying 60 per cent of construction costs during the build and maintenance contracted for 25 years.211 ABC News put the cost of stadium plus transport at about A$1.6 billion in late 2017, around A$1,500 per Western Australian household.212

Getting there

Perth Stadium station has six platforms, second on the network only to Perth itself, and event trains run on all lines.213,214 Event tickets include travel.215,216 The Matagarup footbridge to East Perth opened in July 2018; its name means "a place where the river is only leg deep".217,218 In its first year, 76 per cent of fans came by bus or train, and 54 per cent of all fans by train, against a target of 83 per cent.2,211

In, fed, out

Parking is limited and most drivers are sent to the city's car parks.219 Full-strength beer reached the general seating areas only in 2026, eight years after opening, alongside reusable cups for every patron.220 The outside pub, The Camfield, is named after the 1829 settler.208,210

Block 22

FNB Stadium (Soccer City)

Nasrec, Johannesburg · opened 1989, rebuilt 2010 · Kaizer Chiefs, Bafana Bafana

Before

The 1989 stadium, rebuilt in place for the 2010 World Cup, which kept its moat and reused material from the demolished sections. It sits in former industrial land among the mine dumps of the Witwatersrand gold fields.221,222,223

The deal

The City of Johannesburg owns it.224 The 2007–10 rebuild rose from a budget of R1.9 billion to about R3.3 billion, blamed on materials, scope and import duties; the city's development agency gives R3.8 billion.221,225 The city spent more than R120 million on the neighbouring expo centre and R82 million on the pedestrian promenade and bridge.225 We could not establish how the original land was acquired or how the public funding was split.

Getting there

Nasrec Metrorail station and Rea Vaya bus-rapid-transit stops sit at a transport hub linked to the stadium by a promenade of about 350 metres and a bridge over the road and rail tracks, with combined capacity of about 23,280 passengers an hour.225 We could not confirm current match-day services.

In, fed, out

The building is the Calabash, an African pot in fibre-concrete panels with a ring of lights at its base like fire beneath it.221,222 Nelson Mandela addressed a rally here after his release in 1990, and his memorial service was held here in December 2013.222,224,226
