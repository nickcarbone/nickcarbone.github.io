An atlas of local sporting spectacles

The Home Crowd

Fifteen sporting spectacles that belong to the places that stage them, from Friday wrestling at Arena México to Christmas Day in Kirkwall: origins, rules, rivalries, rituals and how to get in.

The fifteen events in this atlas fill stadiums, squares and whole towns, and most of them would make little sense anywhere else. Ireland's best county hurlers play their final in front of more than 80,000 at Croke Park. Florence stages a 27-a-side brawl on sand in a Renaissance square. Ashbourne boards up its shop windows for two days of football with goals three miles apart, and Pikine fills a 20,000-seat arena for wrestlers who are allowed to punch.

Each entry covers where the event came from, with legend kept apart from record, and how it is played. It also gives when and where to go, how an outsider gets in, what the crowd does and the names locals still talk about. The choice favours living traditions with big local followings over shows put on for visitors. Ticketing and dates change, so anything that does is marked with the year it was true.

Grappling & combat · Mexico City, Mexico

Lucha libre

Friday night at Arena México

All year

When

Cards every Friday night at Arena México, all year. The CMLL anniversary show, the company's biggest night, is held in September.7,1

Where

Arena México, Colonia Doctores (opened 1956; about 16,500 for wrestling, though published figures vary). Arena Coliseo in the historic centre (1943) still hosts some shows.7,3,8

Getting in

Box office on the night for ordinary cards; Ticketmaster or the CMLL website in advance for big shows. Prices are low by the standards of big-city sport.3

CMLL was founded by Salvador Lutteroth in 1933 as the Empresa Mexicana de Lucha Libre; it has been the Consejo Mundial de Lucha Libre since 1991.1,2 Every Friday its wrestlers fill the concrete bowl on Calle Dr Lavista, a block of Colonia Doctores that becomes a street market of masks and snacks for the evening.3

The cast divides into técnicos, who fight clean and fly, and rudos, who cheat and brawl. Many bouts are best of three falls and the trios match, three a side, is the staple.2,3 The highest stakes are the luchas de apuestas. Lose a mask-against-mask match and you unmask in the ring and give your real name; wrestlers who no longer wear one bet their hair. The first recorded mask wager, in 1940, cost El Murciélago Enmascarado his mask to Octavio Gaona.2

In 2018 Mexico City declared lucha libre part of the city's intangible cultural heritage. The declaration is the city's own; lucha libre is not on UNESCO's list.4

In the stands

Vendors work the rows with tacos, tortas, beer and micheladas. Shouted insults are part of the show and ringside is the rowdiest part of the house. Masks are sold at street stalls outside and at the official shop inside.3

Names to know

El Santo
Rodolfo Guzmán Huerta (1917–1984) made more than 50 films and was buried in his silver mask.5
Blue Demon
Alejandro Muñoz Moreno took the NWA world welterweight title from El Santo in 1953, the start of the rivalry that defined them both.6

Ball games · Dublin, Ireland

Hurling

The All-Ireland final

Jul

When

The All-Ireland Senior Hurling final has been played in July since the GAA split its season in 2022. It used to fall in late summer.16,17

Where

Croke Park, Dublin: capacity 82,300.18,19

Getting in

No final tickets go on general sale. Most of the allocation goes through county boards and clubs, so the route in is through a GAA club.19

UNESCO added hurling and camogie to its intangible heritage list in 2018. Its listing describes a game that dates back 2,000 years and turns up in the Cú Chulainn saga; that antiquity rests on legend and old manuscripts, not on a documented date.9,10 The modern game belongs to the Gaelic Athletic Association, founded at Thurles in County Tipperary in 1884.10

Fifteen players a side carry ash hurleys and strike a small, cork-centred ball, the sliotar. Over the crossbar is a point; under it, into the net, a goal worth three. The ball may be caught and balanced or bounced on the stick, but never thrown.10

Counties are the unit and memories are long. Kilkenny hold the record with 36 All-Irelands, Cork have 30 and Tipperary 29; Limerick won their 13th in 2026 (all as of 2026).11,12 Tipperary against Kilkenny is a rivalry that goes back more than a century.13 The winners lift the Liam MacCarthy Cup, presented to the GAA in 1922 by Liam MacCarthy, a London-born Irishman, and first awarded in 1923; the original is now in the GAA Museum.11

In the stands

Hill 16, the terrace at one end of Croke Park, was first called Hill 60 and renamed in memory of the 1916 Rising.18 Finals sell out: the 2025 decider between Tipperary and Cork drew 82,331.11

Names to know

Henry Shefflin
Kilkenny forward with a record 10 All-Ireland medals.14
Christy Ring
Cork's great hurler won eight All-Irelands and a record 18 Railway Cup medals; up to 60,000 people attended his funeral in 1979.15

Ball games · Basque Country and Navarre, Spain

Basque pelota

Pelota a mano and the Manomanista

Apr · May · Jun

When

The Manomanista, the singles championship of hand pelota, runs through the spring with its final in late May or early June. The 2026 final was on 31 May.22

Where

The final rotates. Regular stages include Frontón Bizkaia in Bilbao (opened 2011, about 3,000 seats), Labrit in Pamplona (945 seats) and Atano III in San Sebastián; the 2026 final was at Navarra Arena in Pamplona.26,27,22

Getting in

Frontón Bizkaia sells tickets on its own website; smaller frontones sell at the door.26

The Basque wall games grew up in the 18th century as French jeu de paume faded.20 Hand pelota, the most prized of them, is played bare-handed or with only light protection, one against one or in pairs, with a ball of 92 to 95 grams driven against a front wall so the opponent can't return it. The standard court with a left-hand wall is 36 metres long.20

The Manomanista was created in 1940 and first won by Atano III, whose name is now on the San Sebastián frontón.21 The record holder is Julián Retegi, Retegi II, with 11 titles.21 The champion's prize is a txapela, the Basque beret. When Darío Gómez won in 2026 he took the first one to La Rioja in 73 years.22

Jai alai, played with a curved wicker basket, the cesta, is the faster, exported cousin. Britannica says it got the name, meaning "merry festival", when it reached Cuba in 1900, and says ball speeds of around 240 km/h are not unusual.23

In the stands

The betting is part of the spectacle. Corredores stand between court and crowd calling odds on red or blue; a bet is sealed with a paper slip, and by custom the loser pays before leaving.28 Labrit, nicknamed "la Bombonera", still allows betting in the seats.27 A veteran corredor estimates that frontón betting has fallen by about 90 per cent since the 2008 crash.29

Names to know

Aimar Olaizola (Olaizola II)
Manomanista champion in 2005, 2007, 2012 and 2013.24
Juan Martínez de Irujo
Won 13 txapelas, five of them in the Manomanista. His rivalry with Olaizola II, he says, made both men better and filled the frontones.25

Grappling & combat · Pikine, Dakar, Senegal

Senegalese wrestling

Laamb, or lutte avec frappe

All year

When

The national federation runs a long season of fight days; there were 89 days of lutte avec frappe in 2025–26.35 The combats royaux between the top names are single, heavily promoted fixtures.

Where

Arène nationale de lutte, Pikine, on the edge of Dakar: more than 20,000 seats.34,36

Getting in

Big fights sell out, and many are televised live.37

Senegal's national sport is wrestling with punches. Unlike almost every other form of wrestling, laamb allows strikes with the hand. A fighter loses if he is forced onto his back, onto all fours or out of the ring.30 A bout can be over in 90 seconds or run to 15 minutes.31

It grew from village tournaments held after the rains; Culture Trip says it is said to date back to Serer warriors of the 13th century, a tradition rather than a documented date.30 National Geographic describes a sport rooted in centuries-old war training in the villages.31 Today it is a business. The federation's rules set headline fees at 30 to 40 million CFA francs, and one promoter told allAfrica the biggest names earn 100 million or more.32

The prize that matters is the title roi des arènes, king of the arenas, which changes hands only when the holder is beaten. There have been nine kings since Manga 2 won the first in 1984. Sa Thiès took the title from Modou Lô on 5 April 2026 (as of November 2026).33 The arena where such fights happen was financed and built by China and opened on 22 July 2018, during a visit by Xi Jinping; before that, wrestling used football stadiums.34

In the stands

The build-up can last longer than the fight. Each wrestler arrives with marabouts and drummers and is covered in gris-gris, charms worn on the waist and arms. Then comes the bàkk, a boastful, danced self-introduction meant to unsettle the opponent.30,31 National Geographic followed wrestlers through ritual baths, one of them in milk.31

Names to know

Yékini
Yakhya Diop held the king's title from 2004 until Balla Gaye 2 beat him in 2012, an eight-year reign.33
Modou Lô
King from 2019 until April 2026.33

Grappling & combat · Tokyo, Osaka, Nagoya and Fukuoka, Japan

Sumo

The six grand tournaments

Jan · Mar · May · Jul · Sep · Nov

When

Six 15-day tournaments (honbasho) a year: January, May and September in Tokyo; March in Osaka; July in Nagoya; November in Fukuoka.42

Where

Ryōgoku Kokugikan, Tokyo (opened 1985, about 11,000 seats); EDION Arena Osaka; IG Arena, Nagoya, the July venue since 2025; Fukuoka Kokusai Center.42,43,44

Getting in

Tickets go on sale through the Japan Sumo Association's official ticket service some weeks before each tournament.45 Lower divisions start in the morning; the top division enters the ring in the late afternoon.46

Tradition puts sumo's origins about 2,000 years back, in Shinto ritual; the legendary first wrestler is Nomi no Sukune. These dates are traditional rather than documented. It became a spectator sport in the early 1600s.38,39 The six-tournament year was fixed in 1958, and tournaments have run to 15 days since 1949.38

The rules fit in a sentence: push your opponent out of the ring or make any part of him except the soles of his feet touch the clay.39 Around that sits ceremony. Wrestlers throw salt to purify the ring and stamp to drive out evil. The top division enters each afternoon in embroidered aprons for the dohyō-iri, and a bow-twirling ritual closes the day.38

In the stands

When a yokozuna loses, cushions fly. Throwing zabuton into the ring is officially discouraged as dangerous, and some venues tie them together in pairs.47 At the Kokugikan the snack is yakitori from the building's own kitchens; fans buy it as a lucky food.44,48

Names to know

Hakuhō
Won a record 45 top-division championships before retiring in 2021.40
Taihō
Won 32. His 45-bout winning streak ended in 1969 in a loss that photographs suggested was a mistake by the officials, which helped bring in video review.41

Ball games · Florence, Italy

Calcio storico

Calcio storico fiorentino

Jun

When

Two semi-finals in mid-June, then the final on 24 June, the feast of St John the Baptist, Florence's patron saint.49,50

Where

Piazza Santa Croce, covered in sand and ringed with temporary stands.49,50

Getting in

Tickets go on sale online through TicketOne, often only days before the matches, with a limit per buyer.50

Four teams, one for each historic quarter: the Azzurri of Santa Croce, the Bianchi of Santo Spirito, the Rossi of Santa Maria Novella and the Verdi of San Giovanni.49,50 The Azzurri have won most often since the modern revival.49 Painted balls drawn on Easter Sunday decide the semi-finals.50

Twenty-seven men a side play for 50 minutes on a sand pitch about 100 metres by 50, with no substitutes for the injured or sent off. A ball thrown or kicked over the net along the end is a caccia, and a cannon fires for each one.49,50 Punching, wrestling and choking are allowed; kicks to the head and ganging up on one man are not. Players must be Florentine by birth or by ten years' residence, with no criminal record.49

The best-known match came on 17 February 1530, when Florentines played in defiance of the imperial army of Charles V besieging the city. Giovanni de' Bardi wrote the rules down in 1580. The game faded in the 17th century and was revived in 1930.49,50 Visit Florence still lists a Chianina calf as the prize; other accounts say the winners now play for a celebratory dinner.49,50

In the stands

Each match begins with a costumed historical parade, the corteo storico, which sets out from Piazza Santa Maria Novella and crosses the city to Santa Croce. On the night of the final, fireworks go up from Piazzale Michelangelo.50

Races & gatherings · Siena, Italy

Palio di Siena

The horse race in the Campo

Jul · Aug

When

2 July and 16 August, after several days of draws, trial races and street suppers.51,53

Where

Piazza del Campo, Siena.53

Getting in

Standing in the centre of the square is free, but it fills early and you can't leave once it is closed. Seats in the stands and on balconies are expensive.53,54

Seventeen contrade, Siena's neighbourhood societies, but only ten run each time: the seven that sat out the same race the previous year, plus three drawn by lot. Horses are assigned by lot too. Jockeys ride bareback for three laps of the sloping, sand-covered square, about 90 seconds. They may whip each other's horses, and a horse that has lost its rider can still win.51,52

Horse racing in Siena goes back to the Middle Ages. The race around the Campo took its modern form in the 17th century, and a second race, on 16 August, was added in 1701.51,52 The prize is the drappellone, a painted silk banner by a different artist each year, and there is no prize money.51,53 Animal-welfare groups have long campaigned against the race because of horse deaths.51

In the stands

Each horse is blessed in its contrada's church, and if it leaves droppings there, that is taken as a good omen.54 On the eve of the race each contrada sits down to a long supper in its streets.53 Rivalries between neighbouring contrade are fierce, and a win is celebrated for weeks.54

Names to know

Aceto
Andrea Degortes holds the jockeys' record with 14 wins between 1964 and 1996.51

Races & gatherings · Ulaanbaatar and across Mongolia

Naadam

The three games of men

Jul

When

11–13 July each year in Ulaanbaatar, with local naadams across the country.55,56

Where

National Sports Stadium, Ulaanbaatar, for the opening ceremony and wrestling. The horse races are held outside the city.56

Getting in

Ceremony tickets are limited and usually go on sale about a week before; tour companies sell them in advance.56

UNESCO inscribed Naadam in 2010. It describes a festival of three contests: wrestling, horse racing and archery. Competitors include children, and winners are given titles.55 Naadam has been an annual national holiday since 1921. Older origins in steppe war games and the age of Genghis Khan come from tradition.56

The national wrestling tournament takes 512 wrestlers through nine knockout rounds, with no weight classes. A wrestler loses when anything but his feet or hands touches the ground. Titles go by rounds won, from Falcon at five up to Lion at nine, with Titan reserved for the champion. Wrestlers wear the open-chested zodog jacket and tight shorts, and each has a zasuul, a second who acts as herald.57 The horse races run over roughly 10 to 26 kilometres depending on the horses' age, mostly with child jockeys, a practice that has led to criticism and tighter safety rules.56,58

In the stands

Before and after bouts the wrestlers perform the eagle dance, arms spread like wings; the loser ducks under the winner's arm.57 At the archery, judges sing out "uukhai" when an arrow hits the target.58 Crowds press round the winning horse, whose sweat is thought to bring luck, and the festival food is khuushuur, fried meat pastries.56

Grappling & combat · Edirne, Türkiye

Kırkpınar

Oil wrestling at Edirne

Jun · Jul

When

Three days in late June or early July, ending with the final on the Sunday; dates are set each year.62,60

Where

Sarayiçi meadow, Edirne, the festival's home since 1924.62,60

Getting in

Edirne municipality runs the festival; check its announcements for ticketing.60

Hundreds of wrestlers, soaked in olive oil and wearing kıspet, heavy trousers of water-buffalo or calf leather, compete on grass on the edge of Edirne.59,60 A wrestler wins by turning his opponent's back to the ground or by lifting him and carrying him three steps.61 Until 1975 there was no time limit, and the great bouts ran from morning until dusk.62

The origin is a legend about two soldiers who wrestled until both died; a spring rose by their graves, and the field became Kırkpınar, "forty springs". The festival counts its editions from the early 1360s. The 2026 festival was billed as the 665th, though Hürriyet notes that the exact start date is unclear.61,60 Guinness recognised it in 2001 as the oldest sports competition, a claim complicated by the years when war stopped it.61

A wrestler who wins the head-wrestler title three years running wins the golden belt.61 The festival's patron, the ağa, buys the role at auction.60

In the stands

Forty pairs of davul drums and zurna pipes open the festival, and the golden belt is paraded through the town.59 Before each bout the cazgır, the announcer, introduces the wrestlers in rhyming verse and leads the prayers.59,61

Names to know

Ahmet Taşçı
Head wrestler through most of the 1990s and the last man to win the golden belt outright, in 1997.61,60

Grappling & combat · Bangkok, Thailand

Muay Thai

Rajadamnern and Lumpinee

All year

When

Fight cards run most nights of the week; Rajadamnern's schedule is split between several promotions (as of 2025).68

Where

Rajadamnern Stadium, Rajadamnern Nok Road (first fights 1945). Lumpinee Boxing Stadium, run by the Royal Thai Army, on Ram Intra Road since 2014.63,64

Getting in

Box office on the night or e-tickets online; seats run from ringside down to third class.68

Bangkok's two national stadiums set the standard for the sport. Rajadamnern was ordered in 1941, halted by the war, and held its first fights on 23 December 1945; a roof followed in 1951, and its rules became the model for stadiums across Thailand.63 Lumpinee opened on 8 December 1956 and moved from Rama IV Road to a new building on Ram Intra Road in 2014.64

Thailand's cabinet approved a UNESCO nomination for Muay Thai in March 2024. As of mid-2026 it was not yet inscribed.65,66

In the stands

Before each fight the boxers perform the wai khru ram muay, a slow dance honouring their teachers, and the crowd is asked to keep quiet.68 The band plays sarama on the pi chawa, an oboe-like reed, with drums and small cymbals; the tempo rises with the fight.69 In the stands, gamblers bet on each round with hand signals.68

Names to know

Dieselnoi Chor Thanasukarn
Held the Lumpinee lightweight title for four years in the early 1980s and retired when he ran out of opponents.64
Samart Payakaroon
Lumpinee champion at four weights, then WBC super bantamweight boxing champion in 1986.67

Grappling & combat · Switzerland (host town rotates)

Schwingen

The Eidgenössisches Schwing- und Älplerfest

Aug

When

Every three years, in late August. The 2025 festival was in Mollis, in Glarus, so the next falls in 2028.70,74

Where

A purpose-built temporary arena. Pratteln's in 2022 held about 50,000.73,72

Getting in

Tickets sell out well in advance, and members of the wrestling federation's clubs get a large share. Without one, watch on the big screens outside the arena or on Swiss television.72

Swiss belt wrestling appears in images from the 13th century; the oldest is in Lausanne cathedral. The authorities banned it in the 16th and 17th centuries, partly because it was held on religious holidays. The alpine games at Unspunnen near Interlaken in 1805 helped revive it.70 The federal festival has been held regularly since 1895, and every three years since 1974.70

Two wrestlers in jute shorts pulled over their trousers grip each other by the shorts in a sawdust ring 12 metres across. A bout is won by putting both of the opponent's shoulders on the ground while still holding his shorts. When neither manages it, three judges score the bout.71,70 The winner brushes the sawdust off the loser's back.72

Pratteln drew about 400,000 people over the festival in 2022.73 In Mollis in 2025, Armon Orlik became the 47th Schwingerkönig, and up to 955,000 people watched the final bout on SRF 1.74 The king's prize is a young bull.71,72

In the stands

Between bouts there is Hornussen, a Swiss bat-and-puck game, alongside stone-throwing, alphorns, yodelling and flag-throwing. Lonely Planet calls the crowd family-friendly and harmonious.72

Names to know

Jörg Abderhalden
Won his third king's title in 2007.71

Ball games · Melbourne, Australia

Australian rules football

The AFL Grand Final

Sep

When

Usually the last Saturday of September. It was moved to Brisbane in 2020 and Perth in 2021 during the pandemic.76

Where

Melbourne Cricket Ground: capacity about 100,000.80

Getting in

Most seats go through club, AFL and Melbourne Cricket Club memberships, with limited public packages. Resale at more than 10 per cent above face value is banned under Victoria's major-events law.81

In July 1858 the cricketer Tom Wills wrote to a Melbourne paper proposing a football club to keep cricketers fit in winter. On 17 May 1859 he and three others wrote the first rules at the Parade Hotel, a short walk from the MCG. The AFL calls the result the oldest codified form of football in the world.75 Those first rules already included the mark, a catch from a kick that earns a free kick, and a ban on throwing the ball.75

The Grand Final crowd record, 121,696, was set when Carlton played Collingwood in 1970; with the old standing areas gone it is unlikely to be beaten.76 Carlton, Collingwood and Essendon have 16 premierships each.77 Brisbane's win over Fremantle in 2026 was its third in a row, in front of 100,023 (as of 2026).78 The best player on the day receives the Norm Smith Medal, awarded since 1979.79

In the stands

Before the bounce, Mike Brady sings "Up There Cazaly" and both teams run through paper banners made by their supporters.82

Ball games · Ashbourne, Derbyshire, England

Royal Shrovetide Football

Up'ards against Down'ards

Feb · Mar

When

Shrove Tuesday and Ash Wednesday, 2pm to 10pm each day. The dates move with Easter, so they fall in February or early March.83

Where

The whole town. The ball is "turned up" from a plinth in the Shaw Croft car park; the goals are about three miles apart.83

Getting in

There is nothing to buy: you stand in the street, at a safe distance. The game is not televised.85

Whether you play for the Up'ards or the Down'ards depends on which side of the Henmore Brook you were born. The goals are millstones at the sites of Sturston Mill and Clifton Mill, and a ball is goaled by tapping it three times against the stone.83,84 A goal before 6pm means a new ball is turned up; a goal after 6pm ends the day's play.85

The rules are few. No play in churchyards, cemeteries or private gardens, and the ball may not be carried in a motor vehicle. Shops board up their windows.83,85 The ball is hand-sewn leather filled with cork so that it floats in the brook, and it is painted to a design chosen by whoever turns it up. The scorer keeps it.83

Nobody knows how old the game is, because the committee's archive was destroyed in a fire. The University of Derby points to possible references from the late 17th century onwards.84 It has been "Royal" since the Prince of Wales, later Edward VIII, turned up the ball in 1928.83,84

In the stands

The day begins with a lunch at the Green Man & Black's Head Royal Hotel, where a Shrovetide song written in 1891 is sung, before the procession to Shaw Croft.83

Ball games · Kirkwall, Orkney, Scotland

The Kirkwall Ba'

Uppies against Doonies

Dec · Jan

When

Christmas Day and New Year's Day. The boys' game is played in the morning, and the men's ba' is thrown up at 1pm.88,86

Where

The Mercat Cross on Broad Street, in front of St Magnus Cathedral, and then wherever the scrum goes.88,87

Getting in

It is a street game: no tickets, and spectators are advised to keep their distance.88

When the cathedral clock strikes one, the ba' goes up at the Mercat Cross and hundreds of men close round it. The Doonies win by getting it into the harbour. The Uppies have to touch it against a wall at the south end of town.86,87 Your side is set by where you were born, north or south of the cross, and by family; sons usually follow their fathers.86,87

There is no time limit and no limit on numbers. NorthLink records the longest men's game at seven hours, on New Year's Day 1975, and the shortest at four minutes, on Christmas Day 1952.86 The ball is hand-stitched leather filled with cork dust. NorthLink dates the first records to 1650, though that has not been checked against a primary document.86

In the stands

Shops and houses along the likely routes are boarded up with timber.87 On a cold day you can see steam rising off the scrum as the light goes.88 The winning side picks one man to keep the ba', and he is hoisted up with it.87

Races & gatherings · Braemar, Aberdeenshire, Scotland

The Braemar Gathering

Highland games

Sep

When

The first Saturday of September.89

Where

The Princess Royal and Duke of Fife Memorial Park, Braemar.89

Getting in

Seated tickets go on sale on 1 February (as of 2026). General admission is standing room.92

The Braemar Highland Society has run the gathering in its present form since 1832. Queen Victoria came in 1848, and the reigning monarch has been a regular visitor ever since.89 VisitBritain says the queen gave the society its royal title in 1866.90 Claims that the games go back to King Malcolm Canmore, more than 900 years ago, are legend.89,91

The heavy events are the core: tossing the caber, throwing the hammer and putting the stone. Geoff Capes set Braemar's stone-putting record in 1981.90 Around them run track and hill races, tug of war, piping and Highland dancing.90,91

In the stands

Massed pipe bands march into the arena in the late morning and again in the afternoon.89 The gathering calls itself the most prominent and best-attended Highland games in the world, which is its own description.89
