A literary atlas · fifteen cities, fifteen walks

Novels That Got the City Right

Fifteen cities, each paired with the novel most often named as its truest portrait: who makes that claim, the real streets and buildings in the book, what survives of them, and a one-day walk through each.

Every city has a novel that its readers hand to visitors, and in a few places the book has changed the city back. Dublin holds a festival every June for a day that happened only on paper. A Prague pub has lived for decades on a scene in which a soldier is arrested. Istanbul has a museum built for a woman who never existed.

This is a guide to fifteen of those pairings. For each city it names the novel most often put forward, says who puts it forward and how strong the claim is, and walks through the real places the book uses: the bridge on the first page, the café where a character orders coffee, the house that was pulled down. Every stop is marked by what has happened to it since. Some are still trading. Some survive under another name or use. Some are gone, and some were never there at all, invented by the novelist and pinned to a real street.

The walks are designed to fill a day without hurrying. Most are between two and seven kilometres; a few need a train at one end. Opening hours, ticketing and the fate of old buildings change, so anything that can change is marked as of 2026. Nothing here quotes the novels. The point is to read them, then go.

What survives of the 91 stops

Each bar is one city's walk, coloured by what has become of each stop. Across all fifteen: 54 survive, 16 have changed, 3 are gone, 5 are invented and 13 belong to the author's own story.

  • Changed Still there, altered or put to another use

01 Dublin · Ireland

Ulysses

James Joyce · 1922

The claim: Strong

Who says so

Hardly anyone argues. In 1918 Joyce told his friend Frank Budgen he wanted to describe Dublin so completely that the city could be rebuilt from the book if it disappeared, and the Irish government still tells the story.1 Dublin has marked the novel's day since 1954, and a week-long Bloomsday Festival, now run by the James Joyce Centre, has been held since 1994.2

How the book uses the city

The whole book happens on one day, 16 June 1904, from about eight in the morning into the small hours.32 Leopold Bloom crosses the city from his house at 7 Eccles Street and back, and his path keeps nearly meeting the young Stephen Dedalus's. Joyce built the day out of real shops, pubs, offices and institutions, many of which still trade.41

First published in Paris on 2 February 1922 by Sylvia Beach's Shakespeare and Company, after partial serialisation in The Little Review from 1918.34

The walk

About 3.0 km on foot. Plus: DART train from Sandycove & Glasthule into town, about 13 km. Distances follow an OpenStreetMap foot route between stops.

  1. 1

    James Joyce Tower, Sandycove Survives

    The setting of the opening episode. Joyce stayed here for a few nights in September 1904; it has been a museum since 16 June 1962.56

  2. DART train from Sandycove & Glasthule into town, about 13 km

    2

    Sweny's, Lincoln Place Changed

    Bloom buys a bar of lemon soap here. It no longer dispenses medicine; it is now a bookshop and venue for readings that still sells the soap.7

  3. 3

    National Library of Ireland, Kildare Street Survives

    Episode nine: Stephen lays out his theory of Hamlet to the librarians in the Reading Room, which the library opens to the public on Bloomsday.89 Built 1885–90 by Thomas Newenham Deane and son.10

  4. 4

    Davy Byrne's, Duke Street Survives

    Bloom's lunch of a gorgonzola sandwich and a glass of burgundy. Still a working pub.11

  5. 5

    James Joyce Centre, North Great George's Street Author's

    Not in the book, but it keeps the original front door of 7 Eccles Street in its courtyard.12

  6. 6

    7 Eccles Street (site) Gone

    The Blooms' home. The house was demolished and the Mater Private Hospital stands on the site; the door was rescued in 1967.213 Sources disagree on when the house itself came down.1213

02 London · England

Mrs Dalloway

Virginia Woolf · 1925

The claim: Contested

Who says so

London has no single agreed novel, and Dickens turns up on every list next to Woolf.14 Mrs Dalloway's case rests on the walk itself: the Royal Society of Literature now holds an annual Dalloway Day in June, modelled on Bloomsday,15 and 82 critics polled by BBC Culture in 2015 ranked it the third-greatest British novel.16

How the book uses the city

One Wednesday in June 1923. Clarissa Dalloway, a political hostess living in Westminster, walks up through St James's Park and Piccadilly to Bond Street to buy flowers for her party that night.1718 The same streets carry Septimus Warren Smith, a shell-shocked veteran, and his wife Rezia, and Big Ben's hours keep time for everyone.1718 Scholars still argue about which Wednesday: 13 June and 20 June both have defenders.19

Published 14 May 1925 by the Hogarth Press, the Woolfs' own imprint.17

The walk

About 4.6 km on foot. Distances follow an OpenStreetMap foot route between stops.

  1. 1

    Dean's Yard, Westminster Survives

    Clarissa's starting point. Woolf never gives her house an exact address, but it sits near here.18

  2. 2

    St James's Park Survives

    She crosses Victoria Street and walks through the park.18

  3. 3

    Hatchards, Piccadilly Survives

    Clarissa stops at the bookshop window. Hatchards, founded in 1797, still trades here.1820 Devonshire House, whose parties she remembers, was pulled down in 1924, the year after the novel is set.18

  4. 4

    Bond Street Survives

    Her shopping destination, where the crowd, Septimus and Rezia among it, stares at a mysterious official car.18

  5. 5

    Harley Street Survives

    The Warren Smiths come here to consult an eminent doctor.18

  6. 6

    Regent's Park Survives

    Septimus and Rezia sit and walk here.18

03 Edinburgh · Scotland

The Prime of Miss Jean Brodie

Muriel Spark · 1961

The claim: Contested

Who says so

Trainspotting, Hogg's Confessions of a Justified Sinner and Scott's The Heart of Midlothian all have their champions. Spark's claim is civic: the city named a path across Bruntsfield Links after her in 2018,21 the Vennel steps off the Grassmarket were renamed the Miss Jean Brodie Steps the same year,22 and Ian Rankin has read the novel as a portrait of Edinburgh's split between Old Town and New.23

How the book uses the city

A charismatic teacher and her chosen girls, the Brodie set, at the fictional Marcia Blaine School in the 1930s.2425 The school is modelled on James Gillespie's, where Spark was a pupil, and Miss Brodie partly on her teacher Christina Kay.2526 The set piece is a walk Miss Brodie leads through the Old Town, where the girls see the poverty behind their genteel south side.2423

First printed in The New Yorker, then published as a book by Macmillan in London in 1961.2725

The walk

About 3.0 km on foot. Distances follow an OpenStreetMap foot route between stops.

  1. 1

    Canongate Survives

    On the route of Miss Brodie's Old Town walk.28

  2. 2

    Lawnmarket Survives

    Also on the walk.28 Miss Brodie claims descent from Deacon Brodie, the real 18th-century cabinetmaker and burglar; the pub named after him here is not in the book.23

  3. 3

    Grassmarket and the Vennel Survives

    On the walk.28 The steps at the west end were renamed the Miss Jean Brodie Steps in June 2018, after a scene in the 1969 film.22

  4. 4

    Lauriston Place Survives

    Where the walking party passes a queue of unemployed men.28

  5. 5

    Bruntsfield Links Survives

    The links near the school. The path Spark took every day was named Muriel Spark Walk for her centenary in 2018.2128

  6. 6

    Former James Gillespie's, Warrender Park Crescent Changed

    The model for Marcia Blaine. Gillespie's girls used the building until 1966; it is now University of Edinburgh student housing, with no public access.2930

04 Lisbon · Portugal

The Year of the Death of Ricardo Reis

José Saramago · 1984 O Ano da Morte de Ricardo Reis

The claim: Fair

Who says so

The novelist David Leavitt calls it probably the best-known Lisbon novel and has retraced its walks.31 Antonio Tabucchi's Pereira Maintains, also set in 1930s Lisbon, is the main rival, but it was written in Italian. A full walking route through the book was drawn up by the researcher Fabrizio Boscaglia in 2021.32

How the book uses the city

Ricardo Reis is one of the invented poets Fernando Pessoa wrote as. In Saramago's book he sails home from Brazil at the end of December 1935, just after Pessoa's death, and spends 1936 in a hotel and then a flat, reading newspapers, walking and being visited by Pessoa's ghost.3132 Salazar's regime, the Spanish war next door and the poverty of the city come in at street level.3233

Published in 1984 by Editorial Caminho; translated by Giovanni Pontiero (Harcourt 1991, Harvill 1992).34

The walk

About 5.1 km on foot. Distances follow an OpenStreetMap foot route between stops.

  1. 1

    Casa dos Bicos Author's

    Not in the novel. The José Saramago Foundation is based here, and his ashes lie under an olive tree from his home village outside, placed on 18 June 2011.3536

  2. 2

    Praça do Comércio Survives

    Reis changes money here and watches the river from the arcades; he and Pessoa set off up Rua Augusta in the rain.32

  3. 3

    Rua do Alecrim (Hotel Bragança) Changed

    Reis takes a room with a river view, and Pessoa first visits him here.32 A real Hotel Bragança operated on the street from about 1924; the building reopened as another hotel in 2010.37

  4. 4

    Eça de Queirós statue, Largo do Barão de Quintela Changed

    Reis stops in front of it. The original was vandalised; a replica has stood since 2001.32

  5. 5

    Praça Luís de Camões Survives

    Reis takes a temporary consulting room on the square.32

  6. 6

    Café A Brasileira, Rua Garrett Survives

    He rests over coffee and overhears literary gossip. The bronze Pessoa outside is a later addition.32

  7. 7

    Miradouro de Santa Catarina Survives

    Reis rents a flat overlooking the Tagus here and keeps returning to the Adamastor statue.32

  8. 8

    Cemitério dos Prazeres Survives

    Pessoa's burial place, which the book returns to.32 His remains were later moved to the Jerónimos Monastery, where his monument dates from 1985.3839

05 Barcelona · Spain

In Diamond Square

Mercè Rodoreda · 1962 La plaça del Diamant

The claim: Strong

Who says so

Time Out Barcelona's readers put it first in a 2013 vote for the ten novels of the city.40 Enciclopèdia Catalana calls it one of the most universal novels in Catalan,41 and both the Rodoreda foundation and the city council publish walking routes that start in its square.4243

How the book uses the city

Natàlia, whom her husband calls Colometa, tells her own life in Gràcia, from a dance in Plaça del Diamant during the neighbourhood festival through the Republic, the Civil War and the years after.4140 The city is seen from the pavement and the kitchen: the square, the flat on Carrer del Montseny, the market, Carrer Gran de Gràcia.4240 History arrives as hunger, absence and work.

Written in exile in Geneva in 1960 and published in Barcelona in 1962.44 Peter Bush's In Diamond Square (Virago, 2013) is the third English version; the second, David Rosenthal's, was called The Time of the Doves.4445

The walk

About 3.6 km on foot. Distances follow an OpenStreetMap foot route between stops.

  1. 1

    Rambla de les Flors Survives

    The flower-stall stretch of La Rambla, near the Boqueria, where one of the book's scenes is set.42 The number of stalls has dwindled.

  2. 2

    Jardinets de Gràcia Survives

    Characters walk in these gardens, as Rodoreda did.42

  3. 3

    Former Cafè Monumental, Carrer Gran de Gràcia 25 Gone

    Where the characters meet for vermouth. The café has gone.4246

  4. 4

    Mercat de la Llibertat Survives

    The Rodoreda foundation says the novel's market is probably this one.42

  5. 5

    Carrer del Montseny Survives

    Colometa and Quimet's home after they marry.42 Time Out places their flat at number 9; the foundation names only the street.40

  6. 6

    Plaça del Diamant Changed

    The opening dance. The square was relaid in the mid-1990s and the festival marquee no longer goes up here.47 A metal Colometa by Xavier Medina-Campeny stands in one corner.46

  7. 7

    Refugi 232 Author's

    A Civil War air-raid shelter under the square, not in the novel, open for guided visits on Sunday mornings (as of 2026; book ahead).4248

06 Naples · Italy

My Brilliant Friend

Elena Ferrante · 2011 L'amica geniale

The claim: Strong

Who says so

The city council itself publishes an itinerary of the book's places,49 and in 2024 the New York Times Book Review's survey of writers and critics put it first among the best books of the 21st century.50 Older candidates exist, such as Anna Maria Ortese's Il mare non bagna Napoli, but none has drawn readers into the streets like this.51

How the book uses the city

Lenù and Lila grow up in the 1950s in a poor neighbourhood on the eastern edge of the city. The book never names it, but it is identified with Rione Luzzatti.5253 The rione is a closed world; the centre and the sea are somewhere else, reached through a tunnel and down the Rettifilo, and every trip into town exposes the gap in class.5254 Later volumes move out to the university streets and Chiaia.52

The first of four Neapolitan novels, published in Italian in 2011;53 Ann Goldstein's translation came out from Europa Editions in 2012.55

The walk

About 6.8 km on foot. Distances follow an OpenStreetMap foot route between stops.

  1. 1

    Rione Luzzatti, Via Beato Leonardo Murialdo Survives

    The girls' neighbourhood, built as public housing between the wars.53 A mural of Lila and Lenù by Eduardo Castaldo faces the local library, unveiled in January 2019.56 Local guides run tours of the courtyards.52

  2. 2

    Corso Umberto I (the Rettifilo) Survives

    The road into the city. The girls first venture out along it, and Lila's wedding dress comes from its bridal shops.5149

  3. 3

    Via Mezzocannone Survives

    Lenù's bookshop job, in the later volumes.5152

  4. 4

    Port'Alba Survives

    The book-stall arch that stands for Lenù's studies.5249

  5. 5

    Galleria Umberto I Survives

    Part of the elegant centre where the girls' world collides with money.54

  6. 6

    Gran Caffè Gambrinus, Piazza del Plebiscito Survives

    The café they could only look at from outside; on the piazza the Solara brothers drive them off.5254

  7. 7

    Piazza dei Martiri Invented

    The Solaras' shoe shop, which Lila runs in a later volume. The square is real; the shop is not. Sources differ on who opens it.5249

07 Prague · Czech Republic

The Good Soldier Švejk

Jaroslav Hašek · 1921–23 Osudy dobrého vojáka Švejka za světové války

The claim: Fair

Who says so

Kafka and Meyrink's Golem make Prague's mystical case; Švejk's is popularity and addresses. The Library of Congress calls it the most translated Czech novel, in about 60 languages,57 and Britannica has an entry for a Prague pub only because the book made it famous.58

How the book uses the city

It opens in June 1914, on the news from Sarajevo, with Švejk, a seller of dogs, arrested at his local for talking about it.59 Before he ever reaches the front the first part sends him through real Prague institutions in turn: police headquarters, the criminal court, an asylum, a district police station and the garrison prison up at Hradčany.60616263

Published in parts from 1921; Hašek died in 1923 while writing the fourth, which Karel Vaněk finished. Cecil Parrott's 1973 English translation is the standard one.57

The walk

About 4.7 km on foot. Distances follow an OpenStreetMap foot route between stops.

  1. 1

    U Kalicha, Na Bojišti Survives

    The opening arrest, and the 'six o'clock after the war' appointment. The pub's fame is owed entirely to the book: no biographer records Hašek as a regular.59 In private family hands again since the early 1990s.5964

  2. 2

    Kateřinky asylum, Kateřinská Survives

    The novel's asylum is unnamed, but the specialist Švejk encyclopedia concludes it is almost certainly this one, where Hašek himself spent part of February 1911.62

  3. 3

    Salmovská police station (site) Gone

    A whole chapter after Švejk is thrown out of the asylum. The station moved in 1926 and the building that stands there now dates from 1928.62

  4. 4

    Former criminal court, Karlovo náměstí Changed

    Švejk is examined by a kindly judge who orders a psychiatric commission. It is now the City Court of Prague.61

  5. 5

    U Fleků, Křemencova Survives

    Turns up in Švejk's endless anecdotes rather than as a scene; Hašek drank here.65

  6. 6

    Police headquarters, Bartolomějská Survives

    Švejk spends a night here and is brought back later. Still police premises as of 2018; exterior only.60

  7. 7

    Garrison prison, Hradčany Survives

    In the prison chapel the field chaplain Katz takes Švejk on as his batman. The building is still in use and closed to the public.63

08 Berlin · Germany

Berlin Alexanderplatz

Alfred Döblin · 1929 Berlin Alexanderplatz. Die Geschichte vom Franz Biberkopf

The claim: Strong

Who says so

The German Historical Museum calls it the most important German big-city novel,66 and its American publisher, the great novel of Berlin and the doomed Weimar Republic.67 Michael Bienert's guidebook Döblins Berlin maps its locations street by street.68

How the book uses the city

Franz Biberkopf comes out of Tegel prison in 1927, takes the tram into town and gets off at Rosenthaler Platz, and then tries to go straight in the working-class streets around Alexanderplatz.6669 Döblin, a doctor who practised among Berlin's poor, builds the city out of montage: adverts, tram routes, news items, slang.6670 Alexanderplatz itself is a building site, dug up for the U-Bahn.7172

Published by S. Fischer in 1929.66 Eugene Jolas translated it in 1931 and Michael Hofmann again in 2018.6773

The walk

About 4.8 km on foot. Plus: U6 from Alt-Tegel, change for the U8 to Rosenthaler Platz. Distances follow an OpenStreetMap foot route between stops.

  1. 1

    Tegel prison, Seidelstraße Survives

    Biberkopf's release opens the novel. Still a working prison, opened in 1898; exterior only.7274

  2. U6 from Alt-Tegel, change for the U8 to Rosenthaler Platz

    2

    Rosenthaler Platz Changed

    Where he gets off the tram, overwhelmed; a chapter is named after the square.6869 The tram line is long gone and much of the square has been rebuilt.69

  3. 3

    Münzstraße Survives

    Biberkopf goes to the cinema here.68 The street survives; the cinema has not been traced.

  4. 4

    Alexanderplatz Changed

    The hub of the book, permanently under construction.7172 Rebuilt several times since; Bienert finds nothing left there that recalls Döblin.68 The police headquarters that stood beside it was demolished, and the Alexa mall has occupied the site since 2007.75

  5. 5

    Former central slaughterhouse, Eldenaer Straße Changed

    The novel gives the slaughterhouse a whole chapter.6872 Opened in 1881 and closed in 1991; some brick halls survive among new housing.76

09 St Petersburg · Russia

Crime and Punishment

Fyodor Dostoevsky · 1866 Prestupleniye i nakazaniye

The claim: Strong

Who says so

Cambridge University Library built its 150th-anniversary exhibition partly around the book's St Petersburg,77 and Donald Rayfield, who retraced Raskolnikov's routes, describes the city as central to it.78 The city's own tourism portal lists the houses as sights.7980

How the book uses the city

A stifling summer in the crowded, poor district around the Haymarket and a bend of the Ekaterininsky Canal, now the Griboedov Canal.7778 Raskolnikov, a destitute former student, kills an old pawnbroker to test a theory.81 The novel counts the steps between buildings, and Dostoevsky wrote it while living a few hundred metres from the sites he used.8283

Serialised in The Russian Messenger in 1866 and published as a book in 1867; the first English translation, by Frederick Whishaw, appeared in 1886.84

The walk

About 2.1 km on foot. Distances follow an OpenStreetMap foot route between stops.

  1. 1

    Sennaya Square (the Haymarket) Changed

    Raskolnikov's constant haunt, then a district of stalls, taverns and brothels.85 Heavily rebuilt since.

  2. 2

    Kaznacheyskaya Street 7 Author's

    Dostoevsky's own address while he wrote the novel.83

  3. 3

    'Raskolnikov's house', Grazhdanskaya Street 19 Changed

    Researchers identify it as the house with Raskolnikov's attic. Built in 1831; a 1970 repair took out the basement and a storey. A relief on the corner dates from 1999. Residential: street view only.79

  4. 4

    Kokushkin Bridge Survives

    The bridge the novel's first page calls only K—— Bridge.7785

  5. 5

    'Sonya's house', Griboedov Canal 73 Changed

    Sonya Marmeladova's lodging, by researchers' identification; a rival theory exists. Taller and a different colour now.8285

  6. 6

    'The pawnbroker's house', Srednyaya Podyacheskaya 15 Survives

    Scene of the murders. The novel puts it 730 steps from Raskolnikov's door; walkers usually count more.8278 The identification is still debated.80

10 Istanbul · Turkey

The Museum of Innocence

Orhan Pamuk · 2008 Masumiyet Müzesi

The claim: Strong

Who says so

Pico Iyer argued in the New York Review of Books that the city, more than the lovers, is the book's real subject.86 Pamuk's The Black Book is the other contender. This one has something no rival has: Pamuk planned the novel and a real museum together, and the museum was named European Museum of the Year in 2014.87

How the book uses the city

Kemal, son of a rich Nişantaşı family, is engaged to Sibel and falls for Füsun, a shop assistant and distant cousin. He spends years visiting her family's house and keeping her things.8889 The story runs from the mid-1970s to the early 2000s, and the city's class lines are drawn in streets: Nişantaşı and Teşvikiye for money, the Hilton for society, the steep lanes of Çukurcuma for the old city.878690

Published in Turkish in 2008; Maureen Freely's English translation followed in 2009.8886

The walk

About 3.5 km on foot. Distances follow an OpenStreetMap foot route between stops.

  1. 1

    Teşvikiye Mosque Survives

    On Kemal's private map of streets to avoid; he watched Füsun from a corner of its courtyard.90

  2. 2

    Teşvikiye and Valikonağı junction Invented

    Near here stood the Şanzelize Boutique, where Füsun works: the first box in the museum. The shop is invented.8990

  3. 3

    Hilton, Harbiye Survives

    Kemal and Sibel's engagement party.86 The hotel opened in 1955 and still operates.91

  4. 4

    The Museum of Innocence, Çukurcuma Invented

    A real building standing in for Füsun's fictional family home. Opened 27 April 2012, with 83 cases for the 83 chapters and 4,213 cigarette ends on the entrance wall.87 The novel prints a ticket readers can bring for entry; check current hours and terms (as of 2026).88

11 Cairo · Egypt

Palace Walk (the Cairo Trilogy)

Naguib Mahfouz · 1956–57 Bayn al-Qasrayn

The claim: Strong

Who says so

Each volume takes its title from a real street in al-Gamaliya, the old quarter where Mahfouz was born.92 Newsweek compared his Cairo to Dickens's London,93 and the scholar Rasheed El Enany calls the trilogy the first saga novel in Arabic.92 Midaq Alley (1947) is the other Mahfouz book often named.

How the book uses the city

Three generations of al-Sayyid Ahmad Abd al-Jawad's family, from 1917 into the 1940s.9493 The family house on Bayn al-Qasrayn, in medieval Cairo, is the centre, and the mother, Amina, watches the street through a carved wooden lattice.95 The British occupation and the 1919 revolution reach the family through the same streets and mosques.9295

The trilogy appeared in 1956–57 as Bayn al-Qasrayn, Qasr al-Shawq and al-Sukkariyya;94 Mahfouz wrote it as one book, and the publisher split it in three.92 Mahfouz won the Nobel Prize in 1988.96

The walk

About 1.9 km on foot. Distances follow an OpenStreetMap foot route between stops.

  1. 1

    Bab al-Futuh Survives

    The northern Fatimid gate; Literary Traveler places a Palace Walk scene here in which Ahmad is made to dig for British soldiers.95

  2. 2

    Bayt al-Suhaymi Author's

    Not a scene in the book, but an Ottoman house museum that shows the enclosed world Amina lives in.95

  3. 3

    Darb Qirmiz Author's

    The lane, off Bayt al-Qadi square, where Mahfouz is said to have been born; accounts of the exact house, and of how long he lived in the quarter, differ.97

  4. 4

    Bayn al-Qasrayn, al-Muizz Street Survives

    'Between the two palaces', once the plaza between two Fatimid palaces.98 The family house is invented; the Mamluk monuments around it stand.9598

  5. 5

    Al-Hussein Mosque Survives

    Amina's single, consequential visit to the shrine.95 An active mosque.

  6. 6

    El Fishawy café, Khan el-Khalili Author's

    Mahfouz's favourite café, where he met other writers.95

  7. 7

    Naguib Mahfouz Museum Author's

    In the Sufi lodge beside the Abu al-Dhahab Mosque; opened on 14 July 2019 with his library and belongings.99100

12 Tokyo · Japan

Norwegian Wood

Haruki Murakami · 1987 Noruwei no Mori

The claim: Contested

Who says so

No poll names it the Tokyo novel, and the Guardian's list of the best books set in the city picked Murakami's After Dark instead.101 Its claim is that it uses more real, walkable Tokyo than any of his other books: Waseda University runs features walking readers through its settings,102 and its long walk along the old moat has become a pilgrimage route.103

How the book uses the city

Toru Watanabe is a student in late-1960s Tokyo, living in a private men's dormitory; Waseda says both are modelled on itself and on the Wakeijuku hall where Murakami lived.104102 Much of the Tokyo half of the book happens on foot or by streetcar: walks with Naoko beside the outer moat, the one-car tram to Midori's neighbourhood, drinking in Shinjuku.102103 DUG, the Shinjuku jazz bar long linked with the book, closed on 27 June 2026.105

Published by Kodansha in 1987; Jay Rubin's English translation appeared in 2000.106

The walk

About 5.9 km on foot. Plus: Tozai Line from Waseda to Iidabashi, then JR to Yotsuya. Distances follow an OpenStreetMap foot route between stops.

  1. 1

    Wakeijuku dormitory, Mejirodai Survives

    The model for Toru's dorm; Murakami lived here for about six months from April 1968. Still a student residence; view from outside.104102

  2. 2

    Waseda tram stop, Toden Arakawa Line Survives

    Toru rides the streetcar to Midori's home. Tokyo's last surviving Toden line.102

  3. 3

    Haruki Murakami Library (Waseda Building 4) Author's

    Not in the novel. Opened 1 October 2021 in a building remodelled by Kengo Kuma.107 Check entry arrangements (as of 2026).

  4. 4

    Waseda Building 2 (former library) Changed

    The library where Toru spends much of his time; it now houses the Aizu Museum.102

  5. Tozai Line from Waseda to Iidabashi, then JR to Yotsuya

    5

    Yotsuya and Sotobori Park Survives

    Toru and Naoko meet by chance on a train, get off at Yotsuya and start walking along the moat embankment.103108

  6. 6

    Iidabashi Survives

    The walk continues past here along the moat.102108

  7. 7

    Ochanomizu Survives

    And on along the Kanda River. The book's walk carries on to Komagome, a full day's distance.103108

13 New York · United States

A Tree Grows in Brooklyn

Betty Smith · 1943

The claim: Fair

Who says so

New York has too many novels for one to win outright; Wharton, Fitzgerald and Baldwin all have cases. In 2017 the city put this one on the five-book shortlist for its first One Book One New York read,109110 and in 2018 its branch library became an American Library Association Literary Landmark.111

How the book uses the city

Francie Nolan grows up poor in Williamsburg in the early 1900s, first on Bogart Street and later in flats near Lorimer and Grand.112113 The book maps her world block by block: the pushcart market, the El, the church and above all the branch library where she reads her way along the shelves. Smith grew up in the same streets at the same time.113114

Published in 1943.112

The walk

About 2.4 km on foot. Distances follow an OpenStreetMap foot route between stops.

  1. 1

    Bogart Street, Bushwick Survives

    The Nolans' first home. No particular building is identified.113

  2. 2

    Most Holy Trinity, Montrose Avenue Survives

    The parish says the novel never names its church but plainly describes this one, where Smith herself was baptised in 1897.115 Built 1882–85.116

  3. 3

    Graham Avenue at Grand Street Changed

    Francie's pushcart market. The street still trades; the carts are gone.113

  4. 4

    Lorimer and Grand Survives

    The streets of the family's later flats.113

  5. 5

    Leonard Library, Devoe Street Survives

    Francie's library, and Smith's. Opened on 1 December 1908 as a Carnegie branch;117 a Literary Landmark since 2018.111 Reopened in August 2025 after renovation.118

14 San Francisco · United States

The Maltese Falcon

Dashiell Hammett · 1930

The claim: Fair

Who says so

Armistead Maupin's Tales of the City is the obvious rival, and beat it in one 2016 ranking.119 Hammett's claim is in brass: two American Library Association Literary Landmarks, at John's Grill and Hammett's old flat,120121 and a plaque on Burritt Street marking a death that never happened.122

How the book uses the city

Sam Spade, a private detective with a downtown office, is pulled into a hunt for a jewelled bird after a client lies to him and his partner is shot.123 Almost everything happens within a few blocks of Union Square: hotels, the Geary Theatre, office buildings, alleys off Bush Street and a grill on Ellis Street. Hammett, a former Pinkerton detective, wrote it a few blocks away.124125

Serialised in Black Mask in 1929 and published as a book by Knopf in 1930.123126

The walk

About 2.6 km on foot. Distances follow an OpenStreetMap foot route between stops.

  1. 1

    891 Post Street Author's

    Hammett's home from 1926 to 1929, where he wrote the novel; Spade's flat is modelled on his. Plaque since 2005; private building.121124

  2. 2

    Geary Theatre, Geary Street Changed

    Joel Cairo has tickets for a play here.127 Still a theatre, now renamed the Toni Rembe Theater.

  3. 3

    John's Grill, Ellis Street Survives

    Spade eats a quick meal here; the Mercury News calls it the only restaurant the book names. Open since 1908 and full of Hammett memorabilia.120125128

  4. 4

    Palace Hotel, New Montgomery Street Survives

    Spade has lunch here, by the Mercury News's account of the book's sites.125 Still a hotel.

  5. 5

    Burritt Street Invented

    The plaque at the alley mouth marks the spot where Spade's partner, Miles Archer, is killed. Spoiler warning: the plaque itself names the killer.129122

  6. 6

    Dashiell Hammett Street Author's

    Formerly Monroe Street, renamed for him in 1988.130

15 Los Angeles · United States

The Big Sleep

Raymond Chandler · 1939

The claim: Fair

Who says so

Chandler wrote several LA novels, and some readers prefer The Long Goodbye. The Big Sleep, Marlowe's first novel, is the one on TIME's All-TIME 100 list and the Guardian's 100 best novels,131132 and in 1994 the city named the corner of Hollywood and Cahuenga Raymond Chandler Square.133

How the book uses the city

Philip Marlowe is hired by a dying oil millionaire over a blackmail matter involving his younger daughter.132 The case runs through 1930s Hollywood, the Hills, the rich West Side and the beach towns, mixing real streets like Hollywood Boulevard and Franklin Avenue with invented ones.134135

Published by Knopf in spring 1939, built from two of Chandler's earlier stories; the first edition sold barely 13,000 copies.132136

The walk

About 0.7 km on foot. Plus: Walk to Hollywood/Western and take the Metro B Line to Wilshire/Vermont. Distances follow an OpenStreetMap foot route between stops.

  1. 1

    Hollywood Boulevard at Las Palmas Invented

    Roughly where readers have placed Geiger's bookshop, which Marlowe watches. The shop is invented.135

  2. 2

    Musso & Frank Grill Author's

    Not in this book, but Chandler drank in its back room and Marlowe visits in The Long Goodbye.137

  3. 3

    Raymond Chandler Square, Hollywood and Cahuenga Author's

    Named in 1994. Tradition puts Marlowe's office in the 1921 Security Trust building on the corner, though it is a later novel that names his 'Cahuenga Building'.133138139

  4. Walk to Hollywood/Western and take the Metro B Line to Wilshire/Vermont

    4

    Bullocks Wilshire, Wilshire Boulevard Changed

    Marlowe meets Agnes by the car-park entrance under the green tower.140 The store opened in 1929 and closed in 1993; it is now Southwestern Law School, with occasional public tours.141142

How this was put together

The pairings come from published evidence where it exists: readers' polls, city-run itineraries, festivals, literary landmarks, and critics who have argued the case. Where the evidence is thin or a rival has as good a claim, the entry says so in its claim label: Strong where the pairing is close to undisputed, Fair where it is one of two or three serious candidates, Contested where the city has no consensus. Places are included only when a source ties them to the novel, and identifications made by researchers rather than the author are labelled. Coordinates are from OpenStreetMap, and walking distances follow its foot routes. Wikipedia is used only as secondary support and is marked as such in the sources.
