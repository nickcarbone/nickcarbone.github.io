Election logistics · Explainer with tools

Chain of Custody

How a vote becomes a certified result, stage by stage: the American system that goes to the polls on 3 November, ten other democracies, and the times the chain broke.

By Nick Carbone · 9 stages · 11 countries · 16 incident reports

On 3 November, Americans will elect all 435 members of the House of Representatives, 35 senators (33 in the regular cycle plus special elections in Ohio and Florida) and 36 governors28. The results that appear on television that night will be unofficial. The official ones take days or weeks, and they emerge from a supply chain that most voters never see: rolls compiled months earlier, ballots proofed line by line, sealed containers signed for by two people at a time, scanners tested before anyone votes on them, and a canvass that has to reconcile every ballot against every voter who signed in.

The scale of that operation is easy to underestimate. In the 2024 general election, the US Election Assistance Commission counted:

158.2mballots counted, 20241
95,815polling places1
772,433poll workers1
6,461reporting jurisdictions1

That last figure is the number of units that report to the federal survey; the Brennan Center puts the number of local election jurisdictions at about 10,0004. There is no national election authority. Each state writes its own rules, and counties or towns carry them out, which is why almost every sentence about how Americans vote needs the words "in most states".

Other democracies have made very different choices about the same problems. India sends polling teams on foot, by boat and by helicopter so that, as a rule, no voter has to travel more than 2 km54. Brazil has voted entirely on electronic machines since 200073. Germany uses no voting machines in federal elections and counts every ballot by hand in public65. Estonia now takes more than half its parliamentary votes over the internet109. Japanese voters write their candidate's name by hand83.

Below, the process is broken into nine stages. Pick a stage to see how it works in the US, how other countries handle it, and where it has failed. Pick a country to relabel the whole chain.

01

The nine seals

    Seal S-01

    Register the voters

    In the United States

    In most states, getting on the roll is the voter's job. Registration usually closes 15 to 30 days before an election, though Connecticut allows seven days, Colorado eight and New York ten5. The National Conference of State Legislatures lists 25 states plus Washington DC with same-day registration, though in a few it is available only during early voting or only in presidential races6, and about half the states have some form of automatic registration, typically through motor-vehicle offices127. North Dakota has no voter registration at all: voters show valid ID at the polls5.

    The roll for 2024 held 211.1 million active registrations, and 234.5 million once inactive records are included1. Inactive records are the residue of people who moved or died; the gap between the two numbers is the maintenance problem every county clerk inherits.

    Elsewhere

    • Germany builds the roll automatically from municipal residence registers. For February 2025 the cut-off was 12 January, and every voter was posted a polling card by 2 February66.
    • Japan also compiles the register from residence records; citizens 18 and over who have lived in a municipality for three months are included without applying83.
    • Brazil makes registration compulsory for literate citizens aged 18 to 70, and collects ten fingerprints, a photo and a signature74128.
    • Great Britain moved to individual registration in 2014, making each person responsible for applying129.
    Seal S-02

    Build and print the ballot

    In the United States

    Americans vote on long ballots: federal, state, county, city, school-board and judicial races, plus referendums. Every distinct combination of contests is a separate "ballot style". For November 2024, Maricopa County in Arizona said it was "creating, proofing, and printing 21,534 different ballot styles", with 265 contests across all of them, on its first two-page ballot since 20067.

    The EAC's guidance is to proof every style against the candidates' official filings, run each one through every scanner and accessible voting device, and test with decks of deliberately hand-marked ballots8. Paper itself became a risk in the 2022 cycle: the EAC warned that lead times for raw materials had grown "from days or weeks to months" and told offices to order envelopes at least four months ahead9.

    The first hard deadline falls long before Election Day. Federal law requires states to send ballots to military and overseas voters who have asked for them at least 45 days before a federal election10. In 2026 that was Saturday 19 September; Michigan told its clerks the date "requires strict compliance"11.

    Elsewhere

    • France prints a separate ballot paper for every candidate. Voters are advised to take several, to keep their choice secret, then choose in a compulsory booth and put a single paper in an envelope67.
    • Japan prints no candidate names on national ballots: voters write the name, or a party name for the proportional vote83. The paper is a polypropylene film that unfolds itself in the box, which speeds up the count84.
    • India loads candidate symbols into its machines through Symbol Loading Units, which the Supreme Court ordered in 2024 to be sealed and stored for at least 45 days after results51.

    Where it breaks

    Palm Beach County's 2000 "butterfly ballot" (Florida 2000), Antrim County's late design changes (Michigan 2020) and a change of paper stock in Maricopa (Arizona 2022).

    Seal S-03

    Cast the vote

    In the United States

    In 2024, 52.8 million ballots were cast in person on Election Day, 48.9 million in person before it and 46.8 million by post1. (The EAC's own percentages, which leave out ballots of unknown type, put early in-person voting at 35.2% and Election Day at 37.4%2.) Eight states plus DC run their elections almost entirely by post: California, Colorado, Hawaii, Nevada, Oregon, Utah, Vermont and Washington15. Utah has since legislated to phase that out by 2029130.

    Most Americans mark a paper ballot that a scanner reads. Verified Voting projected that in 2024 about 69% of registered voters lived where hand-marked paper is the norm, 25% where everyone uses a ballot-marking device that prints a paper record, and 5% where direct-recording electronic machines are used12. The EAC found that over 98% of jurisdictions used paper ballots or an auditable paper record; the exceptions were 80 jurisdictions in three states2.

    Voting machines are generally kept off the internet. The federal standard adopted in 2021 denies certification to systems with wireless functions enabled13. Older tabulators have been fitted with modems to send unofficial results, and in 2019 researchers found more than 35 election systems that had been left online14.

    About three dozen states (36, by Ballotpedia's count) request or require identification at the polls22. Staffing is the persistent weak point: 47.9% of jurisdictions reported serious difficulty recruiting or keeping poll workers in 20242.

    Elsewhere

    • India ran about 1.05 million polling stations with roughly 5.5 million electronic machines in 20244950. In Gir forest a team of about ten set up a booth for one voter, a monk named Mahant Haridas Udaseen53.
    • Indonesia staffed 820,161 domestic polling stations with 5.7 million workers, seven per station, on one day in February 2024101. Voters mark their five ballots by punching a hole with a nail103.
    • Australia makes voting compulsory, with a A$20 penalty for unexplained non-voting89. In 2025, 56% of votes were cast before polling day87. The "democracy sausage" from polling-station barbecues was the Australian National Dictionary Centre's word of the year for 201690.
    • Great Britain required photo ID at a general election for the first time in 2024; the Electoral Commission estimates 0.08% of people who tried to vote in person, about 16,000, did not get a ballot because of it58.
    • Estonia counted 312,181 internet votes in 2023, more than the 301,620 cast on paper109.

    Where it breaks

    Queues at 10pm (Britain 2010), ballots running out (Berlin 2021, Houston 2022), hoax threats (2024) and exhaustion (Indonesia 2019).

    Seal S-04

    Seal, move and check

    In the United States

    Between the voter and the count, ballots pass through many hands, and the defence is paperwork. The EAC's chain-of-custody guidance says most materials need at least two witnesses at each transfer, often declared members of opposing parties; seal serial numbers are recorded at every handover, every broken seal is logged, drop-box collections are timed and documented, and the number of ballots is reconciled against the number of voters19. About two-thirds of states offered drop boxes in 20243.

    Postal ballots get a second gate: the envelope. Of almost 48 million returned in 2024, 584,463 (1.2%) were rejected, most often for a missing or mismatched signature1. NCSL lists 33 states plus DC with a process for voters to "cure" a signature problem16.

    Deadlines differ too. Fourteen states plus DC count postal ballots cast by Election Day that arrive afterwards. On 29 June 2026, in Watson v. Republican National Committee, the Supreme Court ruled 5–4 that federal law allows them to17.

    Elsewhere

    • India planned about 4 lakh (400,000) vehicles for its 2024 election; the Election Commission said 135 special trains carried security forces and 1,692 air sorties were flown50131. Officials took boats to Majuli, a river island in Assam, and trekked about 12 km to Nongriat in Meghalaya, a village of 139 voters55.
    • Australia's remote voting teams covered 162,000 km in 2025, visiting 354 localities87.
    • Germany counts postal ballots on boards of five to nine members, at least five present for the count65. 37% of German voters voted by post in 202563.

    Where it breaks

    1,370 Senate ballots that vanished between counts (Western Australia 2013) and postal votes opened too early (Austria 2016).

    Seal S-05

    Count

    In the United States

    Most ballots are read by optical scanners, either at the polling place or at a central count. The pace of the count depends heavily on when states let officials start opening and checking postal ballots. Pennsylvania's pre-canvass cannot begin until 7am on Election Day, and Wisconsin opens and tabulates postal ballots only on Election Day itself. Georgia may begin processing on the third Monday before the election, and Nevada scans from 15 days out18. States that start late report postal votes late, which is why the order in which results arrive says little about who will win.

    Elsewhere

    • Germany counts at each polling station in public, and anyone may watch65. About 675,000 volunteers staffed the 2025 election64.
    • France also counts by hand at the polling station, and voters can volunteer to help67.
    • Britain moves sealed boxes to constituency count centres, where counting usually runs through the night.
    • Japan reads the handwritten names with sorting machines; the market leader, Musashi, says its machines handle 660 ballots a minute85.
    • India held seven phases of polling between 19 April and 1 June 2024, then counted the votes on 4 June48.
    • Estonia decrypts internet votes on election night with a key held by members of the National Electoral Committee, after stripping voters' digital signatures from their votes110.
    Seal S-06

    Report unofficial results

    In the United States

    Election-night numbers are unofficial. They come from counties uploading tabulator results, are revised as postal and provisional ballots are processed, and become final only once the canvass is certified23. Reporting errors at this stage are usually caught later. Two recent examples: in Antrim County, Michigan, in 2020 and in the New York City mayoral primary of 2021, when the Board of Elections published a ranked-choice tally that included 135,000 test ballots3246.

    Elsewhere

    • Brazil's machines each print a results bulletin that is posted on the polling-place door, then send their results to the Superior Electoral Court (TSE), which starts publishing totals at 17:00 Brasília time75.
    • Mexico runs a preliminary results system and a statistical quick count; in 2024 the electoral institute announced the quick count at about midnight95.
    • Germany's Federal Returning Officer announces a provisional result on election night132.

    Where it breaks

    Results transmission was central to the court's ruling in Kenya 2017.

    Seal S-07

    Canvass and audit

    In the United States

    The canvass is the slow, dull part, and the part that matters: counties add in late and provisional ballots, check the number of ballots against the number of voters, and investigate any gap. Almost 93% of states reported running logic and accuracy tests on their equipment in 20243.

    As of April 2026, every state has a post-election audit in law; Alabama was the last, in April 202620. Seven states require risk-limiting audits, which hand-check a statistically chosen sample of paper ballots large enough to catch a wrong outcome: Colorado, Georgia, Maryland, Nevada, Rhode Island, Texas and Virginia2021.

    Elsewhere

    • India hand-counts the paper slips from five randomly chosen machines per assembly segment, a number the Supreme Court raised from one in 201952. In April 2024 it rejected petitions for checking every machine51.
    • Australia recounts every House ballot after election night and distributes preferences in full; 88 of 150 seats needed a full distribution in 20258891.
    • Mexico's district councils reopened and recounted 116,397 ballot packages, 68.16% of polling stations, in 2024100.

    When it held

    Georgia's full hand tally of about 5 million presidential ballots (2020).

    Seal S-08

    Recount and contest

    In the United States

    Ballotpedia counts 29 states plus DC with automatic recount rules, many triggered by a margin of 0.5%24; nine states changed their recount laws in 2025, so specific thresholds need checking. In November 2024 Pennsylvania's US Senate race went to an automatic statewide recount with a margin of about 28,000 votes out of more than 6.9 million25.

    Voters whose eligibility is in doubt cast provisional ballots, which are counted only once verified. The Help America Vote Act of 2002 made them a federal requirement and paid states to replace punch-card and lever machines after the Florida recount26.

    Elsewhere

    Courts in Austria, Kenya, Australia and Germany have all ordered elections re-run after logistical failures, usually without finding fraud. In most of these cases the test was whether the irregularities could have changed the result; Kenya's court held that failing to run the election lawfully was enough on its own.

    Where it was contested

    Florida 2000, Arizona 2022, Austria 2016, Kenya 2017, Brazil 2022.

    Seal S-09

    Certify

    In the United States

    Each state sets its own certification deadline, from a couple of days after the election to more than a month23. Certification is meant to be ministerial: officials confirm the count, they do not judge it. When the Cochise County supervisors in Arizona missed their deadline in 2022, a judge ordered them to certify the same day (see the report).

    For presidential elections, the Electoral Count Reform Act of 2022 names the governor (unless state law says otherwise) as the official who certifies a state's electors, makes the vice-president's role in the count "solely ministerial", and raises the bar for objections in Congress to one-fifth of each chamber27.

    Elsewhere

    • Germany: the Federal Electoral Committee fixed the final 2025 result on 14 March, 19 days after the vote63.
    • Mexico: the Electoral Tribunal declared the 2024 presidential election valid on 14 August, ten weeks after polling day99.
    • Indonesia: the 2024 national result was set on 20 March, 35 days after the vote101.
    • France: the Constitutional Council proclaims the presidential result; in 2022 it did so three days after the run-off133.
    • Australia: seats are declared only when the outcome is mathematically certain, and the 2025 writs were returned on 12 June8788.

    Where it breaks

    Cochise County 2022.

    03

    Incident reports

    Sixteen times the chain broke, bent or held under pressure. Each report separates what happened from what courts or official reviews established. Where someone alleged wrongdoing, the report says who and whether it was proven.

    Ballot design · RecountFlorida · 2000

    The butterfly ballot and the 537 votes

    George W. Bush carried Florida, and with it the presidency, by 537 votes out of almost six million29. Palm Beach County's two-column "butterfly" ballot confused voters; a peer-reviewed study later estimated it caused more than 2,000 Democratic voters to pick Pat Buchanan by mistake30. The recount of punch cards with partly detached "chads" was stopped by the Supreme Court.

    Established: In Bush v. Gore (12 December 2000) the Court held that recounting under standards that varied from county to county violated equal protection, and that no valid recount could finish in time31. Congress passed the Help America Vote Act in 200226. The butterfly-ballot figure is a scholarly estimate, not a court finding.

    Postal votesAustria · 2016

    Annulled, then delayed by glue

    Alexander Van der Bellen won the May 2016 presidential run-off by about 30,000 votes. On 1 July the Constitutional Court annulled it: in 14 districts covering about 77,000 votes, postal ballots had been opened early or handled by unauthorised people, and partial results had gone to media before polls closed114. The re-run set for 2 October was then postponed to 4 December because the glue on some postal envelopes was failing115116.

    Established: The court did not rule that any manipulation took place. It annuls when rules against abuse are broken and the votes affected could have changed the outcome, "without speculating whether the violations of the law have actually resulted in manipulation"114. Van der Bellen won the re-run.

    Custody · TransportWestern Australia · 2013

    The 1,370 missing Senate ballots

    During a recount of Western Australia's Senate seats, the electoral commission found that 1,370 ballot papers had disappeared: 1,139 from the Division of Pearce and 231 from Forrest92. An inquiry by a former federal police commissioner, Mick Keelty, concluded their fate was "not likely to be ever fully explained" and made 32 findings on ballot handling and transport92.

    Established: The commission itself asked the High Court to void the result. In February 2014 the court did so: at one critical point in the count the margin had been 14 votes, and 12 the other way in the recount, so the lost papers could have changed the outcome93. The state voted again on 5 April 201494.

    Supplies · QueuesBerlin · 2021

    Three elections, a referendum and a marathon

    On 26 September 2021 Berliners voted in federal, state and district elections and a referendum, on the day of the Berlin Marathon. Ballots ran out, road closures held up deliveries, some voters got papers for the wrong district, and about half the 2,256 polling stations were still taking votes after 18:00117120.

    Established: Berlin's constitutional court found "serious systemic flaws" and voided the state and district elections, which were re-run on 12 February 2023117. The Federal Constitutional Court ordered the federal vote repeated in 455 of the 2,256 districts, which happened on 11 February 2024118119.

    Results transmissionKenya · 2017

    A presidential result thrown out

    The electoral commission declared Uhuru Kenyatta the winner of the August 2017 presidential election with about 54%. Raila Odinga challenged it. On 1 September the Supreme Court declared the result "invalid, null and void" by four judges to two121122. It was widely reported as the first such ruling in sub-Saharan Africa123.

    Established: The court, led by Chief Justice David Maraga, found the commission "committed irregularities and illegalities in the transmission of results". The court did not find wrongdoing by Kenyatta, and the dissenting judges said rigging had not been proved121. Odinga boycotted the re-run on 26 October 2017, which Kenyatta won124.

    StaffingIndonesia · 2019

    The cost of one day

    Indonesia elected its president and its national and regional legislatures on a single day in April 2019: more than 190 million registered voters, five ballots each, all counted by hand at more than 800,000 polling stations125105.

    Established: The election commission's figure, reported by the state news agency ANTARA, is 894 polling-station workers dead and 5,175 ill; officials attributed the deaths to heavy workloads, fatigue and existing illnesses, and most of those who died were over 50 with existing conditions104. For 2024 the commission capped workers' age at 55 and required health certificates106. The Health Ministry still recorded 108 deaths among workers in February 2024107.

    Queues · Closing timeBritain · 2010

    Locked out at ten o'clock

    At the May 2010 general election, just over 1,200 people who were queuing when polls closed at 10pm were not allowed to vote, at 27 polling places in 16 constituencies61. The Electoral Commission blamed poor planning, unsuitable buildings and inadequate staffing.

    Established: Parliament changed the law. Section 19 of the Electoral Registration and Administration Act 2013 lets anyone in the queue at close of poll apply for a ballot60.

    Ballot programmingMichigan · 2020

    Antrim County's wrong numbers

    On election night, Antrim County's unofficial results were badly wrong. The county had changed ballot designs in October but did not update most of its scanners' memory cards, so votes were reported against the wrong contests32. The error became a centrepiece of claims that machines had switched votes.

    Established: A review by the computer scientist J. Alex Halderman for Michigan's secretary of state found human error, "not caused by a security breach", and "no credible evidence" it was deliberate. A hand count of the presidential race matched the final reported results to within 0.08%32.

    Printing · PaperArizona · 2022

    Too faint to scan

    On Election Day 2022, print-on-demand printers at some Maricopa County vote centres produced ballots too faint for the on-site scanners. Voters could leave them to be counted centrally. Kari Lake, the losing Republican candidate for governor, sued to overturn the result.

    Established: A review led by a retired chief justice of Arizona, Ruth McGregor, found the failures were caused by changes to the paper, not by malicious actions: the county had switched to a longer ballot on heavier paper, and the printers' fusers could not hold temperature35. Courts rejected Lake's claims, the last in 20243637.

    SuppliesTexas · 2022

    Harris County runs short of paper

    Republicans said polling places in Harris County, which includes Houston, ran out of ballot paper in November 2022. A state audit agreed that the county "failed to supply appropriate amounts of ballot paper to numerous polling locations", because of a flawed method of estimating need made worse by equipment jams4344.

    Established: The audit did not find that any outcome changed. Of 21 election contests Republicans brought, a judge upheld 20 results and voided one, a judicial race decided by 449 votes, on the basis of 1,430 illegal votes, not the paper shortage45.

    TabulationNew York · 2021

    135,000 test ballots

    A week after the 2021 Democratic mayoral primary, New York City's Board of Elections released a preliminary ranked-choice tally, then withdrew it: it had "inadvertently included 135,000 test ballots in the count"46.

    Established: The board apologised and corrected the tally. Eric Adams was declared the winner on 6 July by 8,426 votes47.

    CertificationArizona · 2022

    A county that would not certify

    Cochise County's Republican-majority Board of Supervisors missed the 28 November 2022 deadline to certify its canvass. The secretary of state and a retirees' group sued.

    Established: On 1 December a judge ruled the board had "exceeded its lawful authority" and ordered it to certify; it did that day38. Supervisors Peggy Judd and Tom Crosby were later indicted39. Judd pleaded guilty in October 2024 to a misdemeanour of failing to perform her duty as an election officer40; Crosby's case had not gone to trial as of May 2025, the latest report we found.

    SecurityUnited States · 2024

    Hoax bomb threats

    On Election Day 2024 polling places in several states received bomb threats. In Fulton County, Georgia, the police chief said 32 of 177 polling places were targeted; two sites were evacuated for about 30 minutes and the county sought court orders to extend hours42.

    Established: The FBI said many of the threats "appear to originate from Russian email domains" and that none had so far been found credible41. The FBI's wording describes where the emails came from, not who sent them.

    Audit · RecountGeorgia · 2020

    Counted three times

    Georgia's risk-limiting audit in November 2020 hand-tallied about 5 million presidential ballots in 41,881 batches. Four counties found ballots that had not been scanned or uploaded, but Joe Biden's lead held33.

    Established: After a machine recount requested by the Trump campaign, the state recertified on 7 December with a margin of 11,779. "We have now counted legally cast ballots three times, and the results remain unchanged," said the secretary of state, Brad Raffensperger34.

    ScaleIndia · 2024

    642 million votes, one counting day

    The Election Commission said 642 million people voted in 2024, including 312 million women56131. Votes were counted on 4 June in 542 of the 543 constituencies; Surat's seat had already gone to a candidate returned unopposed135.

    Established: Repolls were needed at 40 polling stations, against 540 in 201949. The Supreme Court rejected petitions to check every machine's paper slips51.

    Speed · ChallengeBrazil · 2022

    Results within hours, and a challenge rejected

    In the October 2022 run-off, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva was mathematically elected under three hours after polls closed, by one account77. The court that runs Brazil's elections holds public tests in which outside specialists attack its system78. Jair Bolsonaro's Liberal Party asked for votes from most machines to be annulled, citing a log bug.

    Established: The TSE's president rejected the request for "total absence of any evidence of irregularities" and fined the party about 23 million reais79. In June 2023 the TSE barred Bolsonaro from office for eight years for abuse of power and misuse of the media over a 2022 meeting with ambassadors at which he attacked the voting system; the ruling concerned his conduct, not the machines80.

    Estonia, a dispute without an incident. In 2014 a team of independent researchers who had observed the 2013 municipal elections reported "serious architectural limitations and procedural gaps" in Estonia's internet voting and showed in a lab that malware could change votes; they recommended stopping i-voting112. Estonia's electoral officials called the report unfair and said its threats were already known113. The researchers did not claim any real election had been compromised, and Estonia kept the system.

    04

    Reading the American count

    What the chain means for the night of 3 November and the weeks after it. Rules are as of October 2026 and vary by state.

    1. Election-night numbers are unofficial. They will move as postal, late and provisional ballots are added during the canvass23.
    2. The order of the count reflects state law. Pennsylvania and Wisconsin cannot start processing postal ballots until Election Day; Georgia, Nevada and others start weeks earlier18.
    3. Some ballots arrive after the day. Fourteen states plus DC count postal ballots cast by Election Day that arrive later, and the Supreme Court upheld that in June 202617.
    4. Signature problems can be fixed. In 33 states plus DC, voters whose envelope signature is questioned can cure it, within deadlines ranging from poll close to about three weeks after16.
    5. Close races trigger recounts. Many states recount automatically at a margin of 0.5%24.
    6. Paper gets checked. Every state now has a post-election audit law, and seven require risk-limiting audits20.
    7. Certification dates differ. From about two days after the vote to more than a month, depending on the state23.
    8. Military and overseas ballots went out first. The federal deadline for sending them was 19 September 202611.
