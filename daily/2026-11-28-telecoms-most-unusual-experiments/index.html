A ledger of seventeen trials

The Airtime Experiments

Seventeen times mobile operators tried something strange with pricing, ownership, free access, money or a whole new network, and what became of each.

Most of what a mobile network sells has been decided by experiment. Somebody had to try prepaid credit before it became normal, and somebody had to bill a news subscription to a phone before app stores existed. Many of the attempts failed loudly. Iridium went bankrupt nine months after opening1; Blyk, a network paid for by advertisers, lasted less than two years2.

This is a ledger of seventeen of those attempts, from a rechargeable card in Portugal in 1995 to a Japanese plan that costs nothing until you add to it. Each entry sets out the bet, how it worked and what happened afterwards. They are grouped by the kind of rule each one broke: how a network charges, who owns or runs it, what it gives away, whether it handles money, and how a new network gets its first customers.

The loudest carrier brands have their own guide, Loudmouths; Kenya's M-Pesa has Dial *334#. Neither is repeated here.

Thirty years of trials

Numbers match the ledger entries below. Where an experiment ran in stages, the first year is used.

The ledger

EXP-01Pricing1995

Portugal · TMN

The card you could refill

The betLet people pay for mobile calls in advance, and top up the balance at a cash machine.

In September 1995 TMN, the mobile arm of Telecom Portugal, launched a prepaid card called Mimo that could be recharged. Customers topped it up at ATMs with a debit card, using Portugal's shared Multibanco network34.

Prepaid cards already existed: the OECD notes that operators in Germany and Switzerland introduced them earlier the same year, but those cards could not be recharged, were expensive and were aimed at business travellers3. The Évora study says TMN took its inspiration from a German operator's non-rechargeable cards4. So the defensible claim is the OECD's: Mimo was the first mobile prepaid card that could be recharged. MEO, TMN's successor, calls it the world's first prepaid mobile service outright5.

Scratch to see what happened

Became the world's default

What happened. Prepaid went from about 11% of TMN's customers at the end of 1995 to 85% by June 1999. Portugal's mobile penetration rose from 3.4 per 100 people in 1995 to 14.9 in 1997, overtaking the EU-15 average4. By 2000 the OECD was noting that some Portuguese and Spanish operators had more than 80% of their customers on prepaid3.

EXP-02Ownership1997

Bangladesh · GrameenPhone

The phone as a cow

The betLend village women the money for a handset and let them run it as a payphone business.

Iqbal Quadir began pitching the idea in 1993. He argued that a mobile phone could be financed like the cows that microcredit borrowers already bought: "the mobile phone could be the cow"6. GrameenPhone, backed by Norway's Telenor, launched on 26 March 199767.

Village Phone was run by Grameen Bank, GrameenPhone and Grameen Telecom together. Grameen Bank lent the price of a handset to members, mostly women with good repayment records. Grameen Telecom bought airtime at half the normal price, and the "phone ladies" rented calls to their neighbours8. A 1999 report on the pilot described weekly repayments of $5 over three years9. Accounts of the loan differ: the academic study gives $200, while the 1999 report quotes one borrower paying more than $40089.

Scratch to see what happened

Outgrown by its own success

What happened. By April 2005 Telenor counted about 110,000 Village Phones in about 44,000 villages7. Then handsets got cheap and everyone bought one. Average annual revenue per Village Phone fell from $917 in 2003 to $396 in 20068. GrameenPhone itself had about 83 million subscribers by 20206.

EXP-03Launch1998

Global · Iridium

A phone network in orbit

The betPut 66 satellites in low orbit and sell executives a phone that works anywhere on Earth.

Iridium, backed by Motorola, opened for business on 1 November 1998110. Al Gore, then US vice-president, made the launch call by satellite to Gilbert Grosvenor, chairman of the National Geographic Society1011. The 66-satellite constellation cost about $5bn1213.

The handsets cost about $3,0001314. Sources give different per-minute prices, from $3 to $7 at the start1 up to a range of $6 to $3014, depending on the route. The phone needed a clear line of sight to a satellite, so it worked badly indoors and in cars13.

Scratch to see what happened

Bankrupt in nine months, then revived

What happened. Iridium had forecast 500,000 subscribers and signed up about 10,000131. It defaulted on its loans and filed for Chapter 11 in August 1999115. Motorola prepared to bring the satellites down14. Instead an investor group led by Dan Colussy bought the system for about $25m, and a court approved the sale in November 2000, alongside a $72m US Defense Department contract12. Service restarted in March 2001 at under $1.50 a minute, aimed at industry and government16. The revived company reported about 2.5 million billable subscribers at the end of 202517.

EXP-04Launch1999

Japan · NTT Docomo

The internet on the phone bill

The betLet anyone sell content to phone users, bill it on the monthly phone bill and keep a small cut.

NTT Docomo launched i-mode on 22 February 1999 and passed five million subscribers within a year18. Publishers sold subscriptions, typically priced around ¥300, which Docomo added to the customer's phone bill. Docomo kept 9%, far less than the 25–30% that Apple and Google would later take from app developers18. Billing through the phone company gave publishers a way to charge small sums.

i-mode also gave the world emoji: Docomo's Shigetaka Kurita designed the original set of 176 for it18.

Scratch to see what happened

Huge at home, failed abroad

What happened. i-mode peaked at about 49 million subscribers in July 201019. Docomo took it to 18 markets18, starting with E-Plus in Germany and KPN in the Netherlands in 200220, but Europe never took to it. O2 UK stopped selling i-mode handsets in 2007 after about 260,000 sign-ups in two years21, and other operators closed it too22. Docomo said in November 2016 that it would stop shipping i-mode phones23, and the service was switched off on 31 March 202618.

EXP-05Money2000–04

Philippines · Smart and Globe

Money in a text message

The betTurn the phone into a wallet years before anyone wrote rules for mobile money.

In December 2000 the Philippine Star reported plans to launch Smart Money, a cash card linked to a Smart phone and built with MasterCard. Users could move money from a bank account, pay at MasterCard merchants and send money from one card to another24. The GSMA dates the service to 2001 and calls it the world's first mobile money service25; the two sources also name different partner banks.

Globe followed in 2004 with G-Cash, which worked by text message and needed no card. It ran through a separate company, G-Xchange, so that it would fit the central bank's rules2526. There was no specific regulation for mobile money at first. The central bank let the services run and watched, then issued e-money rules in March 200926.

Scratch to see what happened

Slow, then enormous

What happened. Growth was held back for years by strict identity checks, rules on agents, and the fact that agents earned better commissions selling airtime26. Smart Money later became PayMaya, now Maya25. GCash grew from 20 million registered users in January 2020 to 46 million by July 202125; the GSMA now puts it at close to 95 million users25. Kenya's M-Pesa, which came later, is the better-known story (see Dial *334#).

EXP-06Free access2001

South Africa · Vodacom

Please Call Me

The betLet customers with no airtime send a free text asking someone to ring them back.

Vodacom launched Please Call Me in March 2001. The idea came from Kenneth Makate, then a trainee accountant at the company, who took it to Vodacom's head of product development in November 2000. The courts later found that the two men made an oral agreement that Makate would share in the revenue if the product succeeded27.

The service let people who could not afford a call still reach someone. It also led to a court fight that lasted 17 years: Makate sued in 2008.

Scratch to see what happened

Settled in 2025

What happened. In April 2016 the Constitutional Court ruled that Vodacom was bound by the agreement and ordered it to negotiate compensation2728. Vodacom's chief executive set the figure at R47m in 2019; the High Court set that aside in 202228. In 2024 the Supreme Court of Appeal ordered a share of revenue plus interest29, and in July 2025 the Constitutional Court sent the case back for a rehearing30. The two sides settled on confidential terms in November 202531.

EXP-07Pricing2003

Philippines · Smart

Airtime in sachets

The betSell credit in tiny amounts, the way corner shops sell shampoo by the single sachet.

In 2003 the lowest conventional Smart top-up was P300, and more than 95% of Philippine mobile users were prepaid32. That May, Smart launched Smart Load, which let shopkeepers top up a customer's phone over the air in amounts as small as P30, sold at sari-sari corner stores. Smart said outright that it was borrowing the sachet model from consumer goods33.

In December 2003 it added Pasaload, which let users send airtime to each other by text. A Philippine Star columnist reported nearly a million transfers in the first few days, and warned of scams34.

Scratch to see what happened

Won a GSMA award

What happened. Smart Load won the GSMA's 2004 award for best mobile service for the consumer market32.

EXP-08Free access2006

United States · Virgin Mobile USA

Minutes for watching adverts

The betPay prepaid customers in free minutes for their attention.

Virgin Mobile USA launched Sugar Mama in June 2006. Customers signed up online and earned a free minute for watching a 30-second video advert and answering questions about it, a minute for every two branded texts, and a minute for each minute spent on a survey, up to 75 free minutes a month3536. The first advertisers included Xbox, Diet Mountain Dew and the anti-smoking "truth" campaign3536.

Scratch to see what happened

Fate unclear

What happened. By July 2007 Virgin said users had earned more than nine million free minutes, with an average click-through rate of 5.5%, and that advertisers including Nintendo, Subway and Levi's had joined37. Reliable accounts of when, or whether, the scheme formally ended are hard to find.

EXP-09Free access2007

United Kingdom · Blyk

A network paid for by advertisers

The betGive young people free texts and minutes in return for receiving six targeted adverts a day.

Blyk launched in the UK on 24 September 2007, running on Orange's network. It was led by Pekka Ala-Pietilä, a former president of Nokia, and Antti Öhrling, a branding veteran38. Members had to be between 16 and 24. They got 217 free texts and 43 free minutes a month and agreed to receive up to six adverts a day, matched to a profile they filled in38. Launch advertisers included Coca-Cola, L'Oréal Paris and Yell38.

It signed up 100,000 members by around April 2008, ahead of its own target, and claimed an average response rate of 29% to its campaigns39.

Scratch to see what happened

Closed in 2009

What happened. In July 2009 Blyk said its UK network was finishing, and its SIMs were switched off that August240. Trade reports said advertisers had used it more for market research than for brand campaigns, and that it was too small2. Blyk became a partner to other operators, starting with Orange, which launched its own ad-funded service with Blyk40.

EXP-10Ownership2009

United Kingdom · giffgaff

Customers as the call centre

The betRun a network with no phone helpline and pay customers to answer each other's questions.

giffgaff launched on 25 November 2009 as an online-only network owned by O241. It is still wholly owned by O2's parent, now Virgin Media O242. There was no customer service line. Members answered each other's questions on a community forum and earned points for it, for recruiting friends and, for a time, for ideas42. At launch the company said: "We don't have mega advertising budgets so we need our members to help make us famous"41.

The points, called Payback, are worth a penny each and are paid out twice a year as cash, airtime or a donation to charity43.

Scratch to see what happened

Still running

What happened. giffgaff's accounts show more than £21m paid out in Payback since launch, with just over £1m paid in 2024, and about 4.1 million active members at the end of 202444. In 2019 Ofcom fined it £1.4m for overcharging about 2.6 million customers45. In 2023 it began selling 18-month contracts, a step towards an ordinary network42.

EXP-11Pricing2011

United States · Republic Wireless

Wi-Fi first, mobile second

The betRoute calls over the customer's own Wi-Fi and use the mobile network only as a fallback.

Republic Wireless came out of Bandwidth.com, an internet calling company, and launched in beta in November 2011. It offered unlimited talk, text and data for $19 a month with no contract46. Its phones sent calls and data over Wi-Fi when they could and fell back to Sprint's network when they could not. At launch the only phone was a modified LG Optimus costing $19946.

The plan relied on customers using little mobile data. Republic tracked each user's cellular use against the community, a rule it soon dropped4647.

Scratch to see what happened

Closed in 2023

What happened. Dish bought Republic Wireless in 2021, when it had about 200,000 customers48. In July 2023 Dish told customers the brand was closing and moved them to Boost Infinite by the end of August49.

EXP-12Free access2012

72 countries · Wikimedia Foundation

Wikipedia without a data charge

The betPersuade operators to let people read Wikipedia without paying for the data.

The Wikimedia Foundation launched Wikipedia Zero in 201250. Partner operators zero-rated Wikipedia, so reading it did not use up a customer's data. At its peak the scheme involved 97 operators in 72 countries, reaching a potential 800 million people51; another account counts 92 operators52.

It drew criticism on net-neutrality grounds, and Chile's regulator ordered zero-rating schemes including Wikipedia Zero to end in 201450. In Angola, Inverse reported, users exploited the free access to share pirated files51.

Scratch to see what happened

Ended 2018

What happened. In February 2018 the foundation said it would sign no new partnerships and let the existing ones expire that year. It cited a sharp fall in interest since 2016, cheaper mobile data and low awareness of Wikipedia in many of the target markets5153.

EXP-13Ownership2013

Mexico · Talea de Castro and TIC

A village builds its own network

The betIf no operator will come, the community can build and run a mobile network itself.

Talea de Castro, a mostly Zapotec village of about 2,500 people in Oaxaca, had never been served by Telcel. In 2013 it switched on its own GSM network, with technical help from the non-profit Rhizomatica, using open-source software and a two-year test permit5455. The village paid about 400,000 pesos for the equipment55. Subscribers paid 15 pesos a month, calls to the United States cost a few cents a minute, and calls were capped at five minutes because capacity was small54. About 600 people had signed up by August 201354.

In July 2016 Mexico's telecoms regulator, the IFT, granted Telecomunicaciones Indígenas Comunitarias (TIC) a new kind of licence: a social-use indigenous concession to run non-profit mobile networks in parts of five states5657.

Scratch to see what happened

Still running, with one loss

What happened. TIC fought the regulator's spectrum fees and won: in January 2021 the Supreme Court ruled that it need not pay them5859. Sources give different counts of the communities it serves, from about 20 to around 60 localities5859. Talea itself lost more than half its subscribers when Movistar arrived in 2014, and its community network closed for good in March 201955.

EXP-14Free access2015–16

India · Facebook and Reliance Communications

Free Basics meets India

The betGive people a free, limited bundle of websites, chosen and served through Facebook.

Facebook's Internet.org app offered a set of websites that people could use without paying for data. In India it ran on Reliance Communications, and in September 2015 it was renamed Free Basics60. When the regulator, TRAI, opened a consultation on differential data pricing in December 2015, Facebook campaigned hard: billboards in six cities, full-page newspaper advertisements, a Mark Zuckerberg op-ed in the Times of India60.

The Save the Internet campaign argued that it broke net neutrality. TRAI received about 2.4 million comments, and called Facebook's effort a "crudely majoritarian orchestrated opinion poll"61.

Scratch to see what happened

Effectively banned in India

What happened. On 8 February 2016 TRAI banned operators from charging different prices for data depending on the content a user reached6263. Free Basics in India ended. A Facebook board member, Marc Andreessen, tweeted that "anti-colonialism has been economically catastrophic for the Indian people for decades", then deleted the tweets and apologised64. Elsewhere Free Basics reached 63 countries by July 201765, though it later quietly ended in several, including Myanmar66.

EXP-15Pricing2015

United States · Google

A refund for data you didn't use

The betCharge $10 a gigabyte and pay back the value of whatever the customer doesn't use.

Google announced Project Fi on 22 April 2015, at first only for Nexus 6 owners. It cost $20 a month for calls and texts plus $10 for each gigabyte of data, and unused data was credited back in full67. It used both the Sprint and T-Mobile networks, plus Wi-Fi67; US Cellular was added in 201668.

In November 2018 it was renamed Google Fi and opened to iPhones and most Android phones69.

Scratch to see what happened

Still running, now mostly unlimited

What happened. After T-Mobile bought Sprint and Fi dropped US Cellular in 2023, T-Mobile became its only official network70. By its tenth anniversary in April 2025 most of its plans were unlimited71; the $20 Flexible plan is still sold, as of November 202672. That month Google added automatic switching between T-Mobile and a second 5G network on Pixel 9 and newer phones7372.

EXP-16Launch2016

India · Reliance Jio

Free until further notice

The betLaunch a new 4G network by giving everything away for months.

Reliance Jio opened its 4G network across India on 5 September 2016. Under the Welcome Offer, data, calls and Jio's apps were free until 31 December 2016, and Jio promised that domestic voice calls would be free for its customers "forever"74. In December Mukesh Ambani extended the free service to 31 March 2017, with a daily data limit for new users75.

On 21 February 2017 Jio said it had passed 100 million subscribers in 170 days, and announced a paid membership, Jio Prime76.

Scratch to see what happened

Remade a market

What happened. According to TRAI figures, the average price of a gigabyte of mobile data in India fell from Rs 269 in 2014 to Rs 11.78 in 201877; Jio is widely credited with much of that fall, though TRAI's figures don't attribute it. Vodafone India and Idea Cellular merged in 2018 after what Reuters called a bitter price war78, and the private market went from 11 operators to three79.

EXP-17Pricing2021

Japan · KDDI

A plan that costs nothing

The betCharge a base fee of zero and sell data and calls as optional "toppings".

In 2018 Yoshihide Suga, then Japan's chief cabinet secretary, said mobile bills had room to fall by about 40%, and carriers' shares lost about ¥1 trillion in value80. As prime minister he kept up the pressure, and in March 2021 all three big carriers launched cheap online-only 20GB plans: Docomo's ahamo, SoftBank's LINEMO and KDDI's povo818283.

In September 2021 KDDI went further with povo 2.0. The base plan costs ¥0, with data limited to 128kbps. Customers buy toppings as needed, such as 3GB for 30 days at ¥990 or 24 hours of unlimited data at ¥33084. The account can be suspended if no paid topping is bought for 180 days84. A KDDI executive said one 20GB plan had been too rigid for both light and heavy users85.

Scratch to see what happened

Still running

What happened. Its toppings page still sold 3GB for 30 days at ¥99086, and in July 2026 povo added year-long toppings such as 120GB for ¥21,60087.
