The Last-Mile Atlas

Twenty-five European places that look easy to reach by train, and what the final connection from the station really involves: every leg, how often it runs, when it stops, and where it catches people out.

On a rail map, Hallstatt has its own station. So does Rocamadour, and so, more or less, does Nazaré. What the map leaves out is what happens when you step off the train: a boat across a lake, a taxi up from a plateau, a bus that runs only when the tide allows.

This atlas is about that final stretch, usually somewhere between five and thirty kilometres and occasionally much more, from the nearest useful railway station to the place you came to see. For each of 25 destinations it sets out the station, every leg of the onward chain, the typical transfer time, how often the connection runs in high and low season, and the one thing most likely to catch you out.

Some turn out easier than they look. Bellagio has no railway at all, yet a train from Milan and a fifteen-minute boat put you on its waterfront in around two hours. Others are harder than they seem. Holy Island’s bus is timetabled around the tides and, outside the school holidays, runs on two days a week at most. Rocamadour’s station sits on a plateau kilometres from the village, with a bus only in summer.

Timetables change every season. Each frequency here is tied to a dated timetable or source, checked in November 2026. Read them as the shape of a service, and check with the operator before you travel.

Ten unusually manageable last miles

The desk’s ranking, favouring connections that run all year, run often, and take least time from the platform. All ten work as a weekend by rail.

  1. Bellagio Italy

    A 15-minute boat, about 18 crossings a day even in winter, from a station an hour out of Milan.

  2. Hallstatt Austria

    The boat is timed to the trains. Just arrive before the evening.

  3. Giethoorn Netherlands

    An intercity station, then a bus of a quarter of an hour.

  4. Königssee Germany

    A short bus ride and a five-minute walk. The boats are the bottleneck, not the bus.

  5. Bled Slovenia

    Frequent buses cover 4 km from a main-line station.

  6. Saas-Fee Switzerland

    A bus every half hour, from before five in the morning to half past eleven at night.

  7. Mürren Switzerland

    Eighteen minutes by cable and rail. Check the maintenance calendar.

  8. Ærøskøbing Denmark

    A ten-minute walk to a boat that runs all year.

  9. Hydra Greece

    Metro to the port, then a fast boat most of the day in season.

  10. Étretat France

    Slow, but hourly and year-round from the station forecourt.

Bellagio

Italy · Lakes & mountains

  1. TrainVarenna-Esino
  2. Walk / taxiWalk downhill to the Varenna pier
  3. FerryNavigazione Laghi ferry, 15 min
  4. ArriveBellagio
Station
Varenna-Esino: Trenord line from Milano Centrale towards Lecco and Tirano, about 64 minutes, roughly hourly1.
Transfer
30–60 min, depending on the wait for a boat
How often
Winter weekday timetable (2 November 2026 – 14 March 2027): about 18 crossings a day each way; there are more in the summer timetable23.
Seasons & limits
Runs all year, with a reduced service on 25 December and 1 January. Boats have limited capacity, and a ticket bought on board costs €1 more when the ticket office is open2. The alternative is ASF bus C130 from Como, about 70 minutes on the lakeside road4.
The catch
Very little. The only weak point is the walk between station and pier: travellers on forums warn that a seven-minute connection with luggage is too tight, especially uphill to the train5.
Worth the effort
Bellagio sits on the point where Lake Como divides into its two southern arms, with the gardens of Villa Melzi, Villa Serbelloni on the promontory and the Romanesque church of San Giacomo6.
Weekend worksAn hour from Milan by train and a quarter of an hour by boat.
No. 02

Hallstatt

Austria · Lakes & mountains

  1. TrainHallstatt station
  2. Walk / taxiWalk down to the landing
  3. FerryFerry Stefanie across the lake
  4. ArriveHallstatt
Station
Hallstatt station: Salzkammergut line between Attnang-Puchheim and Stainach-Irdning; about 2h15–2h40 from Salzburg with a change7.
Transfer
15–25 min
How often
The boat is timed to meet the trains8. The departure list published by the regional tourist board shows 18 crossings from Hallstatt Markt on school-term weekdays and 15 in the holidays, between 07:07 and 18:50; that list carries 2024–25 dates, so check the current year9.
Seasons & limits
Runs all year: the tourist board says it is the only Austrian lake boat that keeps going through winter. Fares are €4 single and €8 return, cash only10. Without the boat, the route is OÖVV bus 541 from Bad Ischl with a change to the 543 at Gosaumühle, 40–50 minutes; the last bus from Bad Ischl leaves at 18:1611.
The catch
The station is on the wrong side of the lake. Arrival depends on a cash-only boat that stops in the early evening, so a late train leaves you on the far shore.
Worth the effort
The village is the core of the Hallstatt-Dachstein / Salzkammergut Cultural Landscape, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1997, where salt has been mined since prehistory12. About 750 residents receive up to 10,000 visitors on peak days13.
Weekend worksStaying overnight is the way to see the village outside the day-trip peak.

Station
Berchtesgaden Hbf: end of the branch from Freilassing via Bad Reichenhall18.
Transfer
20–30 min to the pier; about an hour to St Bartholomä
How often
Buses run through the day, taking 10–15 minutes19; check the current 841 timetable20. Boats run about every 30 minutes all year21.
Seasons & limits

The bus is easy. The constraint is the boat: a short daytime window in winter and queues in summer.

Greece · Island & tidal

  1. TrainPiraeus
  2. Walk / taxiWalk 15–20 min to the Saronic gate
  3. FerryFast boat, 1h05 direct or up to 2h with stops
  4. ArriveHydra
Station
Piraeus: southern end of Athens Metro line 1; suburban rail also reaches the port44.
Transfer
1h30–2h30
How often
Six to eight boats a day from May to September45. The route runs all year46; winter frequency is lower, and we could not confirm a figure.
Seasons & limits
The fast boats carry no vehicles46, so book ahead for summer weekends.
The catch
The metro reaches the port but not the boat: Saronic boats use gate E8, a 15–20 minute walk away4447. After that the island is easy on foot, because the harbour is the town.
Worth the effort

Mont-Saint-Michel

France · Island & tidal

  1. TrainPontorson (or Rennes)
  2. BusBus to the mainland terminus: 27 min from Pontorson, about 1h15 from Rennes
  3. BusFree Le Passeur shuttle, about 12 min
  4. ArriveMont-Saint-Michel
Station
Pontorson (or Rennes): Pontorson is on the Caen–Rennes line, 9 km away60; Rennes has TGVs from Paris.
Transfer
45–50 min from Pontorson; 1h35–1h45 from Rennes
How often
The 30 March – 11 October 2026 timetable shows several Pontorson departures a day each way61; a separate autumn timetable runs from 12 October60. Rennes coaches run two or three times a day62; the winter 2025–26 brochure lists two63. Le Passeur runs at least 07:30–23:00, and on request by phone outside those hours60.
Seasons & limits
At very high tides some stops are not served61. Rennes coaches are a separate ticket from the train, though timed to Paris TGVs62.

No bus reaches the Mont. Everyone ends at a mainland terminus and takes the shuttle or walks about 3.8 km over the bridge62. Off-season, Rennes has only a couple of coaches a day.
Worth the effort
Mont-Saint-Michel and its Bay has been on the UNESCO World Heritage List since 197964.
Weekend worksA night on or near the Mont gets you there before and after the day-trip peak.

  4. ArriveBelle-Île-en-Mer
Station
Auray: Rennes/Nantes–Quimper line, with TGVs from Paris.
Transfer
2h–2h30
How often
The ferry runs from five round trips a day in midwinter to 20 in July and August65. The Tire-Bouchon train ran in 2026 at weekends from mid-June, daily from 29 June to 31 August and at weekends to mid-September, with four to ten round trips a day66.

About 3h15 by sea; 1h30–2h via Land’s End airport
How often
One round trip a day when running, leaving Penzance at 09:15 and returning at 16:30111112. The tourist board says six days a week, or seven in high season112.
Seasons & limits
The ferry runs from March to November only113. Year-round, a bus from Penzance station meets Skybus flights from Land’s End114110; Newquay flights are suspended from 1 September 2026 to 16 March 2027115. 2026 is Scillonian III’s last season; Scillonian IV is due in March 2027116117.
The catch
From November to March there is no ferry, and the only scheduled link is a small plane from a weather-prone airfield.
Worth the effort
Small boats from the Hugh Town quay reach the off-islands, including Tresco and its subtropical Abbey Garden118.
Better as a longer stayOne sailing each way a day leaves a weekend with about a day and a half on the islands.
No. 22

Holy Island (Lindisfarne)

United Kingdom · Island & tidal

  1. TrainBerwick-upon-Tweed
  2. BusBus 477 over the tidal causeway, 33–36 min
  3. ArriveHoly Island (Lindisfarne)
Station
Berwick-upon-Tweed: East Coast Main Line between Newcastle and Edinburgh.
Transfer
35–40 min, when the bus runs
How often
In term time, Wednesdays and Saturdays only; in school holidays, Monday to Saturday; never on Sundays119. In November 2026 there are two return trips on each running day, and not every Wednesday or Saturday has one120.
Seasons & limits
The causeway floods twice a day121. Journeys are coded to the dates they run, chosen around safe crossing times122. Glen Valley Tours took over the route from Borders Buses in September 2025119. A pre-booked taxi from Berwick costs about £35123.
The catch
The tide sets the timetable. Outside school holidays the bus runs two days a week at most, and missing the return can mean waiting out the tide on the island. Check the council’s crossing times first124.
Worth the effort
Aidan founded a monastery here in 635; the Lindisfarne Gospels were made here, and the Viking raid of 793 was the first significant one in western Europe125.
Weekend, with planningStay on the island to see it after the tide cuts off the day visitors.
No. 23

Iona

United Kingdom · Island & tidal

  1. TrainOban
  2. FerryCalMac to Craignure, Mull, about 50 min
  3. BusWest Coast Motors 496 to Fionnphort, about 1h20
  4. FerryCalMac passenger ferry to Iona, about 10 min
  5. ArriveIona
Station
Oban: end of the West Highland line branch from Glasgow; the ferry leaves from the pier beside the station126.
Transfer
3h–3h15
How often
Oban–Craignure has five to nine sailings a day in the winter timetable (19 October 2026 – 12 March 2027)127. The 496 bus runs three times a day each way, Monday to Saturday, with nothing on Sundays; only the 10:50 and 16:50 from Craignure meet Oban sailings128. Some Iona sailings will wait for the Craignure bus if requested129.
Seasons & limits
On winter Sundays the Iona ferry runs only if requested by 16:00 on the previous working day129. CalMac advises booking even as a foot passenger130. Cars need special permission on Iona126.
The catch
Three timed connections across two islands hinge on one bus, with only two useful connections a day from Oban in winter. A late ferry or a Sunday breaks the chain.
Worth the effort
St Columba founded a monastery here in 563; the site has the medieval abbey and nunnery, early high crosses and the Reilig Odhráin burial ground131.
Better as a longer stayBetter as two or three nights, or with a night on Mull. Avoid winter Sundays.
No. 24

Reine, Lofoten

Norway · Island & tidal

  1. TrainBodø
  2. Walk / taxiWalk a few minutes to the ferry quay
  3. FerryTorghatten ferry to Moskenes, 3–3.5h direct
  4. BusLocal bus or walk on to Reine
  5. ArriveReine, Lofoten
Station
Bodø: end of the Nordland line from Trondheim, about ten hours with two trains a day132.
Transfer
4h–4h30 on a direct sailing with a bus
How often
In summer 2025, up to eight direct sailings a day plus some via Værøy and Røst133. A separate winter timetable runs from 7 September 2026134; we could not confirm its frequency.
Seasons & limits
This open-sea crossing is prone to weather cancellations, mostly in autumn and winter133. Foot passengers who do not reserve travel free134.
The catch
The final stretch from Moskenes is short, and bus 300 runs through Reine135. But late sailings leave you there without one: the 21:00 from Bodø arrives at 00:15136, and there is no local taxi service, so a taxi must be booked in advance133.
Worth the effort
A fishing village of about 300 people between the Reinefjord and the Vestfjord, below the Sherpa-built steps up Reinebringen135.
Better as a longer stayTrondheim to Bodø alone takes about ten hours; plan four nights or more.
No. 25

Geiranger

Norway · Lakes & mountains

  1. TrainÅndalsnes
  2. BusVy Buss VY25 over Trollstigen, about 3h30
  3. ArriveGeiranger
Station
Åndalsnes: end of the Rauma line from Dombås, on the Oslo–Trondheim route137.
Transfer
About 3h30, on the one bus
How often
One bus a day, 1 June to 30 September 2026. Tickets must be bought in advance138139. Outside that season there is no bus over Trollstigen.
Seasons & limits
The Trollstigen road closes every winter140. It was shut by rockfall risk from June 2024 until July 2025141, and from 28 September 2026 it was closed on weekdays (07:00–18:00) for up to four weeks142.
The catch
One bus a day for four months, over a mountain road with a recent rockfall history. Miss it, or come out of season, and this route has no public transport.
Worth the effort
The Geirangerfjord is part of the West Norwegian Fjords World Heritage Site143.
Better as a longer stayBest as a one-way loop: Oslo, Åndalsnes, Geiranger, Ålesund.

How this was put together

Each destination was researched in November 2026 from operators’ timetables and route pages wherever they could be read, then from tourist boards and established travel references. Where a figure comes from a route planner or a travellers’ forum, the tag says so in its source. Where sources disagree, both are given. Fares appear only where an operator or tourist board publishes them.

“Nearest useful station” means the one travellers actually use for the onward link, which is not always the closest: Bled and Nazaré both have a nearer station that works less well. Transfer times are typical door-of-station to centre-of-destination times. The punch ratings, the weekend stamps, the count of connections and the top-ten ranking are the desk’s judgements from the published timetables, not official classifications.
