The urban waterfront access atlas

Reaching the Water

Twenty harbour, river, lake and sea cities: what stands between each one and its water, how it got there, what was removed, and a scorecard comparing public access and how well each waterfront is woven into the city.

20 cities · 4 kinds of water · 139 sources

Most of these twenty cities lost their water in the same few decades and to the same kind of thing. Sydney opened the elevated Cahill Expressway at Circular Quay in 1958 1. San Francisco opened the first stretch of the Embarcadero Freeway in 1959 2, the same year Boston's elevated Central Artery opened between downtown and the wharves 3. Toronto built the Gardiner from 1955 to 1966 4, and Paris opened its right-bank expressway in 1967 5. Before the motorways it was railways and ports: Barcelona's first line ran along the shore from 1848 6, and Bilbao's city-centre bank held a container terminal, a shipyard and a railway before its redevelopment from the 1990s 7,8.

Getting the water back has rarely been the work of a plan alone. San Francisco's voters chose to keep their freeway in 1986; the Loma Prieta earthquake damaged it three years later and it came down 2,9. Barcelona moved its coastal railway underground for the 1992 Olympics 10. Boston Harbor was cleaned up because a federal judge made it happen 11. The surest sign that a city has its water back is that people swim in it, and that depends on sewers: Copenhagen's first harbour bath opened in 2002 after years of work underground 12,13, and the Seine reopened for swimming in 2025 for the first time since 1923 14.

The oldest protections are legal. Massachusetts traces public rights on its shore to colonial ordinances of the 1640s 15; Chicago's lakefront rests on a note on an 1836 canal map that a merchant spent years enforcing in court 16; Hong Kong passed a law in 1997 presuming against further reclamation of its harbour 17. Where no such rule exists, private ownership sets the line. Around Lake Zurich, outside the city, 12.6 km of shore are gaps in the lakeside path 18, and in 2024 the canton's voters rejected an initiative to open them 19. Across the canton of Geneva, a 2021 bill in the cantonal parliament estimates only about 40 per cent of the lakeshore is effectively public 20.

The atlas below takes each city in turn, with a cross-section of the edge as it is now and, where something was removed, as it was. The scorecard at the end compares them on four measures.

How the scores work

Each city is scored from 1 to 5 on four measures, for its central waterfront rather than its whole shore. The scores are the desk's judgement from the documented features cited in each entry, not measurements, and they are there to compare like with like.

Public access
How much of the edge anyone can use, free and by right.
Continuity
Whether you can follow the water on foot without being sent inland.
Transit
Public transport on the water or along it, especially on the ordinary fare system.
Integration
Whether neighbourhoods meet the water directly, or across motorways, railways or levees.

The atlas

Cross-sections run from the city on the left to the water on the right. They are schematic: widths show order and rough proportion, not scale.

Sydney

Australia

Port Jackson (Sydney Harbour) (Harbour and bay)

11 kmcontinuous city foreshore walk, Woolloomooloo to Anzac Bridge, since 202116/20

Sydney's harbour is a commuter network before it is a view, and the city centre's shore has only recently become one continuous walk.

In the early 1930s Sydney Ferries Limited was the world's largest ferry operator, carrying about 40 million journeys a year. After the Harbour Bridge opened on 19 March 1932 patronage fell to 14 million and the fleet was halved within twelve months. Circular Quay has been the centre of the system throughout 21.

The last missing link at Barangaroo opened in 2021, completing an 11 km foreshore walk from Woolloomooloo to the Anzac Bridge. Great Walks reported that the stretch had been closed to the public for 200 years 22,23. Barangaroo had become a concrete container apron in the 1960s. The state announced a 22-hectare waterfront precinct in 2003 as shipping moved to Port Botany, and the 6-hectare Barangaroo Reserve opened in August 2015, a rebuilt headland made from about 10,000 sandstone blocks cut on site 23,24. Further out, the headlands came back to the public as defence land passed to the state under a 1979 agreement, including 172.9 hectares at North Head 25.

Still in the way

The elevated Cahill Expressway, opened at Circular Quay in 1958 on a viaduct above the railway. In 1994 the prime minister, Paul Keating, offered A$150 million to demolish it; the New South Wales premier, John Fahey, declined 1. Beyond the centre, long stretches of suburban harbour shore are private.

Getting there

Government ferries fan out from Circular Quay, where trains also stop on the viaduct above the wharves 21,1.

Public access
Centre and headlands public; much suburban shore private.
Continuity
11 km in the centre; patchy harbour-wide.
Transit
Ferries are part of daily transport.
Integration
Expressway still walls off Circular Quay.

Copenhagen

Denmark

Inner Harbour (Harbour and bay)

2002year the first harbour bath opened at Islands Brygge19/20

Copenhagen turned an industrial port into somewhere people swim at lunchtime, and it started underground, in the sewers.

Until 1995, 93 discharge points emptied the city's sewage into the harbour. The utility began clean-up work in the mid-1990s, building twelve large underground basins to hold back overflow from heavy rain 12. By 2002 the water was clean enough for the Islands Brygge harbour bath, which opened on 15 July that year. The city warns swimmers when E. coli rises above 500 per 100 ml 13.

Regeneration followed the port's industries out, beginning in the 1990s on former naval land at Holmen 26. The Harbour Circle is now a 13 km walking and cycling loop through Nyhavn, Christianshavn, Islands Brygge and Sydhavnen 27; the 180-metre Inderhavnsbroen, a bridge for pedestrians and cyclists only, linked Nyhavn and Christianshavn in 2016 28.

Still in the way

Some newer frontages. Kalvebod Brygge, built in the 1980s and 1990s, was described as austere and deserted until the 2013 Kalvebod Waves boardwalks were added to draw people down to the water 29.

Getting there

Harbour buses have run since 2000 on the same tickets as buses, Metro and trains; the fleet went battery-electric in 2020 30.

Public access
Free baths and public quays.
Continuity
13 km loop joined by walking and cycling bridges.
Transit
Harbour buses on the normal ticket.
Integration
Strong in the old centre; some 1990s office fronts are bleak.

Oslo

Norway

Oslofjord (Harbour and bay)

9 kmplanned continuous harbour promenade, Frognerkilen to the Alna, 2008–203018/20

Oslo decided in 2000 to hand its central harbour back to the city, and has spent a quarter of a century doing it.

The city council chose the Fjord City option in January 2000, after the port's 1998 strategic plan, and approved the Fjord City plan in 2008; in 2009 it fixed Sydhavna, to the south, as the permanent port 31,32. Aker Brygge, built in stages from 1985 to 1998 on old shipyard land, came first, and the City Hall square went car-free in 1994 32.

Bjørvika, the old port district east of the centre, was cut off by the railway lines into the central station and a busy fast road 33. The E18 went into the Bjørvika tunnel in 2010, and the surface became a city street 34. The Opera House, completed in 2008, has a roof that rises out of the fjord and is meant to be walked on 33. The city is building a continuous harbour promenade of more than 9 km, from Frognerkilen in the west to the mouth of the Alna in the east 35.

Still in the way

The E18 in the west at Frognerstranda, which still forms a visual and physical barrier 36, plus the railway tracks into Oslo S 33 and the cruise and ferry terminals that remain in the central harbour 31.

Getting there

Ruter ferries from Aker Brygge serve the inner-fjord islands and Nesodden, and ordinary Ruter tickets are valid on all boat routes 37.

Public access
Opera roof, sea baths and public quays.
Continuity
Promenade mostly built; runs to 2030.
Transit
Island ferries on the city ticket.
Integration
Rail yard and western E18 still cut across.

Hong Kong

China

Victoria Harbour (Harbour and bay)

13 kmcontinuous promenade along Hong Kong Island, completed December 202515/20

A harbour shaped for a century by land reclamation now has a law presuming against more of it, and a promenade being stitched together piece by piece.

The Protection of the Harbour Ordinance began as a private member's bill introduced by the legislator Christine Loh in 1996 and passed on 27 June 1997. It sets a presumption against reclamation in the harbour, and in 2004 the Court of Final Appeal ruled against a draft plan for Wan Chai North under it 17. An amendment in force since May 2025 keeps the presumption but lets small harbour-enhancement and non-permanent reclamations be exempted 38. A harbourfront committee was set up the same year; the Harbourfront Commission replaced it in 2010 39.

The government puts the harbour's waterfront at about 73 km, of which about 35 km cannot be joined up as promenade 40. On Hong Kong Island a continuous promenade of about 13 km from Kennedy Town to Shau Kei Wan was completed on 29 December 2025, when the last stretch of the East Coast Boardwalk opened 41. That boardwalk runs underneath the elevated Island Eastern Corridor expressway: the road was bridged around, not removed 40. As of February 2026 the government was aiming for about 34 km of harbourfront promenade on both sides by 2028 42.

Still in the way

Expressways along the shore, port and utility uses, and a Kowloon side that is still in fragments 40,42.

Getting there

The Star Ferry, founded in 1888 by the Parsee merchant Dorabjee Naorojee Mithaiwala, still crosses the harbour 43.

Public access
Promenades free and public; half the edge cannot be joined.
Continuity
Island spine complete; Kowloon fragmented.
Transit
Star Ferry and MTR on both shores.
Integration
Expressways sit between many districts and the water.

San Francisco

United States

San Francisco Bay (Harbour and bay)

1991year demolition of the Embarcadero Freeway began18/20

San Francisco voted to keep its waterfront freeway in 1986. An earthquake reopened the question three years later.

Construction of the double-decked Embarcadero Freeway began in 1955 and its first section opened in 1959. Voters rejected a proposal to demolish it in 1986 2. The Loma Prieta earthquake of 17 October 1989 damaged it badly; the mayor, Art Agnos, pushed for demolition over repair, and it came down from 1991, replaced by a surface boulevard and a waterfront promenade 9,44.

The Ferry Building of 1898 had handled about 50,000 people a weekday in the 1930s, before the bridges opened. Its renovation ended in 2003, and its long nave is now a public market hall 44. The F-line heritage streetcars were extended along the Embarcadero to Fisherman's Wharf in March 2000 45. Around the bay, the Bay Conservation and Development Commission, set up in 1965, controls public access within a 100-foot band of the shore 46.

Still in the way

The boulevard is still a wide multi-lane road between the city and the piers, and the century-old seawall under it is a seismic risk: voters approved $425 million in seawall bonds in 2018 47.

Getting there

Ferries use the terminal behind the Ferry Building; Golden Gate Ferry's Sausalito service began in 1970 48. Streetcars and Muni Metro run along the front 45.

Public access
Public promenade the length of the central front.
Continuity
Continuous along the Embarcadero, patchier beyond.
Transit
Ferries, streetcars and Metro meet at the Ferry Building.
Integration
The boulevard is still wide and busy.

Boston

United States

Boston Harbor (Harbour and bay)

1641first year of the colonial ordinances behind Massachusetts’ public-waterfront law14/20

Boston's harbour walk exists because a law with seventeenth-century roots makes developers build it.

Massachusetts' Chapter 91 waterways programme dates from 1866, but its roots are the Colonial Ordinances of 1641–47, which preserved public rights on the shore for fishing, fowling and navigation 15. New waterfront development has to be set back from the water's edge, and that setback becomes the Harborwalk 49. First conceived in a 1984 plan, the Harborwalk is nearly 40 miles of public walkway, of a planned total of nearly 50 49.

The elevated Central Artery opened in 1959, carrying about 75,000 vehicles a day between downtown and the waterfront. The Big Dig began in 1991, the elevated road was taken down in 2004 3, and the Rose Kennedy Greenway opened in its place in October 2008 50. The water itself was cleaned up under court order after a 1985 lawsuit; the last compliance order in that case was closed in 2016 11.

Still in the way

Gaps in the Harborwalk, and in the Seaport district signs and private-looking streets that put people off land they have a legal right to use 51.

Getting there

State-funded commuter ferries cross the harbour; the route network has changed several times in recent years 52.

Public access
Strong in law; some frontage feels private.
Continuity
About 40 of 50 planned miles built.
Transit
Commuter ferries, mostly peak-hour.
Integration
Greenway reconnected the North End and Waterfront.

Paris

France

Seine (River)

1923last year of legal swimming in the Seine before it reopened in 202515/20

Paris put a 13 km expressway on its right bank in 1967 and has been taking the river back from it ever since.

The Voie Georges-Pompidou, a 13 km expressway along the right bank, opened in 1967 5. Paris Plages, from July 2002, closed the riverside road each summer and laid 3.5 km of sand and deckchairs 53. A 2.3 km stretch of the left-bank lower quay became a promenade in 2013 54. In 2016 the city pedestrianised 3.3 km of the right-bank lower quay; a court annulled the order in 2018, and the appeal court confirmed the city's replacement order in 2019 55,56,57. The banks of the Seine in central Paris have been a UNESCO World Heritage site since 1991 58.

On 5 July 2025 the Seine reopened for public swimming for the first time since 1923, after the clean-up done for the 2024 Olympics, at three sites in the centre, east and west of the city 14,59. The city tests the water daily and posts notice when a site has to close 59.

Still in the way

Most of the old expressway still carries traffic; only 3.3 of its 13 km became park 5,55.

Getting there

The Metro and buses serve the quays. The Batobus river shuttle sits outside the Paris public transport network and is not covered by the Navigo pass 60.

Public access
Central banks open and free.
Continuity
Car-free stretches broken by the remaining expressway.
Transit
Good Metro; no river transit on the public network.
Integration
The river is the historic core, but the quays sit below the street.

Lyon

France

Rhône and Saône (River)

1,600parking spaces removed to make the Berges du Rhône17/20

Lyon's left bank of the Rhône was a car park for decades. Now it is a 5 km riverside park, and the Saône has followed.

The left bank of the Rhône had been an open-air car park for decades 61. About 1,600 spaces were removed 62 and replaced with a promenade of nearly 5 km and about 10 hectares, from the Parc de la Tête d'Or to Gerland 63,64, officially opened in May 2007 61.

The Saône followed. Its banks had been neglected, and pedestrian links were long blocked by traffic on the quai Romain Rolland 65. The first phase of the Rives de Saône project, delivered from 2013, created 15 km of riverside walk from Confluence to Île Barbe and into the Val de Saône, with 23 artworks along the way 65. In June 2025 Navigône, a battery-electric river shuttle on the Saône with four stops over 6.2 km, joined the city's TCL public transport network 66.

Still in the way

The former A6/A7 motorway along the Rhône at Perrache and Confluence, which the state approved downgrading to an urban road in 2016 67.

Getting there

Navigône river shuttle, integrated into the TCL network since 2025 66.

Public access
Both central banks are free public space.
Continuity
About 5 km on the Rhône, 15 km on the Saône.
Transit
A river shuttle in the network, still small.
Integration
The former motorway still runs past Confluence.

Seoul

South Korea

Han River (Hangang) (River)

41.5 kmlength of the Han inside Seoul, lined by eleven riverside parks13/20

Seoul lines almost its whole river with free parks, and puts expressways and flood embankments between them and the people who use them.

The Han runs for 41.5 km through Seoul, and its banks hold eleven Hangang parks 68. Their shape dates from the development programme of 1982–86, which built flood embankments and the riverside Olympic expressway at the same time as the parks 68. Because the parks sit in the floodplain inside the levees, they flood: in August 2020 access to all eleven was restricted 69.

In August 2024 the city said the Gangbyeon and Olympic expressways block access between the river and the city, and announced a review of putting sections underground or covering them 70. A related case is Cheonggyecheon, a downtown stream buried under concrete and an elevated highway. The highway was demolished from 2004 and about 5.8 km of stream opened in 2005 71.

Still in the way

The riverside expressways and the levees; the parks are reached through underpasses and bridges 70,68.

Getting there

The Hangang Bus river ferry launched in September 2025 and was suspended within days for safety checks; the full route resumed in March 2026 72,73. Subway lines cross on the bridges.

Public access
Huge free parks, closed in floods.
Continuity
Parks and cycle paths run most of the river.
Transit
Subway nearby; a new and unsteady ferry.
Integration
Expressways and levees separate the parks from neighbourhoods.

Portland

United States

Willamette River (River)

1974year Harbor Drive was removed for a riverside park13/20

Portland tore out a riverside highway half a century ago, and then built a new freeway barrier on the other bank.

Harbor Drive carried about 24,000 vehicles a day along the west bank in the early 1970s 74. After a task force set up in 1968 by Governor Tom McCall, it was removed in 1974 75; the Congress for the New Urbanism calls it the first major highway to be removed on purpose 74. The park was dedicated in 1978 and renamed after McCall in 1984; the city gives its area as 30.9 acres 75.

Interstate 5, built along the east bank in the 1960s, cut the Central Eastside off from the river 76. The Eastbank Esplanade, built in 1998–2001, runs 1.5 miles between the freeway and the water and includes a 1,200-foot floating walkway 77. The water got cleaner: the Big Pipe sewer project, finished in 2011, cut combined sewer overflows into the river by 94 per cent 78.

Still in the way

I-5 on the east bank, in places only a few metres from the path 76.

Getting there

No public river transit. A proposed passenger ferry was still struggling for funding and dock agreements in 2025 79.

Public access
West bank fully public.
Continuity
Park and esplanade form a loop via bridges.
Transit
No transit on the water.
Integration
Good on the west bank, poor on the east.

Bilbao

Spain

Nervión estuary (Ría de Bilbao) (River)

1997year the Guggenheim opened on former port land13/20

Bilbao's city-centre estuary was port and industrial land. The Guggenheim is the famous part of a larger clearance.

Abandoibarra, on the left bank in the city centre, held a container terminal, a shipyard and a railway line; from the 1990s the port moved to the outer harbour 7. The public company Bilbao Ría 2000 redeveloped it to a master plan by César Pelli, Diana Balmori and Eugenio Aguinaga; the riverside promenade and the Pedro Arrupe footbridge opened in 2003 and the scheme was finished in 2011 8. The Guggenheim Museum opened in 1997, and the case study for the Lee Kuan Yew World City Prize describes a three-kilometre pedestrian promenade 80.

The Euskotren tram, opened in 2002, runs beside the river through Abandoibarra 81. Downstream, Zorrotzaurre became an island when a canal was opened in 2018, partly to reduce flood risk 82.

Still in the way

Post-industrial land downstream, and water quality still too uncertain for routine swimming, which a 2026 city plan for river leisure aims to change 83.

Getting there

The Euskotren tram runs along the riverside 81.

Public access
Central promenades free and public.
Continuity
About 3 km in the centre; downstream unfinished.
Transit
Riverside tram; no public boats.
Integration
Strong in the centre, weak downstream.

Chicago

United States

Lake Michigan (Lake)

26 of 30miles of city shoreline that are public parkland (Friends of the Parks, c. 2008)15/20

Chicago's lakefront is public because of a note on an 1836 canal map and one merchant who kept suing.

In 1836 the canal commissioners' map marked the land east of Michigan Avenue as public ground, “Public Ground – A Common to remain forever Open, Clear & free of any buildings or other Obstructions Whatever”. The mail-order merchant Aaron Montgomery Ward took the city to court repeatedly to enforce it, in Illinois Supreme Court cases from 1897 to 1910 16. Daniel Burnham's 1909 Plan of Chicago pushed the same idea 84, with the line that “not a foot of its shores should be appropriated by individuals to the exclusion of the people” 85.

Much of the lakefront is made land, filled in places up to a mile from the original shore 86. Of 30 miles of city shoreline, 26 are public parkland 85. The Lakefront Trail was split in 2018 into separate trails for cycling (18 miles) and walking (18.5 miles) 87.

Still in the way

Lake Shore Drive, eight lanes wide, which the Congress for the New Urbanism calls a noisy, dangerous and polluting barrier 88.

Getting there

Buses run along the drive. There is no year-round public transport on the lake; the Chicago Water Taxi works the river 89.

Public access
26 of 30 miles public parkland.
Continuity
Separate walking and cycling trails since 2018.
Transit
No year-round transit on the lake.
Integration
An eight-lane drive between neighbourhoods and parks.

Toronto

Canada

Lake Ontario (Lake)

70%share of central waterfront land in public hands, according to the city’s 2001 plan10/20

Toronto owns most of its central waterfront and still has a motorway and a rail viaduct between it and downtown.

The city's 2001 central waterfront plan carried a photograph captioned “the Gardiner Expressway acts as a barrier between the city and the waterfront” and noted that about 70 per cent of the land was already public 90. Waterfront Toronto was created by the three levels of government in 2001 91. The elevated Gardiner, built between 1955 and 1966, lost its section east of the Don River in 2001 4, but the rest stands. The railway viaduct is a barrier too: the Gardiner East assessment notes that in its study area it limits access to four underpasses 92.

Queens Quay reopened in 2015 with a wide promenade and the missing downtown link of the Martin Goodman Trail 93; the Unwin Avenue connection in 2019 completed a waterfront trail of more than 20 km 94. In the Port Lands, a flood-protection project has given the Don River a new mouth and created a new island 95.

Still in the way

The Gardiner, Lake Shore Boulevard and the rail viaduct 92.

Getting there

The island ferries, publicly owned since the city bought the Toronto Ferry Company in 1926 96, and a streetcar along Queens Quay 93.

Public access
Mostly public land, access still being built.
Continuity
Continuous trail only since 2019.
Transit
Island ferries and a waterfront streetcar.
Integration
Motorway and rail wall off downtown.

Zurich

Switzerland

Lake Zurich and the Limmat (Lake)

1887year the public lakeside quays were inaugurated19/20

In Zurich the city end of the lake has been public since the 1880s. Further along the shore, voters have declined to open it.

Before the quay works, most of the city's lakeshore was privately owned and inaccessible. The engineer Arnold Bürkli's Quaianlagen, about 3 km of quays on reclaimed land, were built in the 1880s and inaugurated in 1887 97,98,99. Bathing is part of civic life: the present building of the women-only Frauenbad dates from 1888 100, and the Oberer Letten river bath on the Limmat, built in 1952, is a few minutes from the main station 101.

Beyond the city, the shore is a different story. A direct lakeside path covers just under 26 km, almost half the cantonal shore, and 12.6 km are gaps, mostly private houses on the water 18. On 3 March 2024 voters in the canton rejected an initiative for public lakeside paths by 64 per cent to 36 19.

Still in the way

Inside the city, little. Outside, private gardens on the water 18.

Getting there

The first lake steamer sailed in 1835 102,99; lake boats have been part of the regional ZVV fare network since 1990 102.

Public access
City quays public since 1887.
Continuity
Continuous in town; gaps along the wider lake.
Transit
Boats on the ordinary fare network.
Integration
The old town sits on the water.

Geneva

Switzerland

Lake Geneva (the Rade) (Lake)

72%of voters who refused to demolish the Bains des Pâquis in 198817/20

Geneva's harbour basin is ringed by public quays. Most of the rest of the canton's lakeshore is private.

The quays went up as the fortifications came down: the Quai du Mont-Blanc from 1853 and the Quai Gustave-Ador in stages from 1856. Together they give about 6 km of pedestrian quays around the Rade 103,104. The Bains des Pâquis began as wooden baths in 1872 and were rebuilt in concrete in 1931–32 105. When demolition was proposed, 72 per cent of voters rejected it on 25 September 1988 106. The Jet d'Eau has reached 140 metres since 1951 107,108.

A free public beach at Eaux-Vives, 350 metres long, opened in June 2019 109. Across the canton, though, a 2021 bill tabled by members of the cantonal parliament estimates that only about 40 per cent of the lakeshore is effectively public 20.

Still in the way

Busy roads behind the quays in town; private plots along most of the cantonal shore 103,20.

Getting there

The yellow Mouettes water-buses, from a company formed in 1897, cross the Rade on the regional fare network 110.

Public access
Rade public; most cantonal shore private.
Continuity
About 6 km of quays around the Rade.
Transit
Water-buses on the fare network.
Integration
Quays double as traffic roads.

Barcelona

Spain

Mediterranean (Open sea)

1992Olympics that moved the railway and gave the city its beaches18/20

Until the late 1980s Barcelona's seafront was railway sidings and industry. The Olympics were the reason to change it.

Poblenou grew beside the Barcelona–Mataró railway of 1848, the first on the Iberian peninsula; before the Olympics the seafront held a goods station, obsolete industry and waste ground, cut off from the district 6. For 1992 the railway tracks that had separated the city from the beach were moved underground, and 6 km of beachfront were regenerated 10. Five wooden footbridges cross the coastal ring road between the Vila Olímpica and the beaches 111. Before 1966 the Somorrostro shantytown on the beach had about 15,000 residents 112.

In the old port, the Moll de la Fusta of 1987 put a promenade on top of a partly sunken road 113. The city now has ten beaches along almost 5 km of coast 112, and in June 2026 the Spanish state agreed to transfer to it 5.2 km of shoreline from Barceloneta to the Fòrum 114.

Still in the way

The Ronda Litoral, crossed by bridges 111, and the working port to the south-west.

Getting there

Metro line 4 stops at Barceloneta and Ciutadella–Vila Olímpica, a few minutes from the sand 115.

Public access
Public beaches, mostly city-owned.
Continuity
Unbroken from Barceloneta to the Fòrum.
Transit
Metro a short walk away.
Integration
The ring road still has to be crossed.

Rio de Janeiro

Brazil

Atlantic and Guanabara Bay (Open sea)

4 kmof promenade along Copacabana, redesigned by Burle Marx in 197017/20

Rio's beaches are public and continuous. The six-lane avenue beside them is the price.

Avenida Atlântica was built along Copacabana in 1908 116. Its first pavements, with a black-and-white wave pattern borrowed from Lisbon, date from the avenue's widening in 1919 116. In the 1970s a landfill widened the beach and the promenade, and Roberto Burle Marx redesigned the four-kilometre promenade, keeping the waves on the beach side 117,118,116. The avenue is six lanes and 4 km long 119.

On Guanabara Bay, the Aterro do Flamengo was built on landfill under Governor Carlos Lacerda, from a proposal by Lota de Macedo Soares, with landscaping by Burle Marx. It opened in 1965 and covers about 1.2 million square metres 120.

Still in the way

Avenida Atlântica on weekdays, and the expressways that run through the Aterro. On Sundays and holidays one side of the avenue is closed to cars 119, and on Sundays the Aterro's roads close for leisure 121.

Getting there

Three metro stations serve Copacabana, a few blocks inland 119.

Public access
Public beaches, continuous promenade.
Continuity
The full 4 km, then on to the Aterro.
Transit
Metro a few blocks inland.
Integration
Six lanes of traffic on weekdays.

Tel Aviv

Israel

Mediterranean (Open sea)

14 kmof seafront promenade, by the municipality’s figure of 8.7 miles18/20

Tel Aviv's grid runs straight to the sea, and in 2018 it removed the wall that stood between the street and the sand.

The municipality counts 13 beaches along the coast and a promenade of 8.7 miles. One, the religious beach, has separate days for men and women 122. The promenade began in 1939 as a narrow raised walk and was rebuilt in the 1980s 123.

The old raised boardwalk, its designers wrote, acted as a border blocking the natural flow of people between city and beach 124. The central promenade was rebuilt between 2012 and 2018: 2.3 km, about 17 metres wide including steps 124, with a steep wall replaced by wooden steps down to the sand 125. At the north end, a promenade on the old port's sea wall opened in 2005 123.

Still in the way

A strip of hotels and the concrete Kikar Atarim complex 125, and future towers on the site of Sde Dov airport, closed in 2019 126.

Getting there

Buses and walking; no rail line runs along the seafront.

Public access
Thirteen municipal beaches.
Continuity
Promenade from Jaffa to the north.
Transit
Buses only to the sand.
Integration
The wall is gone and streets run to the sea.

Havana

Cuba

Straits of Florida (Open sea)

1901year construction of the Malecón began14/20

Havana's Malecón is a sea wall with no beach that the city treats as its living room.

Work began in 1901 under the US military government of Leonard Wood, starting at the Castillo de la Punta, and the wall was pushed west in stages for decades 127. Sources disagree about when it was finished, with dates in the late 1940s and 1950s 128,127, and about its length: Frommer's says about 7 km, Urbipedia about 8 129,128. Behind it runs a broad six-lane avenue 128.

There is no sand, and people swim and fish off the rocks 129. The sea comes over: during Hurricane Irma in September 2017 flooding reached about 2.25 metres at one hospital near the shore 130. An Italian–Cuban study calls the area one of the most vulnerable in the country 131.

Still in the way

The six-lane avenue, the wall itself, and the waves that come over it.

Getting there

Buses; Havana has no metro, only a limited suburban rail network 132.

Public access
Open and free along its length.
Continuity
Unbroken for 7–8 km.
Transit
Buses; no metro.
Integration
Six lanes and storm flooding limit use of the water.

Nice

France

Baie des Anges (Mediterranean) (Open sea)

7 kmof the Promenade des Anglais, whose full-length renovation began in 201517/20

Nice's promenade started as a work-relief scheme paid for by British winter residents and is now part of a World Heritage site.

In 1822 the Anglican minister Lewis Way raised money among British residents to pay unemployed local people to build a path; it was finished in 1824 as a way 2 metres wide and named the Promenade des Anglais in 1844, according to the city 133. Widening works were inaugurated in 1931 133. Today it runs for about 7 km 133,134, and since 2021 it has been part of the UNESCO World Heritage site “Nice, Winter Resort Town of the Riviera” 135.

The beaches are pebbles, and the city tops them up every year to make up for erosion; in spring 2026 it added about 6,000 tonnes 136. The city's beach listing in 2026 included 14 sub-let private beaches among the public ones 137.

Still in the way

The road itself, which the city says has been recalibrated to three traffic lanes, alongside a two-way cycle track 133. Private beach concessions take some of the frontage 137.

Getting there

Tram line 2 runs parallel to the coast between the port and the airport, underground through the centre; its last section opened in December 2019 138,139.

Public access
Mostly public; some private concessions.
Continuity
7 km unbroken.
Transit
Tram parallel to the coast.
Integration
A busy road between town and beach.
