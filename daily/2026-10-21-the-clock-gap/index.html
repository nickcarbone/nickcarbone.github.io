The ClockGap

Twenty places where a government set the clocks on purpose and wrote down why, from Franco's Spain to Pyongyang's half-hour, with a live board showing how far each one runs from the sun.

By Nick Carbone · researched and designed with Claude

On Sunday 25 October, at one in the morning Greenwich time, the European Union's clocks go back an hour. The United States waits until Sunday 1 November.1,2 For that one week, New York and London are four hours apart instead of five, and every call or market open pegged to the usual five-hour gap shifts by an hour. In March it happens again for two or three weeks, because America springs forward first.

Neither date is set by nature. Europe's is fixed by a 2001 directive; America's by the Energy Policy Act of 2005, which pushed the end of summer time into November from 2007.1,2 The European Parliament voted in March 2019, by 410 to 192, to end seasonal clock changes altogether. The plan has stalled among member governments, which can't agree whether to keep summer or winter time, and Europe's clocks are changing again this weekend.3,4

Strictly, the earth turns 15 degrees an hour, and a country could set its clock by where it sits. Very few do. Every legal time is a choice, and this guide collects twenty places where a government (or, in one case, an occupying army) made that choice and the reason is on the record: a decree, a speech, a vote. Each entry gives the decision, the stated reason, what happened next, and the gap between the clock and the sun in the main city, in minutes.

How to read the gap

Each entry ends with a small ruler. The yellow disc is local mean solar noon, worked out from the city's longitude (four minutes per degree). The red wedge shows where the clock puts noon. A long wedge to the right means the clock runs ahead of the sun, so mornings are dark and evenings long. Where a place uses summer time, the pale extension shows the extra hour.

These are mean-time figures. The true sun drifts by up to about a quarter of an hour either way through the year, so treat them as close, not exact.

Europe's borrowed hours

Seven European decisions, most of them about which neighbour to keep time with.

01UTC+1 · summer UTC+2

Spain

Decided
Ordered 7 March 1940, in force 16 March · Presidencia del Gobierno under Franco
Reason on the record
“La conveniencia de que el horario nacional marche de acuerdo con los de otros países europeos” (the advantage of keeping national time in step with other European countries).

The order ran in the official gazette on 8 March 1940 and set the change for 11pm on Saturday 16 March: Spanish clocks went forward 60 minutes, from Greenwich time to an hour ahead of it, matching Germany's standard time.5 The order itself names no country. That it was meant to line Spain up with Nazi Germany is the reading historians and journalists have given it since, and the hour was never handed back.6,7 The Canary Islands, far to the south-west, stayed an hour behind the mainland.7

Madrid sits west of Greenwich, so in winter the sun is highest at around a quarter past one, and NPR and others have linked the country's late lunches and dinners to the clock. A Congress of Deputies subcommittee on working hours recommended a return to GMT in 2013, and in 2018 Pedro Sánchez's government set up an expert commission.6,8 Its report, delivered in March 2019, recommended keeping the current zone and advised against any hasty change without consensus. None has come.72

02UTC+1 · summer UTC+2

The Netherlands

Decided
16 May 1940 · ordered by the German occupation authorities
Reason on the record
None of its own: German time was imposed after the Dutch capitulation. No documented reason has been found for keeping it after 1945.

Until 1940 the Netherlands kept its own time, based on the mean solar time at the Westertoren church in Amsterdam: GMT plus 19 minutes and 32 seconds, made the legal time for the whole country in 1909 and rounded to a tidier plus 20 minutes in 1937.9 Just after the Dutch capitulation, the German high command announced that German time would apply. Germany was already on its summer hour, so Dutch clocks went forward one hour and 40 minutes in a single night.10,11

Central European Time stayed after the liberation. Nobody seems to have written down why; the record here is a gap, and this entry is the exception to our rule, included because the imposed hour is so plainly documented.9

03UTC+0 · summer UTC+1 (Azores an hour behind)

Portugal

Decided
1992 to Central European Time, Cavaco Silva government · 1996 back to Western European Time, Guterres government
Reason on the record
1992: to follow, in working hours, “the countries with which it maintains more frequent contacts”, and to save energy. 1996: an observatory report found Portugal's geography called for Greenwich time.

Portugal is the one country in this guide that adopted its neighbours' hour to match them, then gave it back. Decree-law 124/92 moved the mainland onto Central European Time, with summer time at two hours ahead of Greenwich, to match the European partners it traded with.12,14 Lisbon sits nine degrees west of Greenwich, so in summer twilight lasted past 10pm, while in winter children went to school in the dark.12,13,14

Soon after taking office in late 1995, António Guterres's government asked the Lisbon Astronomical Observatory to review the question. Its report pointed back to Greenwich time, and in March 1996 Portugal skipped the spring change, which returned it to Western European Time.12,13

04UTC+0 all year

Iceland

Decided
7 April 1968 · Althing, Act no. 6/1968 on the reckoning of time
Reason on the record
Proposed by University of Iceland astronomers: twice-yearly changes confused international flight schedules, meant resetting every clock and disturbed sleep, and Icelanders liked their light summer evenings.

Before 1968 Iceland's legal standard time was an hour behind Greenwich, and summer time brought it level. The 1968 act simply kept the summer hour all year, which leaves Reykjavík nearly an hour and a half ahead of its own sun.15,16

The decision has been challenged repeatedly. Bills to bring back clock changes have failed since 1994, and in 2018 a health ministry working group recommended moving to an hour behind Greenwich, citing “social jet lag”. In 2020 the government decided to leave the clocks alone, noting that the change would cost residents about 13 per cent of their waking daylight hours.15,16

05UTC+3 all year (Moscow); 11 zones in all

Russia

Decided
27 March 2011, permanent summer time under Medvedev · 26 October 2014, reversed by Federal Law 248-FZ
Reason on the record
2011: Medvedev said the clock changes “really disturb the human biorhythm”. 2014: the reversal followed widespread complaints about pitch-dark winter mornings.

Dmitry Medvedev announced in February 2011 that Russia would stop going back to winter time. Clocks went forward on 27 March and stayed there, and for three years Russia stayed on summer time all year.17,18,19

The cost showed up in the mornings. Many Russians disliked getting up on pitch-dark winter mornings, and a sleep specialist told TASS that dark mornings were worse for health than dark evenings.73 The Duma passed a reversal in July 2014, the Federation Council approved it, and at 2am on 26 October 2014 clocks went back an hour for good, returning Moscow to three hours ahead of Greenwich with no seasonal change.19,20

06UTC+3 all year

Turkey

Decided
8 September 2016 · Council of Ministers decision in the Resmî Gazete · made permanent by presidential decision, October 2018
Reason on the record
“In order to benefit more from daylight”, in the government's words, repeated in the 2018 decision.

Turkey spent decades on Eastern European Time, two hours ahead of Greenwich in winter and three in summer. In September 2016 the government decided to stay on summer time, and the fall-back due at 4am on 30 October 2016 simply did not happen.21,22

Further decisions kept the summer hour in 2017, and a presidential decision published in October 2018 made three hours ahead of Greenwich apply “all year round every year”. Turkey now keeps Moscow's time all year, and in winter runs an hour ahead of Athens.23

07UTC+0 (since 20 September 2026)

Morocco

Decided
Adopted 26 October 2018, in force the next day: permanent GMT+1 by Decree 2.18.855 · 20 September 2026, back to GMT by Decree 2.26.530
Reason on the record
2018: a government study found switching confused citizens; an extra “hour of natural light” and lower peak electricity use. 2026: a response to citizens' expectations.

Morocco's 2018 switch arrived with almost no notice. The Government Council approved the decree on Friday 26 October, less than two days before clocks were due to go back, and the country stayed an hour ahead of Greenwich instead. The minister responsible, Mohamed Benabdelkader, said the new hour had “plus d'avantages que d'inconvénients”, more advantages than disadvantages.24,25 The decree allowed a temporary return to GMT, which Morocco used during Ramadan.24

Eight years later the government undid it. Decree 2.26.530, adopted in June 2026, repealed the 2018 decree, and at 2am on Sunday 20 September 2026 clocks went back an hour. Aziz Akhannouch's government presented it as an answer to what citizens had asked for, and its spokesman, Mustapha Baitas, called the decision “définitive”.26,27,28,29 Phones and computers rely on the global time zone database for this; devices that haven't updated since mid-2026 may still show the old hour.29

One country, one clock

Four governments that decided a single time mattered more than the sun across their territory.

08UTC+8 across the whole country

China

Decided
From 1949 · adopted through broadcasting as the People's Republic took power, rather than by a single decree
Reason on the record
The official National Time Service Center describes “Beijing Time” replacing the old coastal standard's name, which no longer suited the new state.

Republican China had drawn up five time zones, from five and a half to eight and a half hours ahead of Greenwich. The country now uses one, roughly 60 degrees of longitude wide. The Chinese Academy of Sciences' time service tells it as a gradual adoption: Beijing's Xinhua radio first broadcast “Beijing Time” on 28 September 1949, and by early 1950 everywhere except Xinjiang and Tibet used it. Many secondary accounts credit Mao with ordering it in 1949; the official history doesn't.30,31

In the far west the gap is extreme: in Kashgar, solar noon can come as late as about 3.10pm.31 Many people in Xinjiang, especially Uyghurs, use an unofficial local time two hours behind Beijing, while government offices, railways and airlines keep to Beijing time.31,32

09UTC+5:30 across the whole country

India

Decided
Indian Standard Time introduced 1906 · sole national time after independence; Calcutta time ended in 1948, Bombay time in 1955
Reason on the record
A single time for the whole country. Central governments have since said they have no plans for a second zone.

IST is set on the 82.5°E meridian, near Mirzapur in Uttar Pradesh,71 which puts it five and a half hours ahead of Greenwich. Calcutta and Bombay kept their own local times into independent India, Calcutta until 1948 and Bombay until 1955.34 Across the country's width, the National Physical Laboratory found that sunrise and sunset in Dong, in Arunachal Pradesh, and Ghuar Mota, in Gujarat, are nearly two hours apart.33

The north-east pays for the single zone: dawn there comes around 4am in June, and in winter it gets dark early in the afternoon. Tea estates in Assam have long kept their own unofficial “bagaan time”, an hour ahead of IST, and in 2014 Assam's chief minister, Tarun Gogoi, proposed adopting it for the state.34,35 In 2018 the National Physical Laboratory, India's timekeeper, proposed a second zone of six and a half hours ahead of Greenwich for the north-east. The central government has said it has no plans to change; one minister cited a study estimating power savings of only 1.2 to 1.5 per cent.33,36

10UTC+5 across the whole country

Kazakhstan

Decided
1 March 2024 · Government Decree No. 20 of 19 January 2024
Reason on the record
The trade ministry's announcement cited experts saying a single zone would “positively affect biological rhythms and human health” and “eliminate temporary barriers” between regions.

Until 2024 most of Kazakhstan, including Astana and Almaty, was six hours ahead of Greenwich, and the west an hour behind that. At midnight on 1 March 2024 everywhere but the west put its clocks back an hour, and the whole country now keeps one time across about 40 degrees of longitude.37,38

It was unpopular. A petition to “return the time” drew more than 50,000 signatures, and a December 2024 Demoscope poll found about 53 per cent against and 22 per cent in favour.40 A planned three-year health study was dropped in 2025, and President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev told the National Kurultai that a large country with a relatively small population should have a single time zone and that the discussion “should be stopped”.39,40

11UTC+8

Malaysia and Singapore

Decided
1 January 1982 · motion moved by Prime Minister Mahathir Mohamad; Singapore followed on the same date
Reason on the record
Mahathir said the half-hour gap between the peninsula and Sabah and Sarawak had led to disputes over working hours. Singapore cited its close ties, trade and finance with Malaysia.

Before 1982 Peninsular Malaysia was seven and a half hours ahead of Greenwich and the Borneo states eight. Parliament passed the change in December 1981, and at 11.30pm on New Year's Eve peninsular clocks jumped to midnight.41 Singapore's government announced on 20 December 1981 that it would move too, citing its many close ties with Malaysia; business leaders warned that otherwise there would be “half an hour of reduced contacts”.42

The result puts Kuala Lumpur and Singapore, both west of Beijing, on the same clock as Beijing, Perth and Manila, and about an hour ahead of the sun.41,42

Half-hours and quarter-hours

Four offsets that break the whole-hour grid, each for a reason someone stated.

12UTC+5:45

Nepal

Decided
1 January 1986 · clocks moved from India's +5:30 to +5:45
Reason on the record
The standard meridian was set to run near Gauri Shankar, a peak east of Kathmandu, nearer the country's middle than the capital.

Nepal is one of very few places a quarter-hour off the grid. At midnight on 1 January 1986 its clocks went from India's time to 15 minutes ahead of it.43,45 The meridian chosen runs near Gauri Shankar, about 100km east of Kathmandu, and works out at 86.25°E, or exactly five hours and 45 minutes ahead of Greenwich.43,44

The dates are disputed: some accounts, including the Nepali Times, put Nepal's adoption of a time of its own in 1956, while the time zone database records the move to +5:45 in 1986.44,45

13UTC+9 (UTC+8:30 from 2015 to 2018)

North Korea

Decided
15 August 2015, “Pyongyang Time” by decree of the Supreme People's Assembly Presidium · 5 May 2018, reversed
Reason on the record
2015: KCNA said Japan had committed “unpardonable crimes”, including “depriving Korea of even its standard time”. 2018: a gesture of reconciliation after the inter-Korean summit.

Korea had been nine hours ahead of Greenwich since 1912, under Japanese rule, a time based on the 135°E meridian that runs through Japan. On the 70th anniversary of liberation, 15 August 2015, North Korea put its clocks back half an hour to a standard based on 127.5°E, which runs through the peninsula.46,47 Seoul warned of practical problems, including at the joint industrial complex in Kaesong.46

It lasted less than three years. After meeting Moon Jae-in at Panmunjom on 27 April 2018, Kim Jong Un said he had been sad to see two clocks on the wall of the Peace House, one for each Korea, and that “since it is us who changed the time standard, we will return to the original one.” Clocks went forward on 5 May 2018.48,49

14UTC−4 (UTC−4:30 from 2007 to 2016)

Venezuela

Decided
9 December 2007, half an hour back under Hugo Chávez · 1 May 2016, restored under Nicolás Maduro
Reason on the record
2007: Chávez said children were arriving at school “dead tired” after rising before dawn. 2016: energy saving during a drought-driven electricity crisis.

Chávez's half-hour was a return: Venezuela had used four and a half hours behind Greenwich from 1912 to 1964.50 Announcing the change, he said children “have to get up at five in the morning” and “arrive at school dead tired”, and added that he didn't care if people called him crazy.51

Maduro reversed it in 2016 for a different reason. A drought had drained the Guri reservoir, which supplies about two-thirds of the country's electricity, and the government wanted more evening daylight to cut demand. “This extra half hour of sunlight will allow a better electricity saving,” said Jorge Arreaza, then science minister. The same package rationed power to shopping malls.52,53

15UTC−3:30 · summer UTC−2:30

Newfoundland

Decided
Standard Time Act 1935, Commission of Government · 1963 attempt to join Atlantic Time withdrawn
Reason on the record
1935: one time for the dominion, close to St John's solar time. 1963: the province backed down in the face of public opposition.

In a 1910 debate, members of Newfoundland's Legislative Council balked at calling it “colonial time”. One proposed a time of the island's own: “Let us have a time of our own - a good time.”55 The 1935 act set the whole dominion three and a half hours behind Greenwich, close to the solar time of St John's at its far eastern edge.54,56

The half-hour has survived every attempt to round it off. Joey Smallwood's government tried to move the province to Atlantic Time in 1963 and withdrew after stiff public opposition; a similar idea had failed in 1951.54,56 In 1988 the province tried “double daylight time”, putting clocks forward two hours from April to October. It was not repeated; children ended up going to school in the dark in October.56,57

The date line and the sun

Five places that moved the calendar, or refused the clock change, for trade, unity, heat or health.

16UTC+13 (UTC−11 until 2011)

Samoa

Decided
Thursday 29 December 2011 was followed by Saturday 31 December · Tuilaepa Sailele Malielegaoi's government
Reason on the record
“In doing business with New Zealand and Australia, we're losing out on two working days a week,” Tuilaepa said.

Samoa skipped Friday 30 December 2011 and jumped from the American side of the International Date Line to the side shared by its main trading partners. Tuilaepa noted that while Samoans were in church on Sunday, “they're already conducting business in Sydney and Brisbane.” By government decree, workers were paid for the Friday that never happened.58,59 Tokelau, a New Zealand territory to the north, made the same jump.60

The move reversed one made in 1892, when, according to the Associated Press, American traders persuaded Samoa onto the US side.58 Look at the ruler below: in standard time, Samoa's clock sits exactly as far from the sun as it did before 2011. The hour stayed where it was; only the date changed.

17UTC+14 in the Line Islands; +12 in Tarawa

Kiribati

Decided
1 January 1995 · President Teburoro Tito's government moved the eastern islands across the date line
Reason on the record
The old line split the country, with the west a day ahead of the east, so government offices could do business with each other only four days a week.

Kiribati's islands straddle the 180° meridian, and until 1995 the date line ran through the middle of the country. Tito had promised to fix it; from 1 January 1995 the Phoenix and Line Islands moved to the western date, and the Line Islands became the first inhabited place on Earth to start each new day, 14 hours ahead of Greenwich.61,62

Kiribati made the most of it later. In 1999 it renamed Caroline Island “Millennium Island” and held a celebration there for the year 2000; Tonga and New Zealand's Chatham Islands disputed the claim to be first.62 According to van Gent, the millennium angle was noticed only afterwards; the reason the government gave was unity.61,62

18UTC−7 all year (Navajo Nation observes summer time)

Arizona

Decided
21 March 1968 · state law opting out of daylight saving under the federal Uniform Time Act of 1966
Reason on the record
The heat. An extra hour of evening sun in an Arizona summer meant longer, hotter evenings and more air-conditioning, not savings.

Arizona tried daylight saving time in 1967 and dropped it the next year, using the exemption the federal law allowed states.63,64 The historian Calvin Schermerhorn has written that in 1967 “energy consumption soared”, because in most of the state “air conditioners had to run longer”.74 Newspaper editorials kept up the argument; ABC15 has collected the Arizona Republic's.65

The Navajo Nation, which spans Arizona, New Mexico and Utah, observes summer time so its clocks match across state lines. The Hopi Reservation, surrounded by Navajo land, does not. In summer, driving east on State Route 264 from Tuba City, the clock changes several times in under 100 miles.63,64

19UTC−6 all year in central Mexico

Mexico

Decided
30 October 2022 · Ley de los Husos Horarios, passed by the Senate 26 October 2022
Reason on the record
Public health, plus minimal energy and economic savings, according to Andrés Manuel López Obrador's government.

Mexico had observed summer time nationally since 1996, to save energy. López Obrador's government argued the savings had become negligible and the health costs real. The Senate approved the new law by 59 votes to 25, with 12 abstentions, it was published in the official gazette on 28 October 2022, and the clocks went back for the last time two days later.66,67,68

Municipalities along the US border kept US-style summer time, so that border cities stay in step with their neighbours across the line.67,68

20UTC−3 all year

Paraguay

Decided
Law passed by Congress 24 September 2024, promulgated by President Santiago Peña 11 October 2024
Reason on the record
To “improve the quality of life of Paraguayans” and make better use of daylight, said Raúl Latorre, president of the Chamber of Deputies.

Paraguay put its clocks forward for summer on 6 October 2024 and has not put them back. The law sets official time for the whole country at three hours behind Greenwich, its old summer hour, ending the seasonal switch; it had failed twice in the previous legislature.69,70

The result is later sunsets all year. Some legislators asked for the effect on schoolchildren in winter mornings to be studied, especially in the interior.70

Checking a time zone story

Time zone changes attract good stories, and some of them are better than the record. A short method for the next one you meet:

  1. Find the instrument. A decree, a law or a gazette notice has a number and a date. The Spanish order of 1940 says nothing about Germany; the Nazi alignment is historians' reading of it, which may be right but is a different kind of fact.
  2. Separate the stated reason from the suspected one. Kiribati said unity and later sold the millennium. Both can be true; only one was the reason given.
  3. Check the arithmetic. Longitude divided by 15 gives the sun's offset. If a country is said to have “moved closer to the sun”, the numbers will show it.
  4. Check the database. The volunteer-run tz database, which phones and computers use, records every change with dates and often the gazette text; its mailing list is where many of this guide's details were confirmed.
